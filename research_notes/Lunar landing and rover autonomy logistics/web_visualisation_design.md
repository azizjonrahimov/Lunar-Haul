# Web visualisation design for a research-grade lunar logistics simulator site

Research notes compiled 2026-10-15. Scope: how credible scientific/aerospace organisations present interactive 3D simulations and research results on the web, and what design language and technical stack a professor/engineer-facing lunar logistics simulator should adopt. Every factual claim carries an inline source; where primary sources could not be reached (rate limits, 403s, JS-only pages) this is stated in the Gaps.

Headline conclusions for the report writer:
- The credible references share a restrained visual grammar: near-black-on-white or dark-neutral text at ~0.8 alpha, one sans-serif (often an OFL face), hairline rules at ~10% alpha, generous line-height (~1.7), numbers presented with units and provenance, and 3D views that are *instruments* (camera presets, time scrubber, trails, labels) rather than cinematics.
- For lunar terrain plus vehicles plus time scrubbing, the two realistic stacks are (a) CesiumJS + Cesium Moon 3D Tiles (asset 2684829, up to 5 m terrain/1 m imagery at the poles, free Community tier with revenue/funding caveats) or (b) Three.js + NASA-AMMOS `3d-tiles-renderer` (Apache-2.0, JPL-authored, used on Mars rover missions) loading the same 3D Tiles or a self-hosted LOLA-derived mesh. Both are permissively licensed. Babylon.js is a full game engine (1.4 MB gz vs 168 kB for Three.js) and is the weaker fit for a data-visualisation site.
- Chart conventions for research audiences are explicit in Vega-Lite's `errorband` (extent `ci` = 95% bootstrapped CI; default `stderr`), Observable Plot's `linearRegressionY` (`ci` default 0.95, `fillOpacity` 0.1), Tufte (data-ink ratio, small multiples) and the Okabe-Ito / Paul Tol / Crameri colour work.
- Site structure: the Nerfies/Academic-project-page lineage (title, authors, links row, teaser, abstract, BibTeX) for paper pages; Observable Framework-style static generation with build-time data loaders; Keep a Changelog for the changelog; viewing without sign-in (as Google Earth Engine Apps do) with sign-in only for saving/submitting.

## Key Question 1: Which specific design decisions (fonts, grid, palette, chart style, 3D camera/lighting) recur across the credible sites?

### Takeaway
The credible exemplars converge on quiet typography (one sans, body ~16–17 px, line-height ~1.7, text at ~80% black), hairline dividers at ~10% alpha, direct labelling rather than legends, colour-blind-safe qualitative palettes of at most 7–9 hues, and 3D scenes that are physically grounded (sun as a single directional light, LRO-derived terrain) with explicit time controls (play/pause/reverse, speed ring, scrubber) rather than decorative motion.

### Cited Findings

**Typography and text colour (Distill, Tufte, OWID, project pages)**
- Distill's article stylesheet sets `d-article { font-size: 16px }` at ≥768 px, rising to `font-size: 1.06rem; line-height: 1.7em` at ≥1024 px; body text colour is `rgba(0,0,0,0.8)`; h2 is 24 px → 36 px at ≥1024 px with `line-height: 1.25em`; h3 18 → 20 px weight 700; h4 is 14 px, weight 600, uppercase; blockquotes are `rgba(0,0,0,0.6)`; hairlines and `hr` are `rgba(0,0,0,0.1)`; link underlines are `rgba(0,0,0,0.4)` darkening to 0.8 on hover; citations use `#668`; code blocks are 14 px; content is placed on a named CSS grid (`grid-column: text`, `kicker`, `screen`) — [Distill template d-article.css](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/distillpub/template/master/src/styles/d-article.css)
- Distill required articles to be written in HTML on its own infrastructure, which "provides default styling and standard academic features while keeping the flexibility of the web"; review criteria were "advancing dialogue", "outstanding communication" (interactive media common but not required) and "scientific integrity" (weaknesses must be disclosed); writing to that standard "often takes 100+ hours"; the journal has been on indefinite hiatus since 2 July 2021 — [Distill journal page](https://distill.pub/journal/)
- Tufte CSS reproduces Edward Tufte's handout style on the web (sidenotes with automatic numbering, the ET Book font), is MIT-licensed, and traces its lineage to Tufte-LaTeX and the R Markdown Tufte handout — [edwardtufte/tufte-css](https://github.com/edwardtufte/tufte-css); MIT licence and ~6,000 stars per [ecosyste.ms listing](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fedwardtufte%2Ftufte-css)
- Tufte coined "chartjunk" for useless, non-informative or information-obscuring elements; the data-ink ratio argues against decoration and against distorting data to fit a decorative scheme; "small multiples" split a crowded multi-series chart into adjacent panels sharing an x-range — [Wikipedia: Edward Tufte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Tufte); the source book is *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information* (1983; 2nd ed. 2001, Graphics Press), whose stated topics include data-ink ratio, small multiples and "design variation vs. data variation" — [edwardtufte.com](https://www.edwardtufte.com/book/the-visual-display-of-quantitative-information/)
- Our World in Data's chart fonts in a 2019 survey were Playfair Display (titles) and Lato (body); the survey notes "Display" fonts are suited only to large sizes — [Datawrapper blog, Fonts for data visualization](https://datawrapper.de/blog/fonts-for-data-visualization). Lato is SIL OFL 1.1, designed by Łukasz Dziedzic (2010) — [Lato OFL file](https://git.stg.centos.org/source-git/lato-fonts/blob/105dc92cb2f92e2ec441b9a2b343234a20bed074/f/Lato2OFL)
- OWID's Grapher codebase is TypeScript with React and MobX; the repository states "It is no longer available under the MIT licence" — you may view and study but not copy, modify or distribute without written permission — [owid/owid-grapher](https://github.com/owid/owid-grapher). This conflicts with OWID's FAQ, which still says Grapher is open source under MIT — [OWID FAQ](https://ourworldindata.org/faq). Treat the repository statement as authoritative and do not copy Grapher code.
- The Academic Project Page Template (derived from the Nerfies page) is a static HTML/CSS page with: paper title, authors, institution, conference; links (arXiv, GitHub, etc.); abstract; teaser video; image carousel; YouTube embed; video carousel; PDF poster viewer; BibTeX block with copy button; "More Works" dropdown; licensed CC BY-SA 4.0 — [eliahuhorwitz/Academic-project-page-template](https://github.com/eliahuhorwitz/Academic-project-page-template). The Nerfies site itself is CC BY-SA 4.0 and asks for the paper (Park et al., ICCV 2021) to be cited — [nerfies/nerfies.github.io](https://github.com/nerfies/nerfies.github.io)

**Palette conventions**
- Okabe & Ito's colour-universal-design palette (first published 20 Nov 2002, revised 2008) is built around: avoid red–green combinations; "Show difference BOTH in color and shape"; label elements directly rather than via a separate key; alternate warm and cool hues and keep brightness/saturation clearly different when two warm or two cool colours sit together — [Okabe & Ito, Color Universal Design](https://jfly.uni-koeln.de/color/)
- Paul Tol's qualitative schemes (page revised 5 Sept 2026): **Bright** (default, 7): `#4477AA #EE6677 #228833 #CCBB44 #66CCEE #AA3377 #BBBBBB`; **Vibrant** (7): `#EE7733 #0077BB #33BBEE #EE3377 #CC3311 #009988 #BBBBBB`; **Muted** (9): `#CC6677 #332288 #DDCC77 #117733 #88CCEE #882255 #44AA99 #999933 #AA4499` (bad data `#DDDDDD`); **Light** (9, for table fills, not lines). Sequential: YlOrBr (9), Iridescent (23, linearly varying luminance so it survives greyscale), discrete rainbow (do not interpolate), smooth rainbow (interpolate). Diverging: Sunset, Nightfall, BuRd, PRGn. Bright/Vibrant/Muted are colour-blind safe; yellow/green, blue/violet and cyan/pink/grey pairs are the most problematic; ~6% of men are green-blind and ~2.5% red-blind — [Paul Tol's Notes](https://sronpersonalpages.nl/~pault/)
- Crameri's Scientific colour maps (e.g., batlow) are perceptually uniform and ordered, readable with colour-vision deficiency and in black-and-white print, and versioned on Zenodo; citation: Crameri, Shephard & Heron (2020), "The misuse of colour in science communication", *Nature Communications* 11:5444, doi:10.1038/s41467-020-19160-7 — [fabiocrameri.ch/colourmaps](https://www.fabiocrameri.ch/colourmaps/)
- The R package khroma implements Okabe-Ito (2008), Tol (2021) and Crameri (2018) schemes together — [khroma on libraries.io](https://libraries.io/cran/khroma)
- World Bank's data-visualisation style guide chooses text colours "to achieve sufficient background contrast for light and dark text as well as for text in category colors" and specifies font sizes and number formatting — [World Bank Data Visualization Style Guide](https://worldbank.github.io/data-visualization-style-guide/)

**3D conventions at NASA/ESA/Cesium**
- NASA's Eyes on the Solar System (JPL release 2022-130, 2 Sept 2022) runs in a web browser with no download, covers "no fewer than 126 space missions", lets users follow paths "as far back as 1949 and as far into the future as 2049", rotate objects, compare side by side, "modulate perspective and lighting", and scroll through guided "interactive journeys" such as Voyager's Grand Tour; the rebuild took more than two years; producer Jason Craig — [JPL news release](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/explore-the-solar-system-with-nasas-new-and-improved-3d-eyes/)
- Earlier Eyes versions were built in Unity3D and required the Unity Web Player plug-in — [Elumenati product page](https://www.elumenati.com/product/eyes-on-the-solar-system/); [Universe Today review](https://universetoday.com/77366/eyes-on-the-solar-system)
- JPL Robotics' browser-based visualisation task (building on "Eyes on Exoplanets") is "developed in C++, JavaScript and WebGL", targets smartphones through engineering workstations, and is intended for both public outreach and flight-operations support — [JPL Robotics research task](https://robotics.jpl.nasa.gov/what-we-do/research-tasks/browser-based-exoplanet-visualization-for-education-public-outreach-and-engineering)
- NASA SVS lunar-south-pole visualisations (Ernie Wright, USRA; Noah Petro, GSFC) are built from LRO laser-altimeter terrain; at the poles the Sun "is never more than 1.5° above or below the horizon" and terrain shadows "reach as far as mountains 120 kilometers away"; the series simulate lighting at two-hour intervals for 2023–2030 (within 2° of the pole) and 2025–2028 (to 80°S) — [SVS 4893 Shadows near the Moon's South Pole](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4893); [SVS 4930](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4930/); [SVS 5027](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5027); [SVS 5228](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5228)
- ESA's "Where is Rosetta?" (2014) shows the spacecraft's position for any chosen date from 2004 launch to 2015, reports distance to Sun, Earth and comet 67P at 12:00 UT, and supports pan/zoom/rotate by mouse or keyboard — [Science Office project page](https://www.scienceoffice.org/projects/where-is-rosetta/). ESA's "View Rosetta's comet" (v1, 13 Aug 2015) is a WebGL shape-model viewer with toggleable trajectory markers; sci.esa.int now notes the site is "under review and not being updated" — [ESA Science](https://sci.esa.int/web/rosetta/-/56100-view-rosettas-comet)
- ESA Hera's 3D GIS is built on PRo3D (an interactive planetary 3D viewer) extended to import custom SPICE kernels for celestial-body and probe trajectories, aimed at scientists planning observations — [IAC-24 abstract](https://iafastro.directory/iac/archive/browse/IAC-24/A3/4B/86032/); [VRVis publication](https://www.vrvis.at/publications/PB-VRVis-2024-024). ESA Juice's tools page lists the SSC 4D Orbit Viewer and a Cosmographia plugin for manoeuvre/pointing visualisation — [ESA Juice tools](https://cosmos.esa.int/web/juice/tools)
- Cesium Stories ("PowerPoint for geospatial") stores one camera view per slide (saved only on "Capture view"), supports rich text/images, measurement tools, metadata styling and time-dynamic data, and shared stories adapt to mobile, desktop and projection — [Cesium Stories](https://cesium.com/cesium-stories); [Cesium blog 2020](https://cesium.com/blog/2020/01/28/cesium-stories/); Cesium staff said there is "no easy path" to embedding Stories in a custom app — [Cesium community thread](https://community.cesium.com/t/cesium-story-like-features-in-our-cesium-app/9553)
- Blacknight Space's Event Horizon (Cesium blog, 22 Sept 2026) is a browser-based 3D mission-visualisation platform for operators, engineers and technical marketers; it streams Cesium World Terrain, imagery and Cesium Moon via Cesium ion, renders spacecraft as glTF/.glb, supports bring-your-own trajectory, attitude and error ellipsoids, moves "from Earth-centric to Moon-centric" views, and lets planners save views and notes at milestones (stage separation, deployment, rendezvous); mobile performance is "on the roadmap"; no benchmarks given — [Cesium blog: Blacknight Space Event Horizon](https://cesium.com/blog/2026/09/22/blacknight-space-event-horizon-cesiumjs/)
- Google Earth Engine Apps: `ui.Panel` is "an upper-level UI container in which to arrange widgets"; layouts are flow (horizontal/vertical) or absolute (`'top-left'`, `'bottom-right'`); `ui.root` is a fixed root panel defaulting to horizontal flow; the documented example adds a 400 px-wide vertical panel beside the map (the sidebar-plus-map pattern) and overlays a panel at `bottom-right` — [Earth Engine UI panels](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/guides/ui_panels). Public apps are "viewable by anyone, without needing to sign in"; the guide advises simple UI elements for non-experts, caching aggregate statistics as table assets, and assuming client code is visible — [Earth Engine Apps guide](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/guides/apps)

**Telemetry overlays (SpaceX)**
- No official SpaceX design document was found. The open-source SpaceXtract tool extracts telemetry from SpaceX and RocketLab webcasts by OCR (OpenCV template matching per screen region), MIT-licensed — [shahar603/SpaceXtract](https://github.com/shahar603/SpaceXtract); a derived API describes the overlay as showing time, velocity and altitude at 30 FPS with derived acceleration and downrange — [SpaceXtract summary](https://gittrend.io/repo/shahar603/SpaceXtract). A fan-made "SpaceX-style" overlay for OBS shows altitude, velocity, downrange distance, mission time and stage info on a transparent background — [Saturn Aerospace Telemetry Overlay](https://spacedock.info/mod/4448/Saturn%20Aerospace%20Telemetry%20Overlay)

### Inferences
- A defensible design system for the lunar site: one OFL sans (Source Sans 3, Inter or IBM Plex Sans) at 16–17 px body with 1.6–1.7 line-height, text at ~80% of foreground, hairlines at ~10% alpha (directly borrowing Distill's measured values), a tabular-figure monospace for telemetry, and a 7-hue Tol Bright or Okabe-Ito qualitative set with Crameri *batlow* (or a greyscale-safe sequential) for elevation/slope maps.
- Credible 3D space visualisations show *provenance* in-frame (data source, epoch, frame) and treat the Sun as a single directional light whose elevation is physically derived; at the lunar pole that means a near-horizontal light (≤1.5°) with very long shadows, so the lighting itself is a scientific statement, not a mood.
- Telemetry overlays that read as professional are flat: mission elapsed time, a handful of state variables with units, no gradients or glow. The SpaceX overlay's canonical trio (T+, velocity, altitude) is a reasonable minimum for a lander; for a rover, add slope, battery/energy, and distance to goal.
- The recurrence of "camera view per slide" (Cesium Stories), "saved views and notes at milestones" (Event Horizon) and "guided interactive journeys" (NASA Eyes) indicates that camera presets plus a narrative step list is the standard way to mix storytelling and free exploration.

### Gaps
- The rendering engine behind the 2022 browser version of NASA Eyes is not documented in any source found (searches for Three.js/WebGL/"Pioneer" returned nothing authoritative); only the earlier Unity version and JPL Robotics' C++/JavaScript/WebGL exoplanet viewer are documented.
- Distill's `font-family` stack and grid-column widths live in other CSS files that were not fetched; only d-article.css values are reported.
- OWID's *current* fonts could not be confirmed (2019 snapshot only; Grapher repo README does not mention fonts).
- Doumont's three laws: the first ("adapt to your audience") is confirmed by the TUGboat review listing; the GeekDad and TUG pages returned 403 so the exact wording of laws two and three (commonly given as "maximise the signal-to-noise ratio" and "use effective redundancy") could not be verified — [TUGboat review listing](https://www.tug.org/books/reviews/tb103reviews-doumont.html); [GeekDad review](https://geekdad.com/2013/04/trees-maps-and-theorems/)
- No official description of SpaceX's overlay typography/colour exists; the fields list comes from third-party extraction tools.

## Key Question 2: Which stack (Three.js + what loader/terrain approach, or CesiumJS/Babylon) do comparable projects use, and what performance did they report?

### Takeaway
Comparable aerospace projects use either CesiumJS with the Cesium Moon 3D Tiles (JPL/NASA-linked, Blacknight, Ansys) or Three.js with NASA-AMMOS's `3d-tiles-renderer` (JPL, used on Perseverance/Curiosity); Babylon.js is the heavier general game engine. Published performance *numbers* are scarce; the best concrete guidance is an absolute pixel budget (~5.2 MP) with DPR derived from it, device tiering with `detect-gpu`, avoiding shader recompiles, and watching the tile LRU cache.

### Cited Findings

**Lunar terrain data and CesiumJS**
- Cesium Moon (Cesium ion asset **2684829**; renamed from "Cesium Moon Terrain" in Sept 2025) resolution: mid-latitudes 60N–60S imagery 100 m (LROC WAC Global Morphology Mosaic) and terrain 59 m (LOLA–SELENE Kaguya TC merge); poles 60–90° imagery 100 m, 85.5–90° imagery 1 m (LROC NAC); polar terrain 60 m (60–75°), 30 m (75–80°), 20 m (80–85°), 10 m (85–87.5°), 5 m (87.5–90°) from LOLA RDR gridded data — [Cesium Moon asset page](https://cesium.com/platform/cesium-ion/content/cesium-moon/)
- Loading code from the same page: `Cesium.Ellipsoid.default = Cesium.Ellipsoid.MOON; const viewer = new Cesium.Viewer("cesiumContainer", { globe: false, sceneModePicker: false, baseLayerPicker: false, geocoder: false }); const tileset = await Cesium.Cesium3DTileset.fromIonAssetId(2684829); viewer.scene.primitives.add(tileset);` — limitations: globe disabled "to prevent it from interfering with the data"; "2D and Columbus View are not currently supported"; "Don't use Earth imagery layers for other celestial bodies"; "Geocoder is not currently supported for lunar locations" — [Cesium Moon asset page](https://cesium.com/platform/cesium-ion/content/cesium-moon/)
- The dataset was built from LRO data, demonstrated at a Digital Twin for Lunar Development Workshop at JPL, is used by Ansys (STK, RF Channel Modeler for lunar polar comms) and Buendea (XOSS with NASA's Advanced Operations Concept Lab), and the engines were updated for "non-WGS84 based ellipsoids" — [Cesium blog, Introducing Cesium Moon Terrain](https://cesium.com/blog/2024/08/06/introducing-cesium-moon-terrain/)
- A Cesium community answer states CesiumJS "only supports one central body", so Earth–Moon scenes are not straightforward; lunar terrain can be swapped in with the Moon ellipsoid — [Cesium community: Fly me to the Moon](https://community.cesium.com/t/fly-me-to-the-moon/9008)
- Cesium ion Community (free) tier: for non-commercial personal projects, exploratory commercial/government development and *unfunded* educational activities; limits 10 GB storage, 15 GB/month streaming, 1,000 Global Imagery sessions; a paid account is required when annual gross revenue or fundraising exceeds $50K, for government project work, for *funded educational research*, or when limits are exceeded; Commercial is $149/month individual ($524 team, 100 GB storage, 150 GB/month streaming), Premium $499/$874 — [Cesium ion pricing](https://cesium.com/platform/cesium-ion/pricing/)
- CesiumJS core is Apache License 2.0, "Copyright 2011-2026 CesiumJS Contributors"; bundled third-party components (Draco, basis_universal, LERC under Apache 2.0; many MIT/BSD libraries; Source Sans Pro under OFL 1.1; some sample assets under CC BY) each carry their own notices — [CesiumJS LICENSE.md](https://github.com/CesiumGS/cesium/blob/main/LICENSE.md)
- CesiumJS `SunLight` is a directional light originating from the Sun with `color` (default WHITE) and `intensity` (default 2.0) — [Cesium SunLight docs](https://cesium.com/learn/cesiumjs/ref-doc/SunLight.html)
- The 2017 Moon Trek CesiumJS demo is described as "a lunar research web portal" incorporating observations from past and current lunar missions (post dated 1 Nov 2017) — [Cesium community: Moon Trek and MAVEN demos](https://community.cesium.com/t/new-cesium-demos-moon-trek-and-maven/6206)

**Three.js route**
- NASA-AMMOS `3DTilesRendererJS` (`npm install 3d-tiles-renderer`) is Apache-2.0 with a Caltech/JPL copyright, has a framework-agnostic core and integrations for Three.js, Babylon.js and React Three Fiber (`3d-tiles-renderer/three`, `/babylonjs`, `/r3f`, `/core`), plugin packages, demos including Dingo Gap Mars, Cesium ion Mars and a lunar demo; gotcha: "No new tiles load once the LRUCache reaches maxSize or maxBytesSize" — monitor `lruCache.cachedBytes`/`isFull()` and raise `maxBytesSize` or `errorTarget` — [NASA-AMMOS/3DTilesRendererJS](https://github.com/NASA-AMMOS/3DTilesRendererJS)
- The maintainer states it "has specifically been used on the Perseverance and Curiosity rover missions"; v0.3.36 added a plugin architecture and orthographic `GlobeControls`; a Cesium Ecosystem Grant funded quantized-mesh support; it supports Cesium ion and Google Photorealistic Tiles providers — [Cesium community announcement](https://community.cesium.com/t/3d-tiles-renderer-a-3d-tiles-implementation-for-three-js-from-nasa-jpl/35187); [three.js forum thread](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/3d-tiles-renderer-a-3d-tiles-implementation-for-three-js-from-nasa-jpl/44136/9.md)
- Three.js is MIT, "Copyright © 2010-2026 three.js authors" — [three.js LICENSE](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/LICENSE)
- Three.js official examples relevant to a lunar scene exist for cascaded shadow maps (`webgl_shadowmap_csm`, `webgpu_shadowmap_csm`), PCSS/VSM soft shadows, `webgl_lod`, instancing (`webgl_instancing_performance`, `webgl_buffergeometry_instancing_billboards`), fat lines (`webgl_lines_fat`), logarithmic depth buffer (`webgl_camera_logarithmicdepthbuffer`) and 230 `webgpu_*` examples — [three.js examples/files.json](https://threejs.org/examples/files.json)

**Three.js vs Babylon.js**
- Bundle size: Three.js v0.175.0 ≈ 168.4 kB min+gz vs Babylon.js v8.1.1 ≈ 1.4 MB (modular imports can shrink it); Three.js >35k GitHub stars and >1.8M weekly npm downloads vs Babylon ≈3.5k stars/≈11k downloads; Babylon bundles physics (Cannon/Ammo/Oimo), a 2D GUI, an inspector and material editor; the author recommends Three.js for "data visualizations, interactive models, or tools that don't need full game-like features" and Babylon for game-like/VR/AR apps (article dated 24 Apr 2025) — [LogRocket: Three.js vs Babylon.js](https://blog.logrocket.com/three-js-vs-babylon-js/)
- Licensing: Three.js MIT, Babylon.js Apache 2.0; Babylon 8.0 ships shaders in GLSL and WGSL; Three.js offers a `WebGPURenderer` — [Cinevva guide](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/threejs-vs-babylonjs)
- Cesium "is primarily focused on geospatial applications, with built-in support for terrain rendering, map projections" whereas Three.js is general-purpose and needs manual work for geospatial data — [StackShare Cesium vs Three.js](https://stackshare.io/stackups/cesium-vs-three-js)

**Performance evidence**
- The Codrops case study (Filip Zrnzevic, "Sixty Frames for the Record") capped the drawing buffer at an absolute pixel budget of 5.2 MP (with LOW/HIGH tiers) and derived DPR from it, because the same DPR gives very different pixel counts on a laptop vs a 5K display; Three.js bakes the light count into each lit material's shader as a compile-time constant, so toggling light *visibility* caused recompile hitches (they toggled intensity instead); a full-screen CSS blend overlay over the WebGL canvas cost 30–45 ms/frame in the browser's GPU process, invisible to in-page profilers — [Codrops article](https://tympanus.net/codrops/?p=119712) (full text returned 403 on fetch; figures are from the search excerpt of the same article)
- three.js forum (Oct 2024): practitioners "always aim for 60 fps", stress steady frame pacing over average rate (a capped 30 fps can be fine; many systems fire rAF at 120–144 Hz), target "shity laptops with integrated GPUs" through Apple M-series, recommend `detect-gpu` to adapt quality and "NEVER max out the client resources"; no draw-call/triangle budgets were given — [three.js forum: minimum performance](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/what-minimum-performance-for-a-standard-website-what-is-the-average-gpu-nowadays/72396)
- `@pmndrs/detect-gpu` (MIT, ESM-only) classifies GPUs from benchmark fps normalised by resolution: Tier 0 = no WebGL/blocklisted/<15 fps (provide non-WebGL fallback), Tier 1 ≥15 fps, Tier 2 ≥30 fps, Tier 3 ≥60 fps; example output `{ tier: 1, fps: 21, gpu: "intel iris graphics 6100" }`; benchmark data can be self-hosted; the upstream benchmark source stopped updating in Dec 2025 — [pmndrs/detect-gpu](https://github.com/pmndrs/detect-gpu)
- A forum report of very low FPS on an Intel UHD 620 laptop with a moderate glTF scene, and a thread advising to check that hybrid-graphics laptops are not routing the browser to the integrated GPU — [three.js forum: UHD 620](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/three-js-performance-on-intel-uhd-620-graphics/24676); [three.js forum: high CPU/GPU usage](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/incredible-high-cpu-gpu-usage-on-some-devices/19312)
- iOS limits canvas size to 4,096 × 4,096 px while most desktop browsers exceed 10,000 × 10,000; exceeding the limit renders the canvas unusable — [MDN canvas element](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Elements/canvas)

**Time-dynamic animation primitives**
- Cesium `PathGraphics` provides `leadTime` (seconds of path shown ahead of the object), `trailTime` (seconds behind), `resolution` (max sampling step, default 60 s), `width` (default 1 px), `material` (default white), `show`, `distanceDisplayCondition` — the standard way to draw trails and ghost/predicted trajectories — [Cesium PathGraphics docs](https://cesium.com/learn/cesiumjs/ref-doc/PathGraphics.html)
- Cesium `Animation` widget: play, pause and reverse buttons with current date/time, surrounded by a "shuttle ring" for speed; no jog wheel "because Cesium treats time as continuous rather than as discrete frames"; dragging the ring or clicking jumps to preset speeds; a realtime button syncs to the system clock (unavailable in `ClockRange.CLAMPED`/`LOOP_STOP` when outside bounds); built as Animation → AnimationViewModel → ClockViewModel → Clock with `clock.tick()` in a `requestAnimationFrame` loop — [Cesium Animation docs](https://cesium.com/learn/cesiumjs/ref-doc/Animation.html)

### Inferences
- For a research simulator whose core is replaying *simulated* trajectories on real terrain, the Three.js + `3d-tiles-renderer` route gives full control of lighting, materials, custom overlays (slope, energy, uncertainty) and bundle size, while still being able to stream Cesium Moon via the Cesium ion plugin or a self-hosted LOLA tileset; CesiumJS is the faster path to a working time-dynamic viewer (Clock, Timeline, PathGraphics, SunLight) at the cost of a single-body model and a heavier, Earth-oriented widget set. Either choice is permissively licensed and used by NASA-adjacent teams.
- Because Cesium ion's free tier excludes *funded* educational research and government project work, a university lab with grant funding should budget for the Commercial tier or self-host LOLA/NAC-derived tiles (3D Tiles is an open OGC community standard, so a self-hosted pipeline is viable); the asset's Terms of Use must be read before redistribution.
- A realistic performance budget for integrated laptop GPUs, assembled from the sources: cap drawing buffer around 2–5 MP (not DPR), target a steady 30 fps floor with 60 fps on Tier 2–3 devices, tier quality via `detect-gpu`, keep the lit-material light count constant, avoid CSS blend/filters over the canvas, and bound the tile cache so far-field tiles stay coarse.
- Polar-sun lighting is best implemented as one directional light with a physically computed elevation (≤1.5° at the pole) plus cascaded shadow maps (Three.js CSM example) or Cesium's SunLight with shadows; because shadows span tens of kilometres, shadow-map cascades or a precomputed illumination texture (as NASA SVS does offline) are needed to avoid aliasing.

### Gaps
- No comparable project published frame-rate or draw-call benchmarks for lunar/space scenes; the Blacknight post explicitly gives no numbers, and the Cesium Moon pages have no performance notes.
- The Codrops article's full text was unreachable (403); only the excerpted figures are cited.
- Cesium Moon's licence type is not stated on its page (it points to ion Terms of Service and the Content Usage and Attribution Guide, with an apparent copy-paste reference to "Cesium Mars").
- No source was found quantifying the share of a research audience on integrated GPUs; the forum consensus is qualitative.
- Babylon.js's official documentation on terrain/time animation was not reviewed; comparison relies on two secondary articles.

## Key Question 3: How do the good sites present a "simulation you can run yourself" with controls and live numbers without clutter?

### Takeaway
The good sites separate a dominant visual canvas from a narrow control/telemetry rail, expose time as a first-class control (play/pause/reverse, speed ring, scrubber), publish only a few labelled numbers with units at a time, and gate motion behind user intent and `prefers-reduced-motion`; when WebGL is unavailable or the GPU is Tier 0 they fall back to static 2D figures or tables.

### Cited Findings
- Earth Engine's documented pattern is a 400 px vertical control panel beside the map, with optional small overlays anchored `bottom-right`; the guide urges "simple UI elements" so non-experts can use expert analyses and recommends caching aggregate statistics rather than computing on the fly — [Earth Engine UI panels](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/guides/ui_panels); [Earth Engine Apps](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/guides/apps)
- Cesium's time UI is a compact cluster: play/pause/reverse + date/time + shuttle ring, with a separate Timeline; time is continuous, not frame-indexed — [Cesium Animation docs](https://cesium.com/learn/cesiumjs/ref-doc/Animation.html)
- Cesium Stories decouples exploration from authored views (views save only on "Capture view") and shares by link/embed — [Cesium Stories](https://cesium.com/cesium-stories)
- Event Horizon lets planners annotate milestones with saved views and notes, and shows trajectories, attitude and error ellipsoids as data layers — [Cesium blog: Event Horizon](https://cesium.com/blog/2026/09/22/blacknight-space-event-horizon-cesiumjs/)
- "Where is Rosetta?" reduces the live numbers to three distances (Sun, Earth, comet) for one chosen date, and supports keyboard as well as mouse navigation — [Science Office](https://www.scienceoffice.org/projects/where-is-rosetta/)
- Distill's reviewers judge "outstanding communication" and warn that "rambling is an easy failure mode"; interactivity is a means, not a requirement — [Distill journal](https://distill.pub/journal/)
- WCAG 2.2 SC 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions (Level AAA): "Motion animation triggered by interaction can be disabled, unless the animation is essential"; parallax scrolling is cited as commonly non-essential; sufficient techniques C39 (CSS `prefers-reduced-motion`), SCR40 (the same query in JavaScript) and a user preference setting — [W3C Understanding SC 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)
- WCAG 2.2 SC 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide (Level A): moving/blinking/scrolling content that starts automatically, lasts more than five seconds and is presented in parallel with other content needs a pause/stop/hide mechanism unless essential; auto-updating content has no five-second exception — [W3C Understanding SC 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)
- CSS cannot gate JS-driven animation (canvas/WebGL, GSAP, etc.); read the preference with `window.matchMedia('(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)')` and listen for `change`; treat it as tiering (keep fades, remove displacement), not a kill switch — [Verdigris Reduced Motion Guide](https://design.verdigris.co/categories/animation/reduced-motion); "reduced motion doesn't mean no motion" — [BOIA on prefers-reduced-motion](https://boia.org/blog/what-to-know-about-the-css-prefers-reduced-motion-feature)
- MDN: "Canvas content is not exposed to accessibility tools as semantic HTML is. In general, you should avoid using canvas in an accessible website or app"; provide alternative content inside `<canvas>` (rendered when canvas or JavaScript is unavailable); the element has no implicit ARIA role and accepts any role — [MDN canvas element](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Elements/canvas)
- Historic W3C work on canvas hit regions (`addHitRegion`, `drawFocusIfNeeded`) tried to link fallback DOM to drawn regions for focus/screen readers — [Creative Bloq on canvas accessibility](https://creativebloq.com/web-design/canvas-element-accessibility-41514740); 2009–2010 W3C threads argued nested fallback content is the accessible alternative and that authors can offer to show the fallback when direct accessibility is impractical — [W3C WAI thread](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/w3c-wai-ua/2009JulSep/0057.html); [W3C canvas-api thread](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-canvas-api/2010JanMar/0220.html)
- `detect-gpu` Tier 0 is defined as the case where you should "provide a non-WebGL fallback" — [pmndrs/detect-gpu](https://github.com/pmndrs/detect-gpu)
- Three.js forum practitioners prefer a steady capped frame rate over a fluctuating higher one — [three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/what-minimum-performance-for-a-standard-website-what-is-the-average-gpu-nowadays/72396)

### Inferences
- Recommended layout: 3D canvas occupying ~65–75% width; a right-hand rail of ~360–420 px (Earth Engine's 400 px precedent) holding scenario selectors, a time cluster (play/pause/reverse, speed, scrubber with event ticks), and 4–6 labelled telemetry readouts with units and a sparkline each; a thin top bar with mission-elapsed time and data provenance; camera presets (overview, chase, polar sun, landing site) as a segmented control. Narrative steps (Cesium Stories style) can be a collapsible left drawer.
- "Live numbers without clutter" follows Tufte/Doumont: show few numbers, label directly, keep decimals consistent, and move everything else to hover tooltips or a secondary table view; Rosetta's three-number readout is the minimal template.
- Keyboard parity is achievable without hit regions: bind arrow keys/WASD to camera, space to play/pause, [ ] to speed, and expose the same state in an always-present DOM telemetry table (which also serves as the screen-reader alternative and the `prefers-reduced-motion`/Tier-0 fallback).
- Fall back to 2D when: `detect-gpu` reports Tier 0 or WebGL is unavailable; the viewport is below ~700 px wide; or the user prefers reduced motion and the scene would autoplay. The 2D fallback should be the same data drawn as a plan-view map plus the time-series panels, not a static screenshot.

### Gaps
- No WCAG or WAI document specific to WebGL/3D content was found; guidance has to be composed from SC 2.2.2, SC 2.3.3 and canvas fallback practice.
- No study was found measuring how researchers/engineers (as opposed to the general public) use time controls in web viewers.
- Observable Plot's and Observable Framework's default dashboard/card styling pages were rate-limited (HTTP 429) and could not be quoted.

## Key Question 4: Which charting conventions, libraries, uncertainty displays, palettes and export methods suit a research audience?

### Takeaway
Research-grade charting on the web means an explicit uncertainty encoding (`errorband` extent `ci`/`stderr`/`stdev`/`iqr`; regression bands with stated CI and low fill opacity), small multiples on shared scales, colour-blind-safe palettes (Okabe-Ito, Tol Bright/Muted, Crameri sequential), direct labels, and one-click SVG/PNG/JSON export; D3 (ISC), Observable Plot (ISC), Vega-Lite (BSD-3) and plotly.js (MIT) are all compatible with a public site.

### Cited Findings
- Vega-Lite `errorband` is "a composite mark that expands into a layered plot"; `extent` options: `"ci"` = "the 95% bootstrapped confidence interval of the mean", `"stderr"` (default), `"stdev"`, `"iqr"` (q1–q3); default colour `#4682b4`; interpolation options include `linear`, `step`, `basis`, `monotone` — [Vega-Lite errorband docs](https://vega.github.io/vega-lite/docs/errorband.html)
- Observable Plot `linearRegressionY`: `ci` is "the confidence interval in [0, 1), or 0 to hide bands; defaults to 0.95"; `precision` is "the distance (in pixels) between samples of the confidence band; defaults to 4"; `stroke` styles the line, `fill` the band (defaults to the stroke) with `fillOpacity` 0.1; the docs warn linear regression is "regularly criticized for pushing people to the wrong conclusions" and illustrate Simpson's paradox with penguin culmen data — [Observable Plot linear-regression.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/observablehq/plot/main/docs/marks/linear-regression.md)
- Observable Plot facets: `fx`/`fy` channels create small multiples on shared band scales; faceting needs ordinal/categorical data (bin continuous variables or use `interval`) — [Observable Plot facets](https://observablehq.com/plot/features/facets); per-facet independent y-scales are not supported ("faceting is meant to plot over shared scales"), the workaround is to normalise — [Observable forum](https://talk.observablehq.com/t/plot-facets-with-varying-scales/5557)
- Overlapping confidence bands are hard to read; split them into separate facets where possible — [DataCamp, Showing uncertainty](https://campus.datacamp.com/courses/improving-your-data-visualizations-in-python/showing-uncertainty?ex=4)
- Vega-Embed actions menu: "Export as PNG/SVG" (default on), "View Source", "View Vega" (off), "Open in Vega Editor"; `scaleFactor` (number or `{svg, png}`) multiplies export dimensions; BSD-3-Clause — [vega/vega-embed](https://github.com/vega/vega-embed)
- plotly.js export: `Plotly.toImage` (data URL) and `Plotly.downloadImage`; formats `png` (default), `jpeg`, `webp`, `svg`, `full-json` (data, layout, frames, config, plotly.js version); `width`/`height` in pixels — [Plotly static image export](https://plotly.com/javascript/static-image-export/)
- Licences: D3 ISC — [compliance listing](https://docs.raynet.de/raynet-one/cloud/2026.2/operations-supplement/en/d3.htm); plotly.js MIT — [licenses.dev](https://licenses.dev/npm/plotly.js/1.58.5) and Plotly's 2016 open-sourcing announcement — [Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1634121/plotly-to-open-source-its-dataviz-code.html); Vega-Lite BSD-3-Clause — [libraries.io](https://libraries.io/npm/vega-lite); Observable Plot ISC ("functionally equivalent to the BSD 2-Clause and MIT licenses") — [Simon Willison](https://www.simonwillison.net/2021/May/4/observable-plot/)
- Observable Framework is "a static site generator for data apps, dashboards, reports, and more", ISC-licensed, with data loaders that "precompute data snapshots at build time, so dashboards load quickly", front-end JavaScript plus any back-end language — [observablehq/framework](https://github.com/observablehq/framework)
- Palettes: Okabe-Ito principles (shape + colour, direct labels, no red–green) — [Okabe & Ito](https://jfly.uni-koeln.de/color/); Tol Bright/Vibrant/Muted hex sets, Iridescent for greyscale-safe sequential, "don't reverse the direction in one scheme when using several" — [Paul Tol](https://sronpersonalpages.nl/~pault/); Crameri maps perceptually uniform, CVD-friendly, B&W-readable, Zenodo-versioned — [fabiocrameri.ch](https://www.fabiocrameri.ch/colourmaps/); publisher-oriented palette recommendations (Okabe-Ito for Nature/PLOS/Cell; Tol Bright for ACS/Elsevier/Springer; Tol Muted for PRL/Wiley) are a secondary heuristic — [ConceptViz blog](https://conceptviz.app/blog/scientific-color-palette-for-research-papers-and-posters)
- Tufte: data-ink ratio, chartjunk, small multiples, sparklines ("a kind of 'word' that conveys rich information") — [Wikipedia: Edward Tufte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Tufte); book details — [edwardtufte.com](https://www.edwardtufte.com/book/the-visual-display-of-quantitative-information/)
- World Bank guide specifies contrast-checked text colours, font sizes and number formatting — [World Bank style guide](https://worldbank.github.io/data-visualization-style-guide/); ONS maintains a public chart style guide — [ONS chart style guide](https://ons-design.notion.site/ONS-chart-style-guide-abc7605a97624dc2bc7f2a3e16379d82)

### Inferences
- Default chart spec for the site: Observable Plot (or Vega-Lite where a declarative JSON spec is useful for reproducibility) with: titled axes including units; `errorband`/area y1–y2 bands at ~0.1–0.2 opacity under a mean line; the CI level stated in the caption ("shaded: 95% bootstrap CI, n = …"); small multiples via `fx`/`fy` on shared scales; Tol Bright or Okabe-Ito for ≤7 categorical series; Crameri *batlow* or Tol Iridescent for slope/elevation; direct end-of-line labels; a visible "Download SVG / PNG / CSV" action on every figure (mirroring Vega-Embed's menu) so figures can go into papers.
- Observable Plot's regression-caveat text is a model for how to caption model-fitted curves on a research site: state the model, state the CI, and point out where the model can mislead.
- Serving the chart *data* alongside the figure (CSV/JSON, as plotly's `full-json` does) is what distinguishes a research artefact from a marketing graphic.

### Gaps
- Observable Plot's default typography/axis styling page could not be fetched (HTTP 429); only the regression and facets docs are cited.
- Observable Plot's exact syntax for CI bands inside facets was not verified from docs.
- Crameri colour maps' licence was not stated on the fetched page (the maps are usually described as MIT; verify on Zenodo before bundling).
- The ConceptViz publisher-palette mapping is a blog heuristic, not publisher policy.

## Key Question 5: How should a research product's site be structured (landing, method/about, team, paper, data, API docs, changelog), how should sign-in be handled, and what stack is typical?

### Takeaway
Research sites that read as credible follow the paper-page template (title, authors with affiliations, link row, teaser, abstract, figures, BibTeX), publish data and code with explicit licences, keep a human-readable changelog, and keep the viewer fully usable without an account; sign-in appears only where persistence or submission is needed, as in Earth Engine Apps (view anonymously, publish with an account) and Cesium ion (account for data tokens).

### Cited Findings
- Paper/project page sections (title, authors, institution, conference; arXiv/GitHub links; abstract; teaser video; carousels; poster PDF; BibTeX with copy button; related works) — [Academic Project Page Template](https://github.com/eliahuhorwitz/Academic-project-page-template); CC BY-SA 4.0 lineage from Nerfies — [nerfies.github.io](https://github.com/nerfies/nerfies.github.io); Quarto and Astro variants render the same page from Markdown — [Quarto demo](https://berg009.codeberg.page/Quarto_Demo/); [Astro academic-project theme](https://a457ba07.previews.astro.build/themes/details/academic-project/)
- Distill's workflow: article in a GitHub repo (private during review), review and editing via GitHub issues, repo made public and transferred to @distillpub on publication; CC BY required; no word limits — [Distill journal](https://distill.pub/journal/)
- Keep a Changelog: "Changelogs are for humans, not machines"; every version has an entry; group by type (Added, Changed, Deprecated, Removed, Fixed, Security); latest first; display release dates in ISO 8601 `YYYY-MM-DD`; keep an `Unreleased` section; state whether Semantic Versioning is followed — [keepachangelog.com 1.1.0](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/)
- Observable Framework: static site output with build-time data loaders, ISC — [observablehq/framework](https://github.com/observablehq/framework)
- Earth Engine Apps: "No Earth Engine account is required to view or interact with a published App"; publishing requires the Apps Publisher role; restricted apps cannot be featured; assume client code is visible — [Earth Engine Apps](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/guides/apps)
- Cesium ion requires an account (free Community tier) to obtain the Moon tileset token; the free tier excludes funded educational research and government projects — [Cesium ion pricing](https://cesium.com/platform/cesium-ion/pricing/); [Cesium Moon](https://cesium.com/platform/cesium-ion/content/cesium-moon/)
- Cesium Stories share by link or embed and adapt to mobile/desktop/projection — [Cesium Stories](https://cesium.com/cesium-stories)
- Hugging Face's Open LLM Leaderboard publishes results, per-example details and the request queue as public datasets; a mirrored changelog entry reads "removed need for tokens in the leaderboard", indicating the view no longer needs authentication; submissions request automated evaluation of Hub-hosted models and a later commit "add a submission limit checker" rate-limits them — [Open LLM Leaderboard text content](https://huggingface.co/spaces/open-llm-leaderboard/open_llm_leaderboard/blob/d16cee27d4c72a4bd357bf8a56df6e1932bfd559/src/assets/text_content.py); [leaderboard mirror commit](https://huggingface.co/spaces/rntc/leaderboard-test/commit/3aa78c24ec0f20dece3e6f6d5e1465e149f0b105); [backend endpoints](https://huggingface.co/spaces/open-llm-leaderboard/open_llm_leaderboard/tree/main/backend/app/api/endpoints)
- OWID's FAQ says Grapher is MIT; the repository says it no longer is — a reminder to state licences unambiguously on the data/code page — [OWID FAQ](https://ourworldindata.org/faq); [owid-grapher](https://github.com/owid/owid-grapher)

### Inferences
- Site map: `/` landing with the live viewer embedded (or a 10-second looping *data* teaser, not stock art) and three numbers that matter; `/method` (model, assumptions, validation, limitations — Distill's "weaknesses must be disclosed"); `/paper` (Nerfies-style block with BibTeX); `/data` (datasets with versions, licences, checksums, CSV/JSON/Parquet downloads); `/api` (OpenAPI-generated reference); `/team`; `/changelog` (Keep a Changelog format, SemVer); `/replay/<id>` (shareable permalink for any run, state encoded in URL).
- Sign-in pattern that does not harm openness: anonymous users can run, scrub, compare and export; URL-encoded state gives shareable links with no account; an optional sign-in (GitHub/ORCID/email magic link) unlocks saving named runs, submitting to a leaderboard and API keys; the leaderboard is publicly readable and submissions are rate-limited (the Open LLM Leaderboard precedent). Never gate the viewer, the paper or the data behind login.
- Typical stack consistent with the exemplars: static site (Observable Framework, Astro or similar) with build-time data loaders for all published figures, a small API (for run submission, saved runs, leaderboard) behind a separate origin, and the simulator engine either compiled to WebAssembly for in-browser replay or served as pre-computed trace files; all chart data published alongside figures.

### Gaps
- No single authoritative source describes "optional sign-in" patterns for research tools; the pattern above is assembled from Earth Engine, Cesium and leaderboard precedents.
- The current Open LLM Leaderboard login requirements could not be confirmed from a live page; evidence is from mirrored commits and older text.
- No source was found quantifying how MIT/Harvard/Stanford lab project pages are structured beyond the Nerfies-lineage templates (which originate at Google/UW), so "lab page conventions" rest on that template's ubiquity (many forks listed in search) rather than a survey.

## Key Question 6: What licensing notes (fonts, icons, 3D libraries, data, imagery) must a public research site respect?

### Takeaway
Everything needed is available under permissive terms — OFL fonts (self-hosted via @font-face, renamed if modified/subsetted with Reserved Font Names), ISC/MIT icons with notices retained, MIT/Apache-2.0/BSD-3/ISC libraries with licence files shipped — but two traps exist: Cesium ion's free tier excludes funded research, and NASA imagery is free while NASA insignia are not.

### Cited Findings
**Fonts**
- OFL FAQ: webpages may use OFL fonts via CSS `@font-face` ("a much better method" than asking visitors to install); WOFF/WOFF2 conversion and subsetting count as modification and therefore generally forbid use of Reserved Font Names unless the font data is unchanged apart from compression; OFL fonts can be bundled with and sold inside software (not sold alone); if you host font files for download you must provide the full package; the FAQ does not require attribution merely for using the fonts on a page (visibility requirements apply to webfont *services*) — [OFL FAQ](https://openfontlicense.org/ofl-faq/)
- OFL summary: fonts may be used, studied, modified and redistributed freely as long as not sold by themselves; derivatives must stay under OFL; documents made with the fonts are not affected — [openfontlicense.org/ofl-fonts](https://openfontlicense.org/ofl-fonts/)
- Source Sans Pro: Copyright Adobe, OFL 1.1, Reserved Font Name "Source" — [Google Fonts metadata mirror](https://os.unplugged.com/werunplugged/unplugged-system/src/branch/main/external/google-fonts/source-sans-pro/METADATA); now distributed as Source Sans 3 on Google Fonts — [Region Nordjylland design guide](https://rn.dk/Service/Nyheder-presse-og-kommunikation/Designguide/Skrifttyper)
- Inter is free and open source; its maintainer advises against the Google Fonts build if avoidable (noted as outdated/no italics at the time) — [rsms/inter mirror](https://gitblind.noratr.app/rsms/inter)
- Lato: OFL 1.1 — [Lato OFL](https://git.stg.centos.org/source-git/lato-fonts/blob/105dc92cb2f92e2ec441b9a2b343234a20bed074/f/Lato2OFL)

**Icons**
- Lucide: ISC License (Copyright Lucide Icons and Contributors, 2026) for the set, plus MIT (Cole Bemis, 2013–present) for icons derived from Feather; both require the notice to appear in all copies — [lucide.dev/license](https://lucide.dev/license)

- Apache 2.0 redistribution requires including the licence, marking modified files and carrying forward NOTICE attributions; it grants no rights to Cesium trade names or marks — [CesiumJS LICENSE.md](https://github.com/CesiumGS/cesium/blob/main/LICENSE.md)

**Data and imagery**
- Cesium Moon tileset use is governed by Cesium ion Terms of Service, Global 3D Content terms and the Content Usage and Attribution Guide; "may not be copied or used except under the terms described" — [Cesium Moon asset page](https://cesium.com/platform/cesium-ion/content/cesium-moon/); [Cesium Moon Terrain page](https://cesium.com/platform/cesium-ion/content/cesium-moon-terrain)

### Inferences
- A compliant `/licences` page for the site should list: fonts (OFL text + copyright lines, renamed if subset), icon notices (ISC/MIT), library licences (MIT/Apache-2.0 with NOTICE, BSD-3, ISC), terrain data terms (Cesium ion or LRO PDS public-domain data if self-hosted), and an explicit statement that NASA imagery is used under NASA media guidelines with no NASA insignia or implied endorsement.

- Google Fonts' own FAQ page is JavaScript-rendered and returned only a heading; the OFL text/FAQ at openfontlicense.org is used instead.

- Licence of Crameri's colour-map files not confirmed from the fetched page.

## Anti-patterns that make a research site look amateur (synthesis across sources)

### Takeaway
The failures the exemplars avoid are: decoration without data (chartjunk, stock space imagery, neon/glow UI), motion without consent (autoplay, parallax), numbers without provenance or uncertainty, unlabelled or unitless axes, rainbow/jet colour maps, and licence ambiguity.

### Cited Findings
- Chartjunk and decoration that distorts data are Tufte's named failures — [Wikipedia: Edward Tufte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Tufte)
- Rainbow/jet colour maps are perceptually non-uniform and unreadable for colour-vision-deficient readers; use perceptually uniform, ordered, CVD-friendly, B&W-safe maps — Crameri, Shephard & Heron (2020) via [fabiocrameri.ch](https://www.fabiocrameri.ch/colourmaps/); do not interpolate discrete rainbow sets — [Paul Tol](https://sronpersonalpages.nl/~pault/)
- Conveying information by colour alone (no shapes/labels) and red–green pairs are explicit Okabe-Ito violations — [Okabe & Ito](https://jfly.uni-koeln.de/color/)
- Parallax and interaction-triggered motion that cannot be disabled fail SC 2.3.3; autoplaying motion over 5 s without a pause control fails SC 2.2.2 (Level A) — [W3C SC 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html); [W3C SC 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)
- Fitting a regression and presenting it without caveat "push[es] people to the wrong conclusions" (Simpson's paradox) — [Observable Plot](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/observablehq/plot/main/docs/marks/linear-regression.md)
- Rambling is "an easy failure mode"; results must be reported accurately and weaknesses disclosed — [Distill](https://distill.pub/journal/)
- Using the NASA insignia or implying NASA endorsement is prohibited — [NASA Media Usage Guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/multimedia/guidelines/index.html)
- Earth imagery layers on another celestial body are explicitly warned against in Cesium's Moon example — [Cesium Moon](https://cesium.com/platform/cesium-ion/content/cesium-moon/)
- A full-screen CSS blend/glow overlay over WebGL cost 30–45 ms/frame — decorative post-effects have a measurable price — [Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/?p=119712)

### Inferences
- "Fake numbers" (telemetry readouts that are not driven by the simulation state, or that show precision beyond the model's) are the research-site equivalent of chartjunk; every on-screen number should be traceable to a logged variable with its units and update rate.
- Stock star-fields and lens flares add nothing a reviewer can verify; a black sky with a correctly placed Sun and Earth (as NASA SVS renders) is both more honest and visually stronger.
- Neon/gamer UI (glowing borders, scanlines, saturated gradients) conflicts with every exemplar's palette (≤9 muted hues, hairline rules at 10% alpha) and with WCAG contrast practice; it also costs GPU time on integrated graphics.

### Gaps
- No empirical study was found that tests how researchers judge credibility of visual styles; these anti-patterns are inferred from design guidelines and the exemplars' consistent avoidance of them.
