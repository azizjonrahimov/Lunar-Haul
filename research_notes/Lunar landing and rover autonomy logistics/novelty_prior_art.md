# Prior-art map and novelty assessment: integrated lunar surface logistics coupling landings with surface transport (current to 2026-10-15)

Scope note. This map covers lunar/Mars surface-logistics simulations (DES, agent-based, optimisation) 2000–2026; landing-pad placement and base layout; landing-slot/pad scheduling; "value of landing precision"; plume-ejecta keep-out coupled with surface operations; multi-fidelity simulation with learned controllers; space-logistics network models (SpaceNet, GMCNF, Ho group); ISRU supply-chain models; NASA "marketplace" statements; and any published scaling relations. The LunarSim baseline (nine-site south-pole base, multi-owner rover fleet, four dispatch rules, fragmentation law, capacity ladders, ~$300/kg moved) is the user's own work and is treated as the thing to be extended, not as prior art.

Method note. About 45 web searches were run before the session's search budget was exhausted; full texts were read for 20+ sources (NASA white papers, OTPS report, Metzger papers, Ishimatsu GMCNF, Ho review, Takubo HRL, Chen multifidelity, Stromgren, Owens, LTV teleoperation study, several arXiv/PLOS/PMC/Frontiers articles). ScienceDirect, MDPI and Taylor & Francis full texts returned HTTP 403, so those items rest on abstracts or search snippets and are flagged "(snippet only)". Google Scholar, NTRS full-text search, DSpace@MIT, Purdue e-Pubs and CU Scholar were not directly searchable with the tools available. The search log is in the final Gaps section.

## KQ0. What has actually been published, by category (the prior-art map)

### Takeaway
The literature splits cleanly into (i) campaign-level space-logistics network models that stop at "the lunar surface" as one node, (ii) surface-engineering studies of pads and plume ejecta that stop at "standoff distance", (iii) rover-autonomy and teleoperation studies that stop at the single vehicle, and (iv) a handful of 2025–2026 agent-based/DES base models that include rovers but not landers' dispersion or plume. No retrieved work couples lander touchdown statistics, plume keep-out and rover autonomy inside one logistics model with cost and service-level outputs.

### Cited Findings

#### A. Surface-logistics simulations (DES / agent-based / optimisation), 2000–2026
- Findiesen, Bayer, Born, McCormick, Bienhoff (Boeing), "Simulation and Analysis of Architectures for a Lunar Surface Outpost", AIP Conf. Proc. 969, 851–860, STAIF 2008. Discrete-event model of south-pole outpost crew activities, ISRU, science and maintenance, used to derive cargo demand (spares, logistics) and lunar-lander requirements. Did NOT model landing dispersion, plume, pad placement or a rover fleet with dispatch rules; full text is paywalled, so model details are from the abstract/snippet only — [AIP PDF](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/acp/article-pdf/969/1/851/11367665/851_1_online.pdf)
- Stromgren, Galan, Cirillo (SAIC/NASA LaRC), "Micro-Logistics Analysis for Human Space Exploration" (AIAA; Constellation-era). System-dynamics (iThink) model of stowage, usage, disposal and carrier handling at a lunar outpost; assumes only "one or two possible opportunities to deliver cargo per year" and that carriers must be "unloaded from the lander, moved to the habitat, and connected". Did NOT model the move itself (distance, vehicles, dispatch), landing error or plume — [NASA PDF](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/logistics_stromgren_micrologistics.pdf)
- Owens, Cirillo, Stromgren, Cho, Lynch, Vega, "Integrated Logistics and Supportability Challenges of Sustained Human Lunar Exploration", ICES-2022-90. States that "lunar landers typically land some distance away from existing assets, and therefore logistics must be transported across the surface to other elements rather than passed through the hatch of two docked vehicles". Artemis Base Camp: ~30-day crewed, ~335-day uncrewed per year. Did NOT quantify the surface transfer step — [NTRS PDF](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220006045/downloads/Owens%20et%20al%20-%20Integrated%20Logistics%20and%20Supportability%20Challenges%20of%20Sustained%20Human%20Lunar%20Exploration%20v4.docx.pdf)
- Vera, Berea, Kennedy (George Mason), "Lunar base agent-based modeling – A benchmark for simulating crewed space missions", PLOS ONE, 27 May 2026. Mesa ABM; baseline 4 astronauts, 2 rovers, 3 months (2,016 h), ~50 km² grid, 10,000 Monte Carlo runs; rovers move 10 grid cells (1 km each) per hour step and recharge/maintain at base; shuttles resupply every 60 days with a small accident probability. Results are crew-centric (e.g., 748.2 ± 72.8 tasks completed, ~20% of scheduled). Did NOT model landing dispersion, cargo delivery variability, plume, or learned controllers; authors list "multiple habitats and logistics infrastructure" as future work — [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0348882)
- Wang, "Agent Mars: Multi-Agent Simulation for Multi-Planetary Life Exploration and Settlement", arXiv 2602.13291 (Feb 2026). 93 LLM-style agents in seven command layers; one scripted scenario is "two vehicles request overlapping landing slots", resolved by an ATC agent allocating landing windows and no-fly zones. Metrics are messages/time/failure rate (e.g., 32.9 s vs 42.4 s wall time with/without shared memory). Did NOT model physical transport, landing dynamics, dispersion or cost; light-time and bandwidth are future work — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13291v1)
- "A lunar surface scenario simulation applying adaptive operating systems" (Journal of Space Safety Engineering, ~2022). Rovers return to base when full or failed; base services one rover at a time so arrivals queue. (snippet only) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2468896722001409)
- Okonkwo, Awolusi et al., "Hybrid simulation framework for autonomous robotics lunar launch and landing pad construction" (Automation in Construction-type venue, 2025). DES + social-force model + A* path planning, Monte Carlo variability; compares equipment configurations on productivity, energy and duration and reports "a nonlinear relationship between equipment quantity and construction performance". Did NOT include landings, plume events during operations, cargo logistics or learned controllers. (abstract/snippet only) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0926580525006569)
- Linde, Lindmark, Ålstig, Servin, "A simulation framework for autonomous lunar construction work", arXiv 2505.22091 (2025). Physics-based multibody + deformable-terrain simulation of an excavator and a dump truck under behaviour-tree control via ROS2; metrics are work time and energy. No learned controller, no landings, two machines only — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22091)
- "A Mixed-Logical-Dynamical Model for Multi-Robot Lunar Construction Task Allocation" (IFAC-PapersOnLine, 2025). MIQP under MPC with battery dynamics and charging; reports base built ~16.4% sooner with ~16.0% less robot energy than a conventional method. (snippet only) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405896325022347)
- PRO-ACT, "Multi-robot cooperation for lunar base assembly and construction", i-SAIRAS 2020: heterogeneous robots unload, transport and assemble an ISRU plant; compares negotiation-based allocation with ILP/MILP — [USRA PDF](https://www.hou.usra.edu/meetings/isairas2020fullpapers/pdf/5073.pdf)
- "Collaborative mission planning for heterogeneous lunar exploration vehicles via adaptive multi-objective optimization with orbital-guided pathfinding", Acta Astronautica 2026: bidirectional A* path costs feeding a multi-objective metaheuristic. (snippet only) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0094576526005473)
- Ahn, de Weck, Geng, Klabjan, "Column generation based heuristics for a generalized location routing problem with profits arising in space exploration", EJOR 223 (2012) 47–59 (Martian surface vehicle routing) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejor.2012.06.018); Lee & Ahn, "Integrated optimization of planetary rover layout and exploration routes", Engineering Optimization 50(1) 2018 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/0305215X.2017.1299718). Both cited in the Ho review as the only surface-routing items; neither involves landers, pads or multi-owner fleets.
- kalasim "Lunar Mining" tutorial: toy DES of harvester robots shuttling ice to a base, concluding five robots suffice — [kalasim](https://www.kalasim.org/animation/lunar_mining/)
- Krawciw, Olmedo, Rehmatullah, Desjardins-Goulet, Toupin, Barfoot (U Toronto/MDA), "Lunar Rover Cargo Transport: Mission Concept and Field Test", arXiv 2601.03371 / IEEE (2026). Mission concept: cargo moved between landers and a habitat about 5 km apart (NASA estimate), up to 10,000 kg/yr; rover 800 kg unloaded, 300 kg payload; 182 kg self-lifting cargo module. Field test (CSA analogue terrain, 120 m × 60 m): 18 semi-autonomous teach segments totalling 164.2 m took 2:02:14; autonomous repeat took 10:32 (8.6% of the time); path RMSE 0.10 m; 5 s delay each way over 2 Mbps. Did NOT model fleet-level logistics, throughput, cost or landing dispersion; "automatic alignment to landers ... so that cargo landers need not land in the same spot" is future work — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03371v1)

#### B. Space-logistics network models (SpaceNet, GMCNF, Ho group) and their stated limits
- SpaceNet (MIT de Weck / Simchi-Levi, v1.3 2007; v2.5 2008–2010, Apache-2.0, repo archived 2023): demand-driven discrete-event simulation over a time-expanded network with transport/wait/transfer processes; lunar scenarios include a Shackleton-rim hub-and-spoke outpost built over seven years at ~3 missions/yr with 7.5 kg/person/day consumables (scenario details snippet only) — [MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2007/spacenet); [GitHub](https://github.com/ptgrogan/spacenet); [AIAA Space 2006 paper, NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20170011140/downloads/20170011140.pdf); [Grogan et al. flexible modular approach](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/265923013_A_flexible_modular_approach_to_integrated_space_exploration_campaign_logistics_modeling_simulation_and_analysis). The surface is a node; no landing dispersion, plume or rover dispatch.
- Ishimatsu, de Weck, Hoffman, Ohkami, Shishko, "Generalized Multi-Commodity Network Flow Model for the Earth-Moon-Mars Logistics System", JSR 53(1), 2016. LP over a multigraph with flow equilibrium/transformation/concurrency matrices; baseline cuts total launch mass to LEO by 68% vs Mars DRA 5.0; lunar ISRU pays off above a productivity of ~1.9 kg/yr per kg of plant (abstract says 1.8). Stated limitations: deterministic, linear, static snapshot; future work is risk, nonlinearity, time evolution and acting as a "front-end tool" to SpaceNet. No surface transport between lunar sites — [MIT PDF](http://strategic.mit.edu/JSR_Final_Manuscript_Ishimatsu.pdf)
- Ho, "Space Logistics Modeling and Optimization: Review of the State of the Art", arXiv 2306.01107v8 (Sept 2024; AIAA 2024-1275). Categorises by application (ISAM, multi-mission campaigns, mega-constellations) and method (network flow, queueing, inventory). Explicitly lists "surface vehicle routing and logistics" among topics NOT covered; calls Takubo et al. "one of the first studies that incorporated machine learning into space logistics"; says most works use simplified trajectory/vehicle/operational models and that higher-fidelity simulation to validate them is one of the "largely unexplored research areas" — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.01107v8)
- Takubo, Chen, Ho, "Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning Framework for Stochastic Spaceflight Campaign Design", JSR 59 (2022) / arXiv 2103.08981. RL chooses campaign-level decisions (vehicle design, ISRU deployment, demand assignment); MILP schedules transport; lunar case with stochastic ISRU production/decay. RL is NOT a vehicle controller and no surface operations are modelled — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.08981)
- Chen, Sarton du Jonchay, Hou, Ho, "Multifidelity Space Mission Planning and Infrastructure Design Framework for Space Resource Logistics", arXiv 1910.04265 (AIAA 2019-4134). "Multifidelity" means varying the granularity of commodity types in the network LP, giving 70–95% computation-time reductions with small cost error. Not multi-physics, no learned surrogates, no surface transport — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.04265)
- Gkaravela, Lee, Chen, "Distributed Space Resource Logistics Architecture Optimization under Economies of Scale", arXiv 2504.16385 (2025): piecewise-linear sizing/cost models embedded in network planning; distributed vs concentrated ISRU; no landing precision or surface transport — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16385). Gkaravela & Chen, "Logistics Analysis for Lunar Post-Mission Disposal", arXiv 2502.13679 (SciTech 2025): multi-commodity flow of ISRU byproducts; claims a "threefold cost reduction" for ISRU-enabled missions — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13679)
- "Evaluating the Periodic Sustainability of Cislunar Logistics Architectures: A Reproducible Methodology with an Artemis III–Derived Case Study", Aerospace 13(9):845 (2026): uses the SpaceNet data model (self-loops and duplicate arcs not represented). (snippet only; MDPI returned 403) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/aerospace13090845)
- Other Ho-group/ISRU network items (no surface ops): event-driven network model with cislunar propellant supply chain — [arXiv 1904.09364](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.09364); hierarchical campaign scheduling with ISRU — [arXiv 2308.00632](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.00632); stochastic interplanetary supply chain planning — [Space (SPJ) 2023](https://spj.science.org/doi/10.34133/space.0014); MIT ISRU oxygen-plant architecture trade (AIAA 2008-7823) — [MIT PDF](http://strategic.mit.edu/docs/3_111_AIAA-2008-7823-ISRU-Architecture.pdf)
- Grogan & de Weck, "Federated simulation and gaming framework for a decentralized space-based resource economy", Earth and Space 2012, pp. 1468–1477 (cited as ref. [84] in the Ho review): the closest antecedent for multi-actor/marketplace simulation in space logistics; full text not retrieved — [via Ho review](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.01107v8)

#### C. Landing-pad placement, base layout, plume keep-out
- NASA ESDMD, "Lunar Logistics, Mobility, and Cargo" (Bhakta, M2M Architecture Workshop, Feb 2025). Relocation-distance drivers: lander shadowing (tens of m, ">50 m"), lander blast ejecta (">1,000 m", for separation from infrastructure or lander ascent), aggregation into habitation zones from available regional landing areas ("up to 5,000 m"); "mobility of cargo and assets ... at ranges of 5 to 5,000 m"; cargo demand 2,000–10,000 kg/yr; LTV uncrewed capacity 800 kg (1,600 kg reduced performance); lander gap 500–12,000 kg; "LTV Standard Road Traversal map (~4 km)"; "Large-scale mobility is not simply scaled up small-scale mobility". No cost or delay quantification — [NASA PDF](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/iparch10-wp-logistics-mobility-cargo.pdf?emrc=57be82)
- NASA, "Lunar Surface Cargo" (ACR24 white paper, 2024): recurring logistics 2,500–10,000 kg/yr plus elements up to 15,000 kg; CLPS task orders 70–475 kg, HDL 0–12,000/15,000 kg, Argonaut up to 2,100 kg; cargo lander reference mission must land "with sufficient precision" and may perform end-of-life "relocation" so cargo is not affected — [NASA PDF](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/acr24-lunar-surface-cargo.pdf)
- NASA, "Safe and Precise Landing at Lunar Sites" (ACR23 white paper, 2023): precision landing "within 50 to 100 meters"; PL&HA "reduces post-landing travel distances between surface assets"; "Whether using robotics or crew members to transfer logistics items, a reduction in distance means a reduction in transfer time and risk"; worked example of 100 m vs 50 m landing-ellipse radius around a crater; Apollo 12 sandblasted Surveyor 3 at 155 m; "NASA currently lacks direct in-situ measurements of PSI phenomena, leaving predictions largely qualitative and uncertain"; co-location "may require asset hardening". Qualitative only — [LPI PDF](https://www.lpi.usra.edu/lunar/strategies/resources/M2M-ACR2023_SafePreciseLunarLandings.pdf)
- Metzger, Britt, Cannon, Kinzel, Fontes, Kring, "Lander plume effects at outpost on Shackleton crater rim", SSERVI ESF 2019. Ejecta mass scales as thrust^2.5 (Lane & Metzger 2015); LM ejected ~2.6 t; a 40 t lander delivering 9 t could eject (40/5)^2.5 = 181× more, ~470 t worst case; erosion within ~12 m radius; ejection velocity scales with log(lander mass); Constellation outpost landing zone 2.2 km from and 290 m below the habitation zone; particles reach it at ejection angles of 10.5° (1 µm) to >50° (>1 mm), so "additional mitigation may be needed". No operations/time dimension — [LPI PDF](https://www.lpi.usra.edu/exploration/publications/MetzgerEtAl_SSERVI%20ESF2019_LanderPlumeEjecta.pdf)
- Metzger & Autry, "The Cost of Lunar Landing Pads with a Trade Study of Construction Methods", New Space 2023 / arXiv 2205.00378. Pad cost $229M at $300K/kg transport, $130M at $100K/kg, $47M below $10K/kg; outer-zone method switches from sintering to polymer below ~$110K/kg; program delay cost = 25% of program present value × discount rate × delay time; launch cadence caps the value of faster construction; includes a "distant future" $300/kg transport case; pad sizing carries a 5 m landing-accuracy margin (snippet). Did NOT model number of landings, rover logistics, or scheduling — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.00378); [New Space](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1089/space.2022.0015)
- Anderson, Saxena, Metzger, "Mitigating plume surface interactions using lunar craters", npj Space Exploration 2(28), 2026. Ballistic ejecta from 238 small craters (LROC NAC DTMs); "umbrella zones" ~100 m for an 8 m lander, ~200 m for a 1 m rover at d/D = 0.15, >500 m in the deepest craters; cites a 200 m base-landing separation from earlier work "with some suggesting 2–3 km"; describes a rover sheltering in an umbrella zone during ascent. Does NOT model rover routes, timing or logistics; off-centre landings within the 100 m margin not modelled — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13263143/)
- Menges & Cannon, "Natural Landing Pads on the Moon and Mars", Acta Astronautica 242 (2026) 219–235: remote-sensing suitability index (slope, roughness, rock abundance, competency) with Monte Carlo weights; pad radii 25–100 m citing the Artemis 100 m accuracy requirement; heavy landers may need standoffs of several km (snippet). No logistics coupling — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0094576526000536)
- "A design methodology for flat slab lunar landing and launch pad systems", Acta Astronautica 2025: lists minimum safe distances for lunar launch/landing operations as an open question. (snippet only) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0094576525001419)
- NASA OTPS, Swiney & Hernandez, "Lunar Landing and Operations Policy Analysis", Report 20220015973 (Sept 2022). Expects ≥22 surface missions in four years, half at the south pole; the 2011 Apollo-site 2 km "descent/landing boundary" rests on 1–10 cm particles travelling up to 1.5 km; quotes Wittal et al. (AAS 20-511, 2020): at 1 km an unprotected base might expect ~8 impacts per landing at 23.5 J each, at 5 km a 37% chance of impact, at 10 km 10%; "absent accurate models, standoff distances will need to be larger"; "landing and ascent infrastructure can reduce the need for ... standoff distances"; Artemis Accords "safety zones" are notification/coordination zones, not exclusion zones; recommends deconfliction mechanisms and that CLPS/HLS respect each other's zones. No scheduling, pricing or logistics model — [NASA PDF](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/nasa-otps-lunar-landing-and-operations-policy-analysis-final-report-2.pdf)
- Base-layout concepts: NASA Common Habitat Base Camp (habitation/landing/power/resource zones; three ATHLETEs to move one logistics module) — [NTRS PDF](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210020956/downloads/A%20Common%20Habitat%20Basecamp%20for%20Moon%20and%20Mars%20Surface%20Operations.pdf); Cohen mobile-base concepts (modules walk 5–10 km from the LZ) — [AIP CP-699](http://spacearchitect.org/pubs/AIP-CP-699-Cohen.pdf); 1980s "Lunar base launch and landing facilities conceptual design" (keep pads away from habitation until reliable surface transport exists) — [NSS PDF](https://nss.org/wp-content/uploads/Lunar-Bases-conference-2-116-Facility-Conceptual-Design.pdf); LSIC pads focus group (proximity to habitat listed alongside plume) — [NTRS PDF](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20205009125/downloads/Lunar%20Landing%20&%20Launch%20Pads%20v1.pdf); "System analysis of a Moon base at the south pole" (screens sites by slope, landing-ellipse size, distance to pole and PSRs; snippet only) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0094576521002289)

#### D. Value of landing precision
- Mehta, Kothandhapani, Vatsal, Head, Shah (TeamIndus/Brown), LPSC 2019 #1843: tiers — pinpoint 1–10 m (needs TRN), medium 10 m–1 km ("ensuring a short traverse"), coarse >1 km (IMU-only, "at lower costs"). Qualitative — [LPI PDF](https://www.hou.usra.edu/meetings/lpsc2019/pdf/1843.pdf)
- MSL Landing Site Engineering Constraints guide: traverse 100–150 m/sol, so 10 km ≈ 85–125 sols ≈ 13–19% of the nominal mission if all time went to driving — the closest thing to a quantified "ellipse-to-time" rule, but for Mars science, not logistics — [NASA PDF](https://marsoweb.nas.nasa.gov/landingsites/msl/memoranda/MSL_Eng_User_Guide_v4.5.1.pdf)
- NASA TRN impact story: Mars 2020 location error 3.2 km → ~40 m; safe-landing probability 80–85% → 99% — [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/stmd/impact-story-terrain-relative-navigation/); [NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/science-research/science-enabling-technology/technology-highlights/terrain-relative-navigation-landing-between-the-hazards/)
- ESA Argonaut: ~1,500 kg cargo; landing accuracy 250 m on flight 1 improving to 50 m by flight 3; first mission by end-2030; missions every 2–3 years; Mission 1 carries a robotic surface-mobility demonstrator — [ESA](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Human_and_Robotic_Exploration/Exploration/Argonaut_Europe_s_lunar_lander_programme)
- Delivery price ladder (Astrobotic Payload User's Guide 2021/22): $300K/kg to lunar orbit, $1.2M/kg to the surface, $4.5M/kg delivered on a rover — [Astrobotic PDF](https://www.astrobotic.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/PUGLanders_011222.pdf)
- Lugo et al. 2022 (SciTech 2022-0609) human-scale lander Monte Carlo: lunar dispersion dominated by state, vehicle and navigation uncertainty (Mars by atmosphere) — [NTRS PDF](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210024283/downloads/nav_moon_scitech2022_v7.pdf)
- NovaMoon (arXiv 2602.08432): PNT infrastructure that shrinks landing ellipses "would maximize payload capability", unquantified — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.08432)

#### E. Learned lander descent controllers (what they report, what they omit)
- Gaudet, Linares, Furfaro, 6-DOF DRL powered descent (PPO, thrust per engine), arXiv 1810.08719: target landing-error ellipse <5 m radius; abstract gives no achieved dispersion statistics or Monte Carlo count; no plume — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08719)

- Aklan et al., "Integrated Lander-Propulsion-GNC Framework", arXiv 2604.23013 (2026): successive convexification (not learned), "sub-50-meter landing precision" in Monte Carlo; no plume — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23013)

#### F. Rover autonomy, teleoperation latency, learned driving on real terrain
- Litaker, Beaton, Li, Lewis (NASA JSC), "LTV Remote Teleoperation Studies Under Four Lunar Communication Latencies" (IEEE Aerospace 2024). Goal: can an LTV cover 6 km in 24 h remotely. Speed including stops 3.24 kph at 0 s vs 2.56 kph at 4 s (12% slower, t(47)=2.26, p=.028); 6–8 s delay "can result in a dangerous state" (rock contact, crater); moving time +24% at 8 s vs 6 s; workload 54% → 68% from 0 s to 4 s; eight operators, ~30 min training (April 2023), ~3 h per latency (August 2023). Did NOT derive intervention rates per km or a fleet-level consequence — [NTRS PDF](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240011392/downloads/Teleops%20IEEE%20Paper%20FY24%20FINAL.pdf?attachment=true)
- Chen, Ye, Qiu, Li (Wuhan/HUST), "High-resolution accessibility and energy cost assessment of 31 priority PSRs at the lunar south pole", Frontiers Astron. Space Sci., 30 Mar 2026. LOLA DEMs at 5 m (87–90°S), 10 m (85–87°S), 20 m (80–85°S); terramechanics (compaction, bulldozing, gravity) for a 500 kg four-wheel rover; slope-penalised Dijkstra; 600 traverses per PSR; median paths ~12–50 km; round-trip mechanical energy 0.4–4.4 kWh; slip/efficiency sensitivity adds +24% (Tier I) to +47% (Tier III). No learned controller, no fleet/logistics — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/astronomy-and-space-sciences/articles/10.3389/fspas.2026.1764826/full)
- Tanaka & Malki (U Houston), DQN global path planning on a 5 m LOLA DEM with thermal and battery models, Sensors 24(3):844, 2024; violations appear once motion stochasticity >0; no slip, intervention or logistics — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10857624/)
- Bouton et al. (JPL), "Learning All-Terrain Locomotion for a Planetary Rover with Actively Articulated Suspension", arXiv 2606.06790 (2026): RL in DARTS with Bekker-Wong terramechanics; cost of transport −37% on a 20° dry-sand slope; zero-shot hardware transfer — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06790)
- Chiu, Wilson, Tumbar, Sukhatme, Myint, "Learning to Drive on Mars", arXiv 2609.24952 (2026): 500 sols / 45 km of Perseverance data with slip and tilt; traversability AUROC 0.874; not embedded in any operations model — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24952)
- Huang & Gao, "End-to-End Vision-Language-Action Policies for Autonomous Lunar Rover Docking", arXiv 2610.19224 (Oct 2026): BC-style policy from safety-aware expert demonstrations in a lunar simulator; no cargo logistics or dispersion — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19224)

#### G. Learned controllers embedded in logistics/DES models (terrestrial analogues)

## KQ1. Has anyone quantified how landing dispersion (m) or pad distance (km) changes surface delivery delay or cost? Any closed-form or empirical relation?

### Takeaway
No. The nearest items are qualitative (NASA 2023: "a reduction in distance means a reduction in transfer time and risk"), band-level (NASA 2025: 50 m / >1,000 m / up to 5,000 m relocation drivers), or from adjacent domains (MSL sols-per-km; Wittal impact-probability vs standoff; Metzger-Autry pad cost vs transport cost). No retrieved paper gives delivery delay or $/kg as a function of landing dispersion or lander-to-base distance.

### Cited Findings
- NASA (2023) states the direction of the effect but gives no function: precision landing "reduces post-landing travel distances between surface assets" and "a reduction in distance means a reduction in transfer time and risk" — [LPI PDF](https://www.lpi.usra.edu/lunar/strategies/resources/M2M-ACR2023_SafePreciseLunarLandings.pdf)
- NASA (Feb 2025) gives distance bands only: shadow >50 m, blast ejecta >1,000 m, aggregation up to 5,000 m; mobility demand "5 to 5,000 m"; no time or cost attached — [NASA PDF](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/iparch10-wp-logistics-mobility-cargo.pdf?emrc=57be82)
- Krawciw et al. (2026) adopt a NASA estimate of ~5 km lander-to-habitat and 10,000 kg/yr but do not vary either or compute cost — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03371v1)
- The only numeric distance-to-time rule found is for Mars science: 100–150 m/sol, so 10 km ≈ 85–125 sols (13–19% of MSL's nominal mission) — [NASA PDF](https://marsoweb.nas.nasa.gov/landingsites/msl/memoranda/MSL_Eng_User_Guide_v4.5.1.pdf)
- The only numeric distance-to-risk relation is impacts vs standoff (8 impacts/landing at 1 km; 37% at 5 km; 10% at 10 km), not a logistics quantity — [NASA OTPS](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/nasa-otps-lunar-landing-and-operations-policy-analysis-final-report-2.pdf)

- Ho's review excludes "surface vehicle routing and logistics" entirely, so the network-flow literature cannot contain such a relation — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.01107v8)

### Inferences
- A dispersion/standoff-to-cost law would be first-of-kind. The natural form (delivery delay or $/kg as a function of pad-to-site distance D, dispersion σ, rover speed under latency and intervention rate) has all its input ranges already published by NASA, which makes the result easy to position as "filling the number NASA left blank".

## KQ2. Has anyone scheduled rover operations around landing plume events, or priced landing-pad slots?

### Takeaway
No operations-scheduling or pricing study was found. Plume work ends at static standoff/umbrella-zone geometry; policy work (OTPS, Artemis Accords safety zones) describes notification and coordination but no scheduling model; the only "landing slot" treatment is a scripted coordination scenario in Agent Mars with no physics or price; terrestrial vertiport/airport slot literature exists but has not been ported to the Moon.

### Cited Findings
- Anderson et al. (2026) describe a rover "sheltered in an umbrella zone while the ascent stage launches" as a scenario, but model no routes, timing or schedules — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13263143/)
- OTPS recommends "mechanisms for consultation and coordination to deconflict surface operations", that CLPS and HLS missions "respect safety zones for each other", and notes safety zones are temporary and "not exclusion zones"; it never proposes timing, slots or fees — [NASA OTPS](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/nasa-otps-lunar-landing-and-operations-policy-analysis-final-report-2.pdf)

- Lunar traffic-management literature argues coordination is needed (≥22 missions in four years; "Guiding Lunar Growth", AMOS 2024; Georgia Tech 2025) and that the Moon has no significant traffic regulation, but offers no scheduler — [ADS](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024amos.conf...72B/abstract); [Georgia Tech](https://www.gatech.edu/news/2025/10/30/more-moon-missions-horizon-avoiding-crowding-collisions-will-be-growing-challenge); [Acta Astronautica 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0094576522005628)

- Metzger & Autry's pad economics stop at construction cost and program-delay cost; no per-landing fee, utilisation or slot value — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.00378)
- The only multi-actor economic simulation found in space logistics is Grogan & de Weck 2012 (federated decentralized resource economy), known here only by title — [via Ho review](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.01107v8)

### Inferences

- Pad-slot pricing via the marketplace auction already in LunarSim would be the first lunar-specific slot-pricing model; the nearest prior art is terrestrial slot coordination and LunarSim's own rover auction, so novelty is defensible if Grogan & de Weck 2012 is checked and cited.

## KQ3. Has anyone embedded trained RL/BC controllers inside a logistics simulation and reported logistics-level effects?

### Takeaway
Not in space logistics. In the space domain, RL has been used only at campaign level (Takubo et al.) and for single-vehicle control (landers, rovers) with vehicle-level metrics. In terrestrial logistics, RL policies are embedded in DES as dispatchers, not as vehicle controllers whose physical outcome distributions feed the DES. Ho's 2024 review names higher-fidelity validation of logistics models as a "largely unexplored" area.

### Cited Findings
- Takubo, Chen, Ho: RL optimises campaign decisions (vehicle design, ISRU deployment, demand assignment) with MILP below it; no vehicle controller; Ho's review calls it "one of the first studies that incorporated machine learning into space logistics" — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.08981); [review](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.01107v8)
- Chen et al. "multifidelity" refers to commodity granularity in an LP (70–95% faster), not to physics or learned surrogates — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.04265)
- RL lander papers report only vehicle metrics: <5 m target ellipse (Gaudet) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08719); 94.8% success in hazardous terrain (Iiyama) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.12432); 133.06 kg fuel vs 133.50 kg NLP, 100 test cases (Wang et al.) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.06722). None mention plume or logistics.
- Learned rover controllers report vehicle metrics only: CoT −37% on 20° sand (Bouton) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06790); AUROC 0.874 traversability from 500 sols (Chiu) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24952); VLA docking competitive with modular baseline (Huang & Gao) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19224); DQN on 5 m LOLA DEM with thermal/battery models (Tanaka & Malki) — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10857624/)
- LOLA-terrain rover energy work is classical, not learned: Dijkstra + terramechanics over 31 PSRs, 0.4–4.4 kWh round trips, slip sensitivity +24–47% (Chen et al. 2026) — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/astronomy-and-space-sciences/articles/10.3389/fspas.2026.1764826/full)
- Terrestrial pattern: RL dispatch policies trained inside a DES (haul trucks, taxis) and compared with rule-based dispatch; the learned object is the dispatcher — [Buildings 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings16112274); [arXiv 2307.07508](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2307.07508); [ScienceDirect 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S3050860625000389)
- Multi-fidelity DES switching (≥1.21× speed-up at 5% accuracy loss) exists but without learned controllers — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/7/10/1056)
- No RL powered-descent paper found that includes plume/dust-erosion in its reward or constraints; Chang'E-5 PSI analysis notes dust entrainment damages engines and blocks visibility, which could motivate such a term — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/361783566_The_Investigation_of_Plume-Regolith_Interaction_and_Dust_Dispersal_during_Chang'E-5_Descent_Stage)

### Inferences
- The "learned controller as stochastic generator" architecture — train lander and rover policies offline, sample their outcome distributions (dispersion, fuel, plume exposure; speed, slip, energy, intervention rate) inside the DES, and report throughput/$/kg/service level — has no space precedent and only partial terrestrial precedent (dispatcher-level RL). This is the strongest methodological novelty available.
- Because LOLA-based energy models exist (Chen et al. 2026) and LTV latency effects are measured (Litaker et al.), a learned rover driver can be validated against published numbers (e.g., 12% speed loss at 4 s; 0.4–4.4 kWh PSR round trips), which strengthens the paper.

## KQ4. Candidate novel contributions, ranked by how defensible "new" is, with closest prior work

### Takeaway
Five contributions remain open. The two most defensible are (1) a quantitative law linking landing dispersion / pad standoff / plume keep-out radius to delivery delay, service level and $/kg, and (2) plume-event-aware fleet scheduling with its cost. The learned-controller-in-the-loop architecture is the strongest methodological novelty. Pad-slot pricing and standoff-vs-traverse pad siting are defensible but have nearer antecedents.

### Cited Findings (ranking with closest prior work)
1. **Dispersion/standoff-to-logistics law** (delay, fill rate, $/kg as f(σ_land, D_pad, R_keepout, rover autonomy)).
