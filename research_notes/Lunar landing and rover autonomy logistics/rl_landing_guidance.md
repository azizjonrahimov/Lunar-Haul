# RL and learned guidance for planetary powered descent and landing (2017-2026), reward shaping, and the LunarLander-v3 / SB3 practicalities

Research date: 2026-10-15. Scope: RL / deep-learning controllers for lunar and Mars powered descent; reward shaping and failure modes; comparison methodology against classical guidance (Apollo, ZEM/ZEV, G-FOLD); the downstream-logistics gap; exact Gymnasium LunarLander-v3 definition; Stable-Baselines3 PPO settings; exporting a small MLP policy to JavaScript.

## Key Question 1 — Who has trained RL agents on realistic lunar/Mars descent, what did they achieve vs classical guidance, and what remains open?

### Takeaway
The only group with a complete, quantitative line of work on physics-based (non-toy) RL landing guidance is Gaudet / Linares / Furfaro / Scorsoglio (Univ. of Arizona + MIT): PPO policies in a 3-DoF and 6-DoF Mars simulator achieve ~1 m-class touchdown dispersion over 10,000-episode Monte Carlo, with fuel 4% above an energy-optimal DR/DV law and ~18% above a GPOPS open-loop optimum; recurrent "meta-RL" policies dramatically beat DR/DV under engine failure (0.3 m vs 144.9 m mean miss). Everything else is either toy-environment work (Gymnasium LunarLander), imitation of optimal control (ESA ACT G&CNets, Zhejiang/MIT groups), or very recent 3-DoF lunar work (Oct 2026 adversarial RL) with no published numbers. No RL policy has flown on a lander; the only flown "learned" G&C is on quadcopters. The open problems named by the authors are 6-DoF lunar extension, the fuel gap to the optimum, reality gap / certification, and reward design.

### Cited Findings

**Gaudet, Linares, Furfaro — "Deep Reinforcement Learning for Six Degree-of-Freedom Planetary Powered Descent and Landing" (arXiv Oct 2018; Advances in Space Research vol. 65 no. 7, 2020)**
- Method: PPO; integrated guidance-and-control policy mapping estimated lander state "directly to a commanded thrust for each engine"; goal is Mars pinpoint landing with a "landing error ellipse < 5 m radius" (design target, not a result) — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08719); journal record — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0273117719309305); OA manuscript — [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/137757)
- Environment fidelity: 6-DoF rigid body, 2000 kg wet mass, four throttleable thrusters each 1000-5000 N, Isp 225 s, Mars gravity [0 0 -3.7114] m/s², RK4 at 0.05 s, guidance update every 0.2 s; training ICs: downrange 0-2000 m, crossrange ±1000 m, altitude 2300-2400 m, descent rate -90 to -70 m/s; training uncertainty mass 1900-2100 kg, gravity perturbed ±0.07 m/s² (x,y) and [3.64, 3.79] m/s² (z), inertia noise ±100 kg·m² diagonal; test-time Gaussian disturbance force with per-episode mean in ±100 N and std 100 N, mass ±5% — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.08719)
- Network: 4-layer tanh MLPs; policy hidden widths 10·obs_dim, sqrt(n_h1·n_h3), 10·act_dim; PPO with KL target 0.001, 120 episodes per rollout/update, ADAM; "Optimization was terminated after 300,000 episodes"; "A policy optimized in the 3-DOF RL environment converges around 70 times faster than in the 6-DOF environment"; trained-policy run time "<< 1ms" — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.08719); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.08719)
- Results (10,000-episode nominal Monte Carlo, Table 7): downrange position 0.4 ± 0.7 m (min -2.9, max 4.5); crossrange -0.1 ± 0.7 m (-5.2 to 2.5); downrange velocity 0.06 ± 0.03 m/s; crossrange velocity -0.01 ± 0.04 m/s; elevation (vertical) velocity -0.93 ± 0.08 m/s (-1.32 to -0.49); pitch -0.016 ± 0.010 rad; roll -0.003 ± 0.011 rad; glideslope mean 22.02, min 7.50 — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.08719)
- Fuel (same 10,000 episodes): 6-DoF RL 291 ± 15 kg (257-352); 3-DoF RL 291 ± 14 kg; 3-DoF DR/DV 279 ± 14 kg; text: "We see a 4% increase in fuel consumption for the 6-DOF RL agent as compared to 3-DOF DR/DV"; vs GPOPS open-loop optimum 250 kg: "making the RL policy fuel consumption 18 percent higher than GPOPS optimal" — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.08719); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.08719)
- Divert test: 5000 Monte Carlo runs with an 800 m downrange + 800 m crossrange divert commanded at 1500 m altitude: average fuel increase 30 kg — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.08719)
- Larger deployment ellipses (20,000 episodes each): 9 km² ellipse 6-DoF RL 308 ± 25 kg vs DR/DV 297 ± 23 kg; 12 km² (3000 m altitude) 340 ± 31 kg vs 319 ± 27 kg — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.08719)
- Not reported: explicit success-rate percentage, glideslope-violation counts, wall-clock training time, discount-factor values — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.08719)

**Gaudet, Linares, Furfaro — "Adaptive Guidance and Integrated Navigation with Reinforcement Meta-Learning" (arXiv Apr 2019; Acta Astronautica vol. 169, pp. 180-190)**
- Method: PPO with a recurrent (GRU) policy and value function, unrolled 1/20/60 (and 120/200) steps; frozen weights adapt via hidden state; baselines are DR/DV energy-optimal guidance (given true mass) and a non-recurrent RL policy — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.09865); [MIT DSpace record](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/135440)
- Environment: Mars 3-DoF point mass, 2000 kg nominal, thrust 2000-15000 N, Isp 225 s, 0.05 s integration, 0.2 s navigation period; training randomization: environmental acceleration ±0.2 m/s², mass ±10% — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.09865)
- Engine-failure experiment (failure probability 0.5; lateral thrust cut by 2.5x, vertical by 1.5x), terminal position / velocity norm (mean / std / max): DR/DV 144.9 / 227.5 / 1322.9 m and 19.51 / 19.90 / 63.13 m/s; non-recurrent RL 0.7 / 0.3 / 2.2 m and 1.00 / 0.62 / 4.76 m/s; Meta-RL 20-step 0.3 / 0.2 / 1.2 m and 0.98 / 0.06 / 1.15 m/s — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.09865)
- High mass-variation experiment (Isp divided by 6 so ~1200 kg average fuel, 1600 kg peak): DR/DV 0.4 / 1.5 / 19.6 m, 0.63 / 0.96 / 5.39 m/s; RL 0.7 / 0.2 / 3.7 m, 0.92 / 0.26 / 5.25 m/s; Meta-RL 60-step 0.6 / 0.2 / 1.1 m, 1.06 / 0.05 / 1.21 m/s — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.09865)
- Integrated guidance+navigation from raw Doppler-radar altimeter beams over a Mars DTM: non-recurrent RL 131 / 101 / 796 m vs Meta-RL 200-step 59 / 42 / 288 m terminal position; terminal velocity 48 vs 23 m/s mean (i.e., not a safe landing from raw beams unless beams are target-pointed, where meta-RL reaches 0.3 / 1.2 / 116.0 m and 1.61 / 0.60 / 64.84 m/s) — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.09865)
- Not reported: success percentages and fuel for most experiments, discount factor value — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.09865)

**Furfaro, Scorsoglio, Linares, Massari — "Adaptive Generalized ZEM-ZEV Feedback Guidance for Planetary Landing via a Deep Reinforcement Learning Approach" (arXiv Mar 2020; Acta Astronautica)**
- Method: RL (actor-critic with REINFORCE-style updates; RBF Gaussian actor outputting ZEM/ZEV gains K_R, K_V and T_f; ELM critic) adapts the gains of classical ZEM/ZEV rather than replacing it; Mars 3-DoF, m_wet 1905 kg, m_dry 1505 kg, Isp 225 s, six thrusters at 3.1 kN, glide-slope limit 4° — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2003.02182); [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.02182v1)
- Fuel (single nominal trajectories, Table 4): 2D: A-ZEM/ZEV 382.75 kg, classical ZEM/ZEV 385.51 kg, GPOPS 352.59 kg; 3D: A-ZEM/ZEV 376.54 kg, classical 378.81 kg, GPOPS 357.25 kg; TOF 84.1 s (ZEM/ZEV) vs 64.7-64.8 s (GPOPS) — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2003.02182)
- Constraint handling: classical ZEM/ZEV violates the 4° glide slope in 2D; the adaptive version does not; 1000-trial 3D Monte Carlo reported as reaching target "without ever violating the constraint", final velocity "below a safe 5 cm/s"; training 2D 503 iterations / 1.78 h, 3D 804 iterations / 20.68 h — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2003.02182)

### Inferences
- "Realistic" RL landing results are essentially Mars 3-/6-DoF (Gaudet et al.); the lunar RL literature is either image-based (Scorsoglio, numbers not retrievable here), toy-Gym, or 3-DoF-only and very new (Oct 2026). A credible lunar 6-DoF RL benchmark with Monte Carlo statistics against DR/DV / GPOPS / convex optimum appears not to exist yet.
- Reported fuel gaps cluster: RL ≈ +4% vs energy-optimal feedback law and ≈ +18% vs open-loop fuel optimum (Gaudet); adaptive ZEM/ZEV ≈ +5-9% vs GPOPS (Furfaro); imitation/BC ≈ 0.1-1% in nominal conditions but brittle (Holt/Origer/Izzo). RL's selling point in every serious paper is robustness (engine failure, mass/thrust uncertainty, OOD), not optimality.
- Training cost figures (300k episodes for 6-DoF; 3-DoF ~70x faster; 1-24 h for PPO G&CNets; ~21 h for 3D adaptive ZEM/ZEV) suggest a 3-DoF lunar PPO study is feasible on a workstation, while 6-DoF needs substantial compute.

### Gaps
- Numerical results for Scorsoglio et al. (JSR 2021 image-based meta-RL) could not be retrieved (AIAA 403; seminar/announcement pages contain no numbers).
- You et al. JGCD abstract/results unavailable (403). Li & Wang 2025 and the Ciabatti ASCEND/2022 numbers rely on search-summary text only.
- No 2022-2026 physics-based 6-DoF lunar RL paper with Monte Carlo numbers was found before the web-search budget ran out; the Chinese-language 2025 "secure RL" paper turned out to use the toy LunarLander-v2.
- No published sim-to-real or hardware-in-the-loop evidence for an RL landing policy on a lander testbed was found; the G-FOLD Xombie flights and SPLICE New Shepard flights are classical-guidance heritage.

## Key Question 2 — Which reward-shaping terms do the serious papers use, and what reward-hacking / failure behaviours are documented?

### Takeaway
The dominant physics-based design (Gaudet et al.) is a dense velocity-field-tracking shaping term plus a control-effort penalty, a constant per-step "stay alive" bonus, large penalties for constraint violation, and a sparse terminal bonus gated on position/velocity/attitude limits, with separate discount rates for shaping vs terminal reward. Documented failure modes are: agents preferring early termination (crashing / violating attitude limits) to avoid accumulating negative shaping, hovering under per-step rewards, the toy LunarLander's "float-and-never-land" behaviour, and loss of nominal precision after adversarial training; GAE is incompatible with multi-rate discounting.

### Cited Findings
- Gaudet 6-DoF per-step reward (Eq. 30, Table 5): α‖v − v_targ‖ with α = −0.01; β‖F_B‖ with β = −0.05 (thrust/fuel proxy); γ·any(q > q_lim) with γ = −100 (attitude-limit violation, terminates episode); δ·Σ −max(0, q_i − q_mgn,i) with δ = −20 (soft attitude margin); constant η = +0.01 per step; terminal bonus κ = +10 only if r_z < 0, ‖r‖ < 5 m, ‖v‖ < 2 m/s, attitude (except yaw) < 0.2 rad, rates < 0.2 rad/s — [ar5iv 1810.08719](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.08719)
- Target velocity field: v_targ = −v_o (r̂/‖r̂‖)(1 − exp(−t_go/τ)), t_go = ‖r̂‖/‖v̂‖, with a piecewise switch at 15 m altitude (r̂ offset to a 15 m hover point above target; v̂ offset by −2 m/s above and −1 m/s below 15 m), v_o = 20 m/s, τ₁ = 100 s — [ar5iv 1810.08719](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.08719)
- Two discount rates: γ₁ for the terminal bonus, γ₂ for all other terms, "a large γ₁ and a smaller γ₂ work better" (values not given); GAE was dropped because it is "not compatible with multiple discount rates" — [arXiv HTML 1810.08719](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.08719); abstract: "using different discount rates for terminal and shaping rewards" "significantly enhances optimization performance" — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08719)
- Documented failure mode (early termination): the η term exists because without it the agent would be "incentivized to violate the attitude constraint and prematurely terminate the episode"; the shaping is "effective, but not optimal" and the authors attribute the 18% gap to GPOPS to it — [arXiv HTML 1810.08719](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.08719)
- Meta-RL (Mars) reward: r = α‖v − v_targ‖ + β‖T‖ + γ + η·(landing bonus), α = −0.01, β = −0.05, γ = +0.01 constant, η = 10; τ₁ = 20 s above 15 m, τ₂ = 100 s below; bonus requires r < 5 m, v < 2 m/s, glideslope > 79°; asteroid variant α = 1.0, τ₁ = 250 s, v_o = 0.5 m/s, r_lim = 1 m, v_lim = 0.2 m/s; observation is [v_error, altitude, t_go] only — [ar5iv 1904.09865](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.09865)
- Adaptive ZEM/ZEV (cost, not reward): burned-mass weight w_m = 0.5, terminal position and velocity error weights 1e-1, impact-point weight 5e-4, biases b_i = 100 at impact and b_f = 10 at final time; glide-slope violation ends the episode — [ar5iv 2003.02182](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2003.02182)
- Adversarial RL (2026) lander reward: r_t^L = r_t^prog / (p_t* + ε) − λ_T·‖T_t‖/T_max, progress measured along a reference velocity field with over-speed penalised; adversary reward is the sparse terminal ‖r(t_f) − r_pad‖; observed "exploit-adapt" cycle and "some loss of precision in milder conditions" after adversarial training — [arXiv HTML 2610.20252](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20252)
- G&CNet RL (Holt/Origer/Izzo): log-scaled terminal rewards with linear coupling of velocity and position scaling, Lambert-arc shaping for fuel-optimal cases (α_L = 0.1), "reward redistribution" spreading terminal reward over the trajectory, control integrated inside the step to decouple control vs action frequency — [arXiv HTML 2507.19535](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.19535)
- Izzo et al. review on reward design: "Engineering an appropriate reward function turns out to be problematic in most cases" and it is unclear "which optimality principle a successful reward corresponds to" — [arXiv HTML 2305.13078](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.13078)
- Gymnasium LunarLander shaping (toy): shaping = −100·sqrt(x²+y²) − 100·sqrt(vx²+vy²) − 100·|angle| + 10·leg1 + 10·leg2; per-step reward = shaping − prev_shaping, minus 0.30·m_power and 0.03·s_power; terminal reward is set (not added) to −100 on crash/out-of-bounds and +100 when the body is "not awake" (at rest) — [lunar_lander.py source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Farama-Foundation/Gymnasium/main/gymnasium/envs/box2d/lunar_lander.py)
- Hovering as a designed objective: SALSA-RL (Li & Maulik, arXiv Feb 2025, v5 Apr 2026) Appendix E modifies LunarLander: "The bonuses for leg contacts and successful landing are removed", "a large negative penalty of -300 is applied for crashes, going out of bounds, or touching the ground", "A survival bonus of +0.2 per timestep is introduced to reward prolonged hovering", "These modifications prevent premature convergence to local reward maxima dominated by landing or crashing" — [arXiv HTML 2502.15512](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15512)
- Anecdotal "float and never land" with DQN on LunarLander: "in some test episodes, the agent doesn't fully land; it just 'floats' over the surface, but doesn't land, and goes upwards" (forum post, 15 Jul 2025, no replies) — [DeepLearning.AI forum](https://community.deeplearning.ai/t/reinforcement-learning-deep-q-learning-lunar-lander/852516)
- Anecdotal reward hacking in a custom drone-landing env (not Gymnasium): rewarding "stability and slow movement near the platform" led to "The drone quickly learned to hover in place indefinitely, racking up rewards without ever landing"; after fixes, the agent "descended past the platform and hovered beneath it, avoiding the crash penalty" — [HyperAI aggregated story](https://hyper.ai/en/stories/746ae19278691165e0c293d5be44bae0)

### Inferences
- In LunarLander-v3 the shaping is a potential difference, so hovering accrues ≈ 0 net shaping while paying 0.30 per main-engine frame; a 1000-step hover costs up to ~300 reward, so pure hovering is already penalised — "floating" episodes are more plausibly explained by the +100 requiring the Box2D body to fall asleep (come fully to rest), which an agent that keeps firing never triggers, and by the 1000-step TimeLimit truncation arriving without any terminal penalty. (Inference from the source code; no paper states this.)
- The Gaudet constant-bonus trick (η > 0) and SALSA-RL's +0.2 survival bonus are the same lever pulled in opposite directions: a positive per-step term discourages early crash-termination but, if too large relative to the terminal reward, encourages hovering/timeout; the two-discount-rate scheme is the authors' answer to balancing these.
- Every serious design penalises control effort (‖F‖ or ‖T‖) rather than fuel mass directly, and all gate the terminal bonus on multi-constraint touchdown conditions rather than a scalar distance — this is the pattern a new lunar study should adopt if it wants comparability.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed paper was found that systematically catalogues reward-hacking behaviours in LunarLander or physics-based landers; evidence is one appendix (SALSA-RL), one forum post, one blog, and the Gaudet early-termination remark.
- Gaudet et al. never publish γ₁/γ₂ values or τ₂ for the 6-DoF paper (table misaligned in the HTML).

## Key Question 3 — Most credible way to compare a learned descent policy to an optimal / classical one (metrics, baselines)

### Takeaway
The accepted protocol in the literature is: dispersed-initial-condition Monte Carlo (≥ 1,000, typically 5,000-20,000 episodes) reporting mean / std / min / max of terminal position and velocity components, attitude and rates, glideslope, and propellant mass; a fuel baseline from an open-loop optimal solver (GPOPS / indirect PMP / lossless convexification) run from the same initial conditions, plus a closed-loop classical law (DR/DV, ZEM/ZEV or Apollo polynomial) run with the same perturbations; robustness swept over mass, thrust/Isp degradation, engine failure, sensor bias and disturbance forces; and stated onboard compute cost. "Success rate" is rarely reported by the strongest papers and should be defined explicitly.

### Cited Findings
- Gaudet 6-DoF uses 10,000-episode nominal Monte Carlo with per-component touchdown statistics (mean/std/min/max), fuel mean/std/min/max, 5,000-run divert test, 20,000-run extended-ellipse tests; baselines: 3-DoF RL, 3-DoF DR/DV (treated "as a proxy for optimal performance, since it is energy-optimal under unlimited thrust"), and GPOPS open-loop optimum (250 kg) — [ar5iv 1810.08719](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.08719); [arXiv HTML 1810.08719](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.08719)
- Gaudet notes DR/DV needed "about 20 kg for a waypoint to give DR/DV a glideslope above 1" so baselines are compared under equal constraints — [ar5iv 1810.08719](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.08719)
- Meta-RL paper compares DR/DV (given ground-truth mass) vs non-recurrent RL vs recurrent RL under identical failure/uncertainty injections, reporting norm of terminal position and velocity as μ/σ/max — [ar5iv 1904.09865](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.09865)
- Furfaro A-ZEM/ZEV compares against classical ZEM/ZEV and GPOPS on identical single nominal trajectories (mass depleted, TOF) plus 1000-trial Monte Carlo for constraint satisfaction — [ar5iv 2003.02182](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2003.02182)
- Holt/Origer/Izzo define "optimality residual" (% and absolute kg or days vs the optimal event solution) and "convergence %" under four perturbation classes (IC, zero-order-hold, orbit determination, execution), reporting position-only and full-state convergence separately — [arXiv HTML 2507.19535](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.19535)
- Wang/Chen/Li compare fuel and flight time against GPOPS-II NLP and against the indirect method on the same ICs, report solver convergence rate of the baseline itself (44/100) and mean inference time over 10,000 trials — [arXiv HTML 2404.06722v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.06722v1)
- Wang 2026 validates against an indirect-method BVP at six initial states plus eighty Monte Carlo runs and reports PMP residuals — [arXiv abs 2607.02741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02741)
- Izzo et al.: DRL sub-optimality must be "noted and quantified"; a learned controller's value is judged against the optimal-control solution and on-board compute (DRL ≈ "three orders of magnitude larger computational times" to train) — [arXiv HTML 2305.13078](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.13078)
- NASA distinguishes precision (scatter) from accuracy (offset): "precision is the measurement of how close landings are to one another, whereas accuracy is a measurement of how close they are to their intended target" — [NASA Safe and Precise Landing paper](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/safe-and-precise-landing-at-lunar-sites.pdf)
- Monte Carlo dispersion is conventionally drawn as nested 68.27 / 95.45 / 99.73% ellipses (TeamIndus GNC paper, 5,000 runs; from search-result summary) — [arXiv 1907.10955](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.10955)
- Toy-env convention: LunarLander "solved" = reward_threshold 200 in registration and "An episode is considered a solution if it scores at least 200 points" — [envs/__init__.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Farama-Foundation/Gymnasium/main/gymnasium/envs/__init__.py); [Gymnasium docs](https://gymnasium.farama.org/environments/box2d/lunar_lander/); HF leaderboard scores "mean_reward - std of reward" ≥ 200 over 10 deterministic eval episodes — [HF Deep RL Unit 1](https://huggingface.co/learn/deep-rl-course/unit1/hands-on)

### Inferences
- A defensible comparison for a new lunar study would be: (1) open-loop fuel optimum per sampled IC (indirect PMP or convex solver) → "optimality residual %"; (2) a closed-loop classical law with equal constraints (ZEM/ZEV or Apollo quadratic) under the same perturbations → robustness delta; (3) report both per-axis touchdown stats and a CEP / 99% ellipse (which NASA-style accuracy vs precision language maps onto); (4) explicit success criteria (e.g., ‖r‖ < 5 m, ‖v‖ < 2 m/s, attitude < 0.2 rad as in Gaudet) so success % is reproducible.
- Because the strongest papers omit success %, a new work that reports success % plus the Gaudet-style component tables would be directly comparable to all of them.

### Gaps
- No community benchmark or shared dataset for learned landing guidance was found (the Izzo review explicitly proposes drone racing as the hardware gym, not a lander).
- Classical baseline primary texts (Klumpp 1974; Guo-Hawkins-Wie 2013; Açıkmeşe-Ploen 2007) could not be fetched (403); their numerical performance figures are not reported here.

## Key Question 4 — Has anyone connected landing-controller performance (dispersion, fuel) to downstream surface operations or logistics?

### Takeaway
No. NASA architecture documents state qualitatively that precision landing shortens cargo transfer distance/time and that landing-site-to-point-of-use relocation (driven by shadow, plume ejecta and aggregation) is "one of the largest drivers of mobility needs", and give distance thresholds (>50 m shadow, >1,000 m ejecta, up to 5,000 m aggregation), but no source quantitatively couples a controller's dispersion statistics or propellant margin to traverse time, rover energy, cargo mass or mission cost. The closest is a 2026 npj Space Exploration paper asking for PSI simulations across the 100 m error margin. This is a genuine gap.

### Cited Findings
- NASA Moon-to-Mars white paper "Lunar Logistics, Mobility, and Cargo" (Feb 2025, S. Bhakta, ESDMD-SAO): "One of the largest drivers of mobility needs on the lunar surface is moving cargo from its landing site to its point of use"; relocation factors: "Separation from lander shadowing (tens of meters)", "Lander blast ejecta constraints (>1,000 m)", aggregation in habitation zones; slide 7: "Min relocation distance to avoid lander shadows >50m", "Min relocation distance to avoid lander blast ejecta >1,000m", "Relocation from potential lander sites to optimal/aggregate surface locations up to 5,000m"; "Integrated architecture operations will necessitate non-trivial relocation and aggregation range demand for cargo and assets"; aggregate demand "on the order of 2,000 to 10,000 kg per year" — [NASA PDF](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/iparch10-wp-logistics-mobility-cargo.pdf)
- Same paper: "cargo offloading and manipulation will need to be conducted before the crew arrives at each landing location (point of origin) and then again at local lunar exploration and habitation sites (point of use). These exploration and habitation sites will likely be located away from each landing location." — [NASA PDF](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/iparch10-wp-logistics-mobility-cargo.pdf)
- NASA "Safe and Precise Landing at Lunar Sites" (2023 Architecture Concept Review white paper): "Precision landing can enable aggregation of surface assets in closer proximity, improving efficiency and risk posture. Whether using robotics or crew members to transfer logistics items, a reduction in distance means a reduction in transfer time and risk. As an example, landing a logistics module closer to a habitat module would ease the transfer of items"; "knowing the final, accurate location of the landed asset will enable planning for landing additional assets"; "While precision landing and close asset aggregation reduce transit times, surface assets will have to mitigate against PSI ejecta, which may require asset hardening"; PL&HA "reduces post-landing travel distances between surface assets" — [NASA PDF](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/safe-and-precise-landing-at-lunar-sites.pdf)
- Anderson, Saxena, Metzger, "Mitigating plume surface interactions using lunar craters", npj Space Exploration 2:28 (12 Jun 2026): "the Artemis program has a stated landing precision requirement of 100 m"; "it is currently unclear what an effect an off-center, but within error margin landing would have" on a crater's ejecta "umbrella zone"; recommends "multiple PSI simulations within the 100 m margin of error should be performed"; cites proposed "base-landing zone separation of 200 m", landing zones "up to 2 or 3 km away from sensitive equipment", prepared pads "with diameters from 50 m to 100 m", and Apollo "3σ landing area of approximately 2000 m across" — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44453-026-00045-w)
- Artemis III requirement "Land within 100 m of a target" and HLS "Human Landing Accuracy Requirements 100 m x 100 m" (from search-result summaries of NASA presentations; not fetched) — [NTRS 20230005904](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20230005904/downloads/PlatteCity_Artemis.pdf); [NTRS 20240009596](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240009596/downloads/ArtemisHLSUpdate_2024.pdf); ESA Moonlight "Landing accuracies required 50 to 100 metres" (search summary) — [ESA slide deck](https://bsgn.esa.int/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/07-Moonlight_LCNS_S4I_2024_Javier-Ventura-Traveset.pdf)
- Apollo 17 traverse planning treated dispersion as a replanning constraint: "Dispersions could still result in landing elsewhere in the 1.5 km radius circle and the mission support elements must be prepared to replan the traverses from any landing point" (search-result summary of the ALSJ document) — [NASA ALSJ](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/alsj/a17/A17TraversePlanningData.pdf)
- Watkins et al. (2021 Decadal white paper on PSI) recommends investing in "studies of long-term infrastructure architectures and mitigation techniques", i.e., the lander-to-infrastructure interaction is recognised as open — [arXiv 2102.12312](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.12312)
- None of the RL/learned-guidance papers above (Gaudet, Furfaro, Scorsoglio, Ciabatti, Leiva-Vélez, Izzo, Holt, Wang) mention surface operations, traverse, cargo or logistics; their downstream metrics stop at touchdown state and propellant — [ar5iv 1810.08719](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.08719); [arXiv HTML 2610.20252](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20252); [arXiv HTML 2305.13078](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.13078)

### Inferences
- NASA treats landing precision as a binary enabler (≤ 50-100 m) and treats relocation distance as set by shadow/ejecta/aggregation (50 m - 5 km), so under current architecture assumptions a controller's residual dispersion (1-100 m) is small relative to the logistics distances — but nobody has tested that assumption by propagating a dispersion distribution into a traverse-time / energy / cargo-throughput model, nor has anyone valued the controller's saved propellant as delivered-payload mass. A model that maps controller dispersion CEP and fuel residual → expected offload traverse distance, rover energy/time, PSI-exposure of neighbours, and payload delivered would be new.
- The 2026 npj paper's request for off-centre-within-margin PSI analysis is the first place where the *shape* of the dispersion (not just its 100 m bound) matters to surface infrastructure; that is a natural coupling point for a learned-controller study.

### Gaps
- No source quantifying traverse cost (time, energy, risk) as a function of landing dispersion; no source converting lander fuel residual into payload or logistics value; no source on controller-level trade (precision vs propellant) for surface operations. Searches on these phrasings returned only Apollo-era traverse planning and PSI papers.

## Key Question 5 — Practical: exact LunarLander-v3 definition; SB3 PPO settings that solve it; exporting a small MLP policy for JavaScript

### Takeaway
LunarLander-v3 (Gymnasium ≥ 1.0.0a2, May 2024) is a Box2D world at 50 FPS with SCALE 30, 600x400 viewport, discrete 4 actions (or continuous 2-D), 8-D observation (normalised x, y, vx, vy, angle, angular rate, two leg flags), potential-difference shaping (−100·distance − 100·speed − 100·|angle| + 10/leg), fuel costs 0.30 / 0.03 per frame, ±100 terminal, 1000-step limit, reward_threshold 200. RL-Zoo PPO (16 envs, n_steps 1024, batch 64, 4 epochs, γ 0.999, λ 0.98, ent_coef 0.01, 1e6 steps) scores 242 ± 32 (discrete) / 271 ± 32 (continuous); the HF course gets 200.20 ± 20.80 in "~20 min" on a Colab GPU. The default policy is two 64-unit tanh layers for actor and critic each; export via torch.onnx (opset 17) and onnxruntime-web, or dump state_dict weights and reimplement the 8→64→64→4 tanh MLP + argmax in JS.

- Deterministic action: `get_actions(deterministic=True)` returns `mode()`; Categorical `mode()` = `th.argmax(self.distribution.probs, dim=1)`; DiagGaussian `mode()` = `self.distribution.mean` — [distributions.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DLR-RM/stable-baselines3/master/stable_baselines3/common/distributions.py)
- ONNX export: wrap `model.policy` in `OnnxableSB3Policy` whose forward is `return self.policy(observation, deterministic=True)` (PPO returns `actions, values, log_prob`), then `th.onnx.export(onnx_policy, dummy_input, "my_ppo_model.onnx", opset_version=17, input_names=["input"])`; requires "PyTorch 2.0+ and ONNX Opset 14+"; export "doesn't include the post-processing step" (clipping / unscaling for continuous actions); for discrete actions you must convert logits to an action yourself; observation preprocessing (e.g., VecNormalize stats) must be reproduced — [SB3 export guide](https://stable-baselines3.readthedocs.io/en/master/guide/export.html)
- Browser inference: SB3 docs link an ONNX Runtime Web example (CarDodgingGym) that exports normalization stats to JSON and runs `ort.InferenceSession.create('my_sac_actor.onnx')` with a `Float32Array` input; TensorFlow.js route requires the unmaintained tfjs-converter with `opset_version=14` and pinned versions; TorchScript (`th.jit.trace` → `freeze` → `optimize_for_inference` → `save`) for C++ — [SB3 export guide](https://stable-baselines3.readthedocs.io/en/master/guide/export.html)
- Manual export: weights via `model.policy.state_dict()` (recommended) or `model.get_parameters()` (includes optimizer state); "check policies.py in each algorithm's folder" for architecture — [SB3 export guide](https://stable-baselines3.readthedocs.io/en/master/guide/export.html)

### Inferences
- With defaults, the discrete LunarLander actor is 8→64 (tanh)→64 (tanh)→4 logits: 8·64+64 + 64·64+64 + 64·4+4 = 576 + 4160 + 260 = 4,996 parameters, trivially re-implementable in ~30 lines of JavaScript from a JSON dump of `mlp_extractor.policy_net.{0,2}.{weight,bias}` and `action_net.{weight,bias}`, with argmax for deterministic play; the critic is not needed at inference.
- Observation scale factors implied by the source (for re-implementing the env or checking a JS port): x normalised by 10 m, y by 6.667 m above the helipad+leg offset, vx multiplied by 0.2, vy by 0.1333, angular rate by 0.4; 1000 steps = 20 s of simulated time at 50 FPS.
- The RL-Zoo/HF recipe (γ = 0.999, λ = 0.98, 16 envs, 1e6 steps, ent_coef 0.01) is the de-facto "solves it" setting; the long horizon γ = 0.999 is consistent with the sparse ±100 terminal reward and 1000-step cap.
