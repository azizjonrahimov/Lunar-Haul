# Lunar powered descent and landing: hard numbers for a south-pole cargo-lander terminal-descent simulation

Compiled 2026-10-15. Each figure carries its source URL and, where known, the date of the figure. "Measured" = flight data or official post-flight statement; "Spec" = published design value; "Est." = third-party or derived estimate. Unless stated, lunar surface gravity is taken as 1.62 m/s^2 (NASA's HLS touchdown-dynamics paper uses 1.63 m/s^2). Items marked "(search excerpt; page not fetched)" were reported in search-engine summaries of pages that returned HTTP 429/403 during this session; treat them as needing confirmation.

## Vehicle compilation: mass, payload, propulsion, sensors, outcomes (Blue Moon, Starship HLS, Nova-C/D, Blue Ghost, Griffin, ispace, SLIM, Chang'e)

### Takeaway
As of October 2026 only five of the target vehicles have actually flown to the lunar surface (Nova-C twice, Blue Ghost once, SLIM, ispace twice, Chang'e-4/6); none of the Artemis-class cargo landers (Blue Moon MK1, Starship HLS, Griffin, Nova-D) has landed. Published hard numbers are good for mass/payload/thrust, patchy for throttle range and Isp, and essentially absent for touchdown-velocity limits of the commercial landers.

- IM-2 (6 Mar 2025, Mons Mouton, 84.79 deg S, 29.20 deg E): on its side inside a ~20 m-wide crater at -173 deg C; last contact 7 Mar 2025 06:15 UTC (<13 h); IM said the altimeter "failed during landing"; dust plume obscured laser/rangefinder readings in terminal descent; struck a plateau, tipped, skidded, settled in crater — [Wikipedia: IM-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IM-2)

**Firefly Blue Ghost (Mission 1 landed 2 Mar 2025)**
- Landed 2 Mar 2025 08:45 UT at 18.562 N, 61.810 E, Mare Crisium, ~4,000 m from the center of Mons Latreille — [NASA NSSDCA](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/nmc/spacecraft/display.action?id=BLUEGHOST)
- Firefly's own retrospective (LPSC 2026): "a light-weight structure supporting a metric ton of propellant"; D'Souza guidance; main engine + eight Spectre thrusters for braking, then "the main engine disengaged, and the remainder of descent was performed with Spectre thrusters only"; "two hazard avoidance maneuvers were autonomously commanded by the vision navigation system"; contact sensed by 3 of 4 footpad sensors; "LRO imagery verified successful touchdown 58 meters from the targeted landing ellipse"; ~60 anomalies dispositioned — [Tackett et al., LPSC 2026 #1958](https://www.hou.usra.edu/meetings/lpsc2026/pdf/1958.pdf)
- Target 18.560 N, 61.807 E; LRO-located rest point 18.5623 N, 61.8103 E; "about 118 meters" from target; landing ellipse circular, 100 m diameter; ten NASA payloads "nearly 100 kilograms" — [Moon Monday #216](https://jatan.space/moon-monday-issue-216/). **Astrobotic (now Voyager Lunar Systems) Griffin**
- Griffin polar configuration: 625 kg payload to surface, up to 14 days surface ops; four landing legs; propulsion: five 700 lbf (3,114 N) pulsed main engines + twelve 25 lbf (111 N) ACS engines, pressure-fed hypergolic M20 (hydrazine derivative) / MON-3; engines "pulsed for throttling"; GNC with Doppler LiDAR for range/range-rate in landing — [Astrobotic Payload User's Guide, Jan 2022](https://www.astrobotic.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/PUGLanders_011222.pdf)

**ispace HAKUTO-R (M1 2023, M2 Resilience 2025)**
- M1 descent began at ~100 km altitude 00:40 JST 26 Apr 2023; passing over "a large cliff approximately 3 kms in elevation" (a crater rim) produced a larger-than-expected altimeter discrepancy; a software filter "designed to reject altitude measurements with a large gap" discarded the data; the lander "estimated its own altitude to be zero" while actually at ~5 km; it reached its <1 m/s target speed at ~5 km, continued slowly until "the propulsion system ran out of fuel", then free-fell — [ispace, 26 May 2023](https://www.ispace-inc.com/2023/05/26/ispace-announces-results-of-the-hakuto-r-mission-1-lunar-landing/)
- M1 masses per JAXA's comparison table: launch ~1,000 kg, landing ~340 kg (excl. fuel) — [JAXA SLIM project review, 26 Dec 2024](https://www.isas.jaxa.jp/en/topics/files/SLIM-press-briefing-20241226.pdf)
- M2 Resilience (5/6 Jun 2025): lost telemetry "193 m above the lunar surface while traveling at 42 m/s"; impact ">50 km/h"; laser rangefinder gave delayed/inaccurate distance readings (candidate causes: lower surface reflectivity, in-flight degradation, high descent speed); root cause stated as flying without a redundant distance sensor; LiDAR to be added; extra cost ~Y1.5B ($10.3M) — [Payload, Jun 2025](https://payloadspace.com/ispace-blames-faulty-hardware-for-failed-lunar-landing/); impact speed "42 meters a second" — [Universe Today](https://www.universetoday.com/articles/hakuto-r-mission-2s-crash-was-caused-by-its-laser-range-finder)
- M2 additional quantities (search excerpt; SpaceNews page returned 429): LRF designed to provide altitude from >=3 km but first measurement came below 900 m; lander was at 66 m/s when it should have been at 44 m/s; LRO-located impact ~282 m south and ~236 m east of target with a ~16 m crater; four candidate causes (reflectivity, laser angle, reduced power, radiation/high-speed degradation); ~$11M of fixes — [SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/laser-rangefinder-problems-blamed-for-second-ispace-lunar-lander-crash/)

**JAXA SLIM (landed 20 Jan 2024)**
- Mass approx. 200 kg dry, approx. 715 kg at launch; H 2.4 m x L 1.7 m x W 2.7 m; two main engines (ceramic, variable thrust); 12 x 22 N thrusters; landing radar; two navigation cameras; landing 2024/1/20 — [JAXA SLIM project review, 26 Dec 2024](https://www.isas.jaxa.jp/en/topics/files/SLIM-press-briefing-20241226.pdf)
- Main engines "around 500 newtons of thrust" each (Est./press) — [NASASpaceflight, Jan 2024](https://www.nasaspaceflight.com/2024/01/slim-landing/) (search excerpt)
- Accuracy: "landing accuracy at around 10m or better" evaluated from images at ~50 m altitude ("beyond this point, obstacle avoidance takes precedence"); horizontal errors of 3.4 m and 10.2 m at the two obstacle-detection images (HV2 #1, #2); final soft landing "about 60m east" (elsewhere 55 m) of target after a propulsion fault caused eastward drift; LRO-estimated site 13.3160 S, 25.2510 E — [JAXA SLIM review 2024](https://www.isas.jaxa.jp/en/topics/files/SLIM-press-briefing-20241226.pdf); JAXA Jan 2024: ~55 m from target, ~10 m or less (possibly 3-4 m) at the 50 m checkpoint, "pinpoint landing within an accuracy of 100m ... has been achieved" — [JAXA press release, 25 Jan 2024](https://global.jaxa.jp/press/2024/01/20240125-1_e.html)

### Inferences
- Hover thrust at 1.62 m/s^2 for the simulation's 3-15 t landed-mass range is 4.9-24.3 kN. A single BE-7 at its 8.9 kN deep-throttle floor (Blue Origin figure) cannot hover a vehicle lighter than ~5.5 t at T/W = 1, so MK1's landed mass (not published; 21.35 t wet, 3 t payload) must be well above that or the engine must pulse. The Apollo DPS floor (4.67 kN) vs a ~5 t LM gave a 10-60% throttle band spanning hover; Chang'e's 1.5-7.5 kN band vs ~1.2 t landed (hover ~1.9 kN, 26% of max) is similarly centered.
- Small landers that flew in 2024-25 all terminated with thrust-to-weight close to 1 on small thrusters: Blue Ghost's 8 x 200 N vs roughly 0.6 t landed (hover ~1.0 kN, ~60% of Spectre total), SLIM's 2 x ~500 N vs ~0.21 t (hover ~0.34 kN), Nova-C's 3.1 kN vs ~0.8-0.9 t (hover ~1.4 kN, ~45%).
- Decomposing the NDL-reconstructed IM-1 contact state (7.8 m/s at -44.4 deg) gives ~5.5 m/s vertical and ~5.6 m/s horizontal, roughly 2x and 6x the values IM announced (2.7 and 0.9 m/s). Either the NDL "first contact" includes a pre-touchdown terrain strike or IM's figures were preliminary; a 7.8 m/s / 45 deg arrival onto a 12 deg slope is fully consistent with a broken leg and tip-over.
- Every tip-over or crash since 2023 traces to the altitude/range sensing chain (IM-1 altimeter never armed; IM-2 altimeter noise plus crater-matching under polar lighting; ispace M1 software rejecting valid altimeter data over a 3 km rim; ispace M2 LRF late by >2 km of altitude), not to guidance or propulsion. SLIM's propulsion fault occurred below 50 m and still produced a survivable landing.

### Gaps
- No published dry mass, throttle floor, or Isp confirmed by Blue Origin for Blue Moon MK1; Blue Origin's own pages returned HTTP 429 in this session. MK1 Pathfinder has not launched (NET Q1 2027), so no measured descent data exist.
- Starship HLS dry mass, landing-engine thrust, landing-leg geometry, and touchdown limits are unpublished; cargo-Starship numbers beyond the 100 t headline and NASA's "12-15 t" cargo-variant statement were not found.
- Nova-C VR900 throttle range and Isp not found in any primary source; the 3,100 N vs 4,900 lbf thrust conflict is unresolved. Nova-D mass, dimensions and engine count are unconfirmed (IM's page redirect-looped).
- Blue Ghost's touchdown-velocity limits, leg geometry, slope tolerance and official landing-dispersion figure (beyond "58 m from the targeted ellipse") were not published; the 1,700 -> 40 m/s braking numbers come from a search excerpt.
- Griffin dry/wet mass and slope tolerance are not in the 2022 PUG; the 6,360 kg TLI figure is unverified.
- ispace M2 numeric details (3 km / 900 m / 66 vs 44 m/s / 282 m S, 236 m E / 16 m crater) could not be verified against ispace's own release.
- Chang'e-4 landing-dispersion relative to its planned target, touchdown velocity, and leg geometry were not found; Chang'e-6 profile is qualitative only in English sources.
- SLIM's touchdown lateral velocity: one search excerpt stated "about nine meters per second to the east", which is implausible for a surviving lander and probably a mis-transcription of a distance; not verified.

## Key question 1: Realistic terminal-descent state space (altitude 0-2 km, velocities, tilt) and control space (thrust, gimbal/RCS) for a 3-15 t lander at 1.62 m/s^2

### Takeaway
Flown and reference profiles agree on a three-phase shape: at ~2 km the vehicle is still moving 50-300 m/s with a near-horizontal or ~45 deg velocity vector, it is brought to a hover or near-hover (0-30 m/s horizontal, 1-15 m/s vertical) somewhere between 500 m and 50 m with hazard detection performed there, and the last 30-150 m is a vertical descent at 0.9-1.5 m/s with horizontal velocity nulled. Control authority is a throttleable main engine with a 10-60% (Apollo), 20-100% (Chang'e, BE-7 per Wikipedia) band, gimbal of order +/-6 deg, and RCS/vernier thrusters of 4-250 N on small landers.

### Cited Findings
- Apollo reference (crewed, ~5 t at landing): PDI at ~50,000 ft (15.2 km) and ~5,560 fps (1,695 m/s); braking phase at fixed max throttle, DPS throttled only in the final 2 minutes of braking; approach phase from 7,000 ft (2.1 km) to 500 ft (152 m) lasts ~1 min 40 s with range decreasing from ~4.5 nmi to 2,000 ft; at low gate (500 ft) 60 fps (18.3 m/s) forward, 16 fps (4.9 m/s) down, attitude ~16 deg off vertical; automatic vertical descent begins at ~150 ft (46 m) at 3 fps (0.9 m/s); landing phase automatic guidance needs 104 s — [Bennett, NASA TM X-58040](https://www.lpi.usra.edu/lunar/documents/Bennett_NASA-TM-X-58040.pdf). A 1968 planning note placed high gate at ~9,000 ft and low gate at ~500 ft, with a slow vertical descent at ~3 fps from ~65 ft — [MSC Internal Note 68-FM-78](https://www.ibiblio.org/apollo/Documents/19740072761.pdf) (search excerpt)
- NASA government-reference Artemis lander (storable-propellant, 3 main engines on the descent element): deorbit from 100 km circular to 100 x 15.24 km; braking burns; approach phase nulls horizontal velocity over the site; "Terminal descent begins at an altitude of 200 m, and the vehicle velocity is reduced further such that the main engines cut off an altitude of 1 m and a velocity of 1 m/s"; pitch/yaw via main-engine TVC, roll via RCS — [Lugo et al., NASA LaRC 2020](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20205003002/downloads/nav.pdf)
- SPLICE/ALHAT human-scale (31 t in LLO): PDI at ~15 km and ~1,700 m/s; TRN from PDI to 5 km; velocimeter from 2 km (NDL can provide velocimetry at 7 km slant range at the Moon); pitch-up 120 s before landing, then hazard detection during approach; "Terminal Descent, is nominally a vertical descent to the landing site from 30 m altitude to touchdown, at a velocity of 1 m/s downward and near zero horizontal velocity" — [SPLICE PL&HA requirements, NASA 2020](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20200002415/downloads/20200002415.pdf)
- SPLICE robotic reference (2,000 kg, 300 kg payload, 5,000 N motor): braking targets a 500 m slant range at 60 deg line of sight, 250 m downrange, ~430 m altitude, with -15 m/s vertical and 30 m/s horizontal velocity and -3.5 m/s^2 horizontal acceleration; approach phase to 50 m altitude directly over the site at -1.1 m/s vertical, 0 horizontal; vertical phase from 50 m to 2 m with zero velocity/acceleration targets — [SPLICE PL&HA requirements](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20200002415/downloads/20200002415.pdf)
- SPLICE touchdown ground rules (derived "from past human lunar missions"): vertical <2.0 m/s and horizontal <1 m/s (3 sigma); attitude within +/-6 deg (3 sigma); attitude rates within 2 deg/s (3 sigma) in all axes; delivery within 50 m radius (3 sigma) — [SPLICE PL&HA requirements](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20200002415/downloads/20200002415.pdf)
- Chang'e-4 (1.2 t) measured: near-vertical at 5.6 km and 85 m/s; ~51 m/s at 1.5 km; zero velocity and hover at 99 m for ~13 s; 12 m lateral divert; 1.5 m/s slow descent from below 99 m to 30 m — [Nat. Commun. 2019 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6760200/)
- IM-1 (2 t wet) measured: 283.6 m/s at 1,934 m AGL; 7.8 m/s at first ground contact with -44 deg flight-path angle and body axis ~4.6 deg from local vertical (NED pitch 85.43 deg) — [NASA NTRS 2025](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20250007036/downloads/aasimndl.pdf)
- Blue Ghost (1.5 t wet): main engine cut-off at ~40 m/s then Spectre-only descent to ~1 m/s at touchdown; two autonomous vision-based diverts — [NSF](https://www.nasaspaceflight.com/2025/03/blue-ghost-im-2-landings/) (search excerpt); [LPSC 2026 #1958](https://www.hou.usra.edu/meetings/lpsc2026/pdf/1958.pdf)
- Griffin/Peregrine descent: unpowered coast after braking, powered descent with continuous main-engine firing guided by OPAL TRN + Doppler LiDAR, terminal descent nulls horizontal velocity, then constant vertical velocity from 30 m — [Astrobotic PUG 2022](https://www.astrobotic.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/PUGLanders_011222.pdf)
- Starship HLS: switches to mid-body landing engines within ~100 m of the surface — [Wikipedia: Starship HLS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_HLS)
- Control actuators seen on flown vehicles: Nova-C gimbaled VR900 + 4.45 N helium RCS — [Wikipedia: Nova-C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intuitive_Machines_Nova-C); SLIM 2 variable-thrust main engines + 12 x 22 N — [JAXA 2024](https://www.isas.jaxa.jp/en/topics/files/SLIM-press-briefing-20241226.pdf); Blue Ghost 1 x >1,000 N + 8 x 200 N — [Firefly](https://fireflyspace.com/missions/blue-ghost-mission-1/); Griffin 5 x 3,114 N pulsed + 12 x 111 N ACS — [Astrobotic PUG](https://www.astrobotic.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/PUGLanders_011222.pdf); Chang'e 1.5-7.5 kN throttleable (7.5 N resolution) — [Spaceflight101](https://spaceflight101.com/change/?p=101); Apollo DPS 4.67-30.4 kN plus FTP, +/-6 deg gimbal — [Bennett](https://www.lpi.usra.edu/lunar/documents/Bennett_NASA-TM-X-58040.pdf)
- NDL sensor envelope (needed to bound where velocity/range feedback is available): three fixed beams at 20 Hz; LOS velocity resolution 0.2 cm/s, range resolution 25 cm; each beam >=4,000 m range (design 5 km to 2 m); LOS velocity up to 218 m/s — [Gragossian et al., AIAA 2020](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20200003107/downloads/20200003107.pdf); [NASA NTRS 2025](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20250007036/downloads/aasimndl.pdf)
- Hazard Detection Lidar envelope: slant range to target 400-600 m, 15 deg off nadir, vertical velocity 10-40 m/s; DEM 100 m diameter at 5 cm/pixel, 5 cm (3 sigma) elevation error, generated in 2 s, transferred in 1 s; must resolve 20 cm hazards and ~5 deg slopes — [Restrepo et al., NASA 2019](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20190034132/downloads/20190034132.pdf)
- HDS example lander parameters used by NASA in safety-map studies: leg pad radius 20 cm, vehicle radius 3 m, slope tolerance 20 deg, roughness tolerance 30 cm, touchdown dispersion 1 sigma = 3 m — [Restrepo et al. 2019](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20190034132/downloads/20190034132.pdf)
- Sensor min/max altitude envelopes assumed in SPLICE navigation trades: altimeter 10 m-50 km (high quality) to 100 m-10 km (low); velocimeter 30 m-10 km (high) to 100 m-3 km (low); TRN 30 m-20 km (high) to 100 m-10 km (low); velocimeter noise 1.7-10 cm/s — [SPLICE PL&HA requirements](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20200002415/downloads/20200002415.pdf)

### Inferences
- A defensible terminal-descent state space for the simulation (altitude 0-2 km): at 2 km, speed 50-300 m/s with flight-path angle between -30 and -90 deg (Chang'e-4 vertical at 85 m/s; IM-1 284 m/s oblique; Apollo high gate ~2.1 km with ~100+ m/s mostly horizontal); between 500 and 50 m, 0-30 m/s horizontal and 1-15 m/s vertical (SPLICE and Apollo low-gate targets); below 30-50 m, 0.9-1.5 m/s vertical and <1 m/s horizontal; body tilt from ~16 deg off vertical at low gate (Apollo) to <6 deg at touchdown (SPLICE), with angular rates <2 deg/s.
- Control space: main-engine thrust band of 20-100% of max (floor ~0.6-0.8 of hover thrust, ceiling ~1.5-3x hover), gimbal +/-6 deg, RCS torque authority from clusters of 100-250 N thrusters on a 3-15 t vehicle (Griffin and Chang'e/Tianwen class), or pulsed main engines if deep throttle is unavailable (Griffin). For a 15 t lander hover is ~24 kN, so a single BE-7 (8.9-44 kN) or 3 BE-7s (MK2) bracket hover comfortably; for 3 t hover is ~4.9 kN, which is below the BE-7 floor.
- Hazard detection should be scheduled where the HDL envelope holds (400-600 m slant range, 10-40 m/s descent rate), i.e. roughly the top of the approach phase; the lidar DEM covers a 100 m-diameter circle, so diverts beyond ~50 m require a second scan or orbital-map hazard knowledge.

### Gaps
- No commercial lander has published its guidance targets (gate altitudes/velocities) or actuator limits; Blue Ghost and Nova-C numbers above are post-hoc reconstructions or press figures.
- Gimbal ranges for BE-7, VR900, Spectre or Chang'e engines were not found.
- No source gives IM-2's or Chang'e-6's altitude-velocity profile.

## Key question 2: Landing precision achievable today vs. required for Artemis base landing pads

### Takeaway
Demonstrated accuracy in 2024-25 is 10 m (SLIM, before obstacle avoidance), 55-60 m (SLIM final), ~58 m from the ellipse / ~118 m from target (Blue Ghost), 250->400 m (IM-2) and 1.5 km (IM-1); NASA's crewed requirement is 100 m (3 sigma), SPLICE's multi-mission ground rule is 50 m (3 sigma), and pad-construction studies assume the pad diameter must match that dispersion (200 m requirement being driven down to 100 m).

### Cited Findings
- HLS-R-0322: "The HLS shall be capable of landing within 100m (3-sigma) of target landing site in order to optimize EVA resources, scientific objectives, fuel consumption, vehicle navigation capabilities and crew schedule" (initial 2024-mission requirement set) — [NASA HLS strategy, 2020](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20200001606/downloads/20200001606.pdf); same 100 m (3 sigma) in the uncrewed demo success criterion between 84 and 90 deg S — [HLS SOW](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/80MSFC20C0034-P00010_Att_J-01_SOW_RIF_TAGGED.pdf)
- NASA Moon-to-Mars white paper (2023): "a precision landing could be qualified as a requirement to safely touch down within 50 to 100 meters of a given target"; worked example shrinking the ellipse radius from 100 m to 50 m to clear a large crater; accuracy (offset from truth) vs precision (dispersion) distinguished — [Safe and Precise Landing at Lunar Sites, NASA 2023](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/safe-and-precise-landing-at-lunar-sites.pdf)
- SPLICE ground rule: all vehicles "must be delivered to within 50 m radius (3s) of a specified landing target" because "future missions may require delivery of multiple landers to the same landing zone" — [SPLICE PL&HA requirements](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20200002415/downloads/20200002415.pdf)
- SLIM: ~10 m or better (3.4 m and 10.2 m at the two 50 m-altitude images); final 55-60 m east after engine fault; JAXA counts the 100 m objective achieved — [JAXA 2024 review](https://www.isas.jaxa.jp/en/topics/files/SLIM-press-briefing-20241226.pdf); [JAXA Jan 2024](https://global.jaxa.jp/press/2024/01/20240125-1_e.html)
- Blue Ghost M1: 58 m from the targeted ellipse (Firefly) — [LPSC 2026 #1958](https://www.hou.usra.edu/meetings/lpsc2026/pdf/1958.pdf); ~118 m from target center against a 100 m-diameter ellipse (independent LRO-based estimate) — [Moon Monday #216](https://jatan.space/moon-monday-issue-216/)
- IM-1: ~1.5 km from intended site — [Wikipedia: Nova-C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intuitive_Machines_Nova-C); IM-2: ~250 m (IM) vs >400 m (NASA) — [Space.com](https://www.space.com/astronomy/moon/heres-why-the-private-athena-lunar-lander-toppled-over-on-the-moon) (search excerpt)
- ispace M2 impact ~282 m S / ~236 m E of target — [SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/laser-rangefinder-problems-blamed-for-second-ispace-lunar-lander-crash/) (search excerpt)
- Griffin design: 100 m x 100 m ellipse with OPAL TRN, down from 24 km x 6 km — [Astrobotic PUG 2022](https://www.astrobotic.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/PUGLanders_011222.pdf); Blue Moon MK1 target "within 100 m" — [Wikipedia: Pathfinder Mission 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Moon_Pathfinder_Mission_1)
- Navigation needed to get there: NASA 6-DOF simulation of the Artemis reference lander shows a perfect-IMU-only solution drifting >3,000 km by touchdown, while adding DSN, star tracker and TRN brings final navigation position error below 4 m — [Lugo et al. 2020](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20205003002/downloads/nav.pdf); SPLICE LinCov found the 50 m / velocity requirements met by a low-quality velocimeter plus medium-quality TRN with a DSN update, and that "precision landing requirements cannot be satisfied without relative sensors" — [SPLICE PL&HA requirements](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20200002415/downloads/20200002415.pdf)
- Pad sizing vs precision: "The current Artemis Program landing pad diameter requirement is 200m, but discussions with the NASA Entry, Descent and Landing Principal Technologist have yielded an effort to reduce it to 100m"; a 200 m pad "is not feasible with a single Starship payload, however 100m or less could be accommodated" (RTV-bound regolith pads, 100-200 t Starship payload assumed) — [Gelino et al., ASCE Earth & Space 2022 (NTRS)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220000334/downloads/In%20situ%20Lunar%20Launch%20and%20Landing%20Pad%20Construction%20with%20Regolith%20and%20Thermoset%20Polymers%20ASCE%20Earth%20and%20Space%202022%20Paper%20SUBMITTED.pdf)
- LSIC notional pad requirement: "The LLP shall support landing within 100m of a given point" — [Mueller, LSIC E&C Focus Group, 30 Oct 2020](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20205009125/downloads/Lunar%20Landing%20&%20Launch%20Pads%20v1.pdf)
- Artemis III site criteria: NASA "accessible" terrain restricted to slopes <5 deg, with sunlight and direct-to-Earth visibility; a site test of slope <=5 deg over >=25% of a 2 km exploration zone — [LPSC 2024 #1695](https://www.hou.usra.edu/meetings/lpsc2024/pdf/1695.pdf); HLS parameters include "maintaining a minimum distance of 100 m from terrain with slopes steeper than 5 deg" — [LPSC 2025 #1149](https://www.hou.usra.edu/meetings/lpsc2025/pdf/1149.pdf); a JGR Planets study uses "at least 100 m away from slopes steeper than 8 deg" for 100 m landing accuracy — [Wueller et al., JGR Planets 2026](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2025JE009434); candidate regions ~15 km^2 each; key considerations include "gentle slope", continuous sunlight, Earth view, "landing accuracy", "surface data resolution" — [Creech, IAC 2025](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20250008728/downloads/25%2008%2026%20IAC_Creech%20BP-1.pdf); down-selection from 13 to 9 regions (Haworth, Malapert Massif, Mons Mouton, Nobile Rim 1/2, de Gerlache Rim 2, Slater Plain ...) — [LPSC 2026 #1901](https://www.hou.usra.edu/meetings/lpsc2026/pdf/1901.pdf) (search excerpts)

### Inferences
- The 100 m buffer-from-steep-slope rule and the 100 m (3 sigma) HLS accuracy are the same number, which is why NASA site studies treat 100 m as the working dispersion radius; a 50 m (3 sigma) capability, as SPLICE assumes and as SLIM demonstrated pre-divert, halves the required pad area and lets the ellipse clear typical 50-100 m craters.
- Only SLIM has demonstrated <100 m accuracy on purpose; Blue Ghost is at the 100 m boundary; both Nova-C missions and both ispace missions exceeded 250 m. For a base-pad simulation, 100 m (3 sigma) is the realistic near-term requirement and 50 m the stretch goal.

### Gaps
- No NASA document found that sets a numerical landing-accuracy requirement specifically for CLPS cargo landers (only HLS and the LSIC/pad studies).
- The ~118 m Blue Ghost figure and the 58 m Firefly figure use different references (target center vs ellipse edge); the official dispersion was not released.
- No NASA pad specification newer than the 2020 LSIC "notional, to be reviewed" list and the 2022 "200 m -> 100 m" statement was found.

## Key question 3: Published touchdown constraints (max vertical/lateral velocity, max tilt, max slope) and fuel margins for terminal descent

### Takeaway
The only fully published touchdown envelopes are Apollo's (10 ft/s vertical with 4 ft/s horizontal, 12 deg effective slope, 6 deg mean slope) and NASA's SPLICE ground rules (2.0 m/s vertical, 1 m/s horizontal, +/-6 deg attitude, 2 deg/s rates, 3 sigma); HLS requires the landed vehicle to be within 8 deg (threshold) / 5 deg (goal) of vertical; commercial landers quote 10 deg slope tolerance (Nova-C, Griffin site selection) but no velocity limits. Apollo carried ~2 minutes of hover propellant below 500 ft and landed Apollo 11 with ~45 s.

### Cited Findings
- Apollo LM design surface: "mean surface slope of 6 deg or less and an effective slope of 12 deg or less, including the effects of depressions or protuberances ... and footpad penetration"; max 24 in from highest protuberance to lowest depression within the footprint; bearing strength 1.0 psi static -> <=4 in penetration, 12 psi dynamic -> <=24 in; sliding friction 0.4-1.0; touchdown velocity envelope initially 10 ft/s vertical and 5 ft/s horizontal, reduced to 4 ft/s horizontal, and finally "for Vv <= 7 ft/sec, Vh = 4 ft/sec; and, for 7 ft/sec <= Vv <= 10 ft/sec, Vh = (40 - 3 Vv)/3 ft/sec"; gear ultimate safety factor 1.35 (1.50 on fittings); the reduced envelope "was still well outside the 3 sigma touchdown-velocity envelope"; tread radii 140-240 in studied, 167 in flown — [Rogers, Apollo Experience Report: LM Landing Gear Subsystem, NASA TN D-6850 (1972)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19720018253/downloads/19720018253.pdf)
- Apollo 11 actual touchdown: "negligible plus-Z velocity, a minus-Y velocity of approximately 2.1 ft/sec, and a minus-X velocity of approximately 1.7 ft/sec"; negligible strut stroking; footpad penetration 0.5-1.5 in and slide 18-22 in from post-flight simulation — [NASA TN D-6850](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19720018253/downloads/19720018253.pdf)
- NASA's 2026 HLS-program touchdown-dynamics verification re-creates the Apollo "Doghouse": "The Doghouse represents the design envelope used to certify allowable combinations of horizontal and vertical impact velocity. The LM Doghouse assumes a relatively steep 12 deg slope"; certification requires the toppling-stability boundary and three strut-stroke boundaries to lie outside it; Apollo 11 reconstruction initial conditions: axial 0.573 m/s, lateral -0.615 m/s, forward -0.213 m/s, roll 2.5 deg, ground slope 4.2-4.5 deg; LM gear geometry 2.239 m / 1.157 m (schematic dimensions); lunar gravity 1.63 m/s^2 — [Apollo 11 LM Touchdown Dynamics V&V for HLS, NASA 2026](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20260001760/downloads/Apollo%2011%20LM%20Touchdown%20Dynamics%20VnV%20HLS.pdf)
- Apollo tilt limits in practice: Apollo 15 rested at 11 deg tilt with one footpad in a 20 ft crater and "very nearly violating the tilt limit for safe ascent"; ALHAT's project manager: "If a lunar module came to rest at an angle beyond 12 degrees tilt the astronauts might not be able to launch"; Apollo 14 at 7 deg, Apollo 16 at 2.5 deg — [LSIC pads deck](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20205009125/downloads/Lunar%20Landing%20&%20Launch%20Pads%20v1.pdf); [NASA 2023 white paper](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/safe-and-precise-landing-at-lunar-sites.pdf)
- HLS landed-attitude requirement: "The HLS shall provide vertical orientation of 0 to 8 deg (threshold) and 0 to 5 deg (goal) from local vertical for surface operations. It is expected that the slope tolerance of the HLS will exceed the acceptable lander tilt angles for the safe and effective execution of critical crew functions" — [NASA HLS strategy 2020, Table 1](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20200001606/downloads/20200001606.pdf)
- SPLICE multi-mission touchdown ground rules: vertical <2.0 m/s, horizontal <1 m/s, attitude +/-6 deg, rates 2 deg/s, all 3 sigma — [SPLICE PL&HA requirements](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20200002415/downloads/20200002415.pdf)
- NASA government-reference Artemis lander: main engine cut-off at 1 m altitude and 1 m/s — [Lugo et al. 2020](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20205003002/downloads/nav.pdf)
- Commercial slope tolerances: Nova-C "designed to stay upright on slopes of up to 10 degrees" (then tipped on a 12 deg slope arriving fast) — [Wikipedia: Nova-C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intuitive_Machines_Nova-C); Astrobotic selects sites with "effective slope <= 10 deg" and rocks <=0.3 m (Peregrine site limit <=12 deg) — [Astrobotic PUG 2022](https://www.astrobotic.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/PUGLanders_011222.pdf)
- Hazard sizes NASA expects landers to tolerate / HDS to detect: 20 cm rocks and ~5 deg slopes; example lander with 20 deg slope tolerance and 30 cm roughness tolerance — [Restrepo et al. 2019](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20190034132/downloads/20190034132.pdf)
- Observed terminal velocities: Apollo 11 ~0.5-0.6 m/s; Chang'e-4 1.5 m/s slow descent to 30 m then free-fall from ~3 m (Chang'e-3) — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6760200/), [Spaceflight101](https://spaceflight101.com/change/?p=101); Blue Ghost ~1 m/s — [NSF](https://www.nasaspaceflight.com/2025/03/blue-ghost-im-2-landings/) (search excerpt); SLIM <=~1.4 m/s vertical but out-of-range lateral/attitude, survived — [NSF](https://www.nasaspaceflight.com/2024/01/slim-landing/) (search excerpt); IM-1 2.7 m/s vertical + 0.9 m/s lateral (IM) or 7.8 m/s total (NDL) onto 12 deg slope, broke a leg and tipped — see vehicle section; ispace M2 ~42 m/s at 193 m, destroyed — [Payload](https://payloadspace.com/ispace-blames-faulty-hardware-for-failed-lunar-landing/)
- Apollo descent propellant margins: nominal delta-V 6,827 fps; "85 fps is added to assure 2 minutes of flying time in the landing phase, that is, below an altitude of 500 feet" (automatic guidance needs only 104 s); 60 fps added for landing-point redesignation "to avoid large craters (1000 to 2000 feet in diameter)"; contingency allowances for a propellant-valve failure (64.7 lb) and low-level-sensor bias (68.7 lb); resulting margin 301 lb "converted to an additional hover or translation time of 32 seconds"; flight controllers call time from low-level light to give an abort-or-land decision at least 20 s before depletion; usable DPS load 7,051 lb fuel + 11,209 lb oxidizer — [Bennett, NASA TM X-58040](https://www.lpi.usra.edu/lunar/documents/Bennett_NASA-TM-X-58040.pdf)
- Apollo 11 landed with about 45 s of propellant remaining (post-flight), low-level light ~72 s before touchdown — [NASA SP-4029, Apollo 11](https://history.nasa.gov/SP-4029/Apollo_11a_Summary.htm); low-level light at ~5.6% of descent propellant, ~2 minutes of hover-thrust burn — [Enginehistory, LM DPS](https://www.enginehistory.org/Rockets/RPE09.41/RPE09.41.shtml) (search excerpts)
- Other margin evidence: SLIM's fault occurred at "approximately 98% through the total propulsion from separation to the Moon landing" — [JAXA 2024](https://www.isas.jaxa.jp/en/topics/files/SLIM-press-briefing-20241226.pdf); Chang'e-6 "Restricted by propellant" raised its hazard-avoidance altitude rather than hovering low — [Aerospace Control and Application 2024](https://yhjcjs.spacejournal.cn/en/article/id/9631); ispace M1 "ran out of fuel" after hovering at ~5 km — [ispace 2023](https://www.ispace-inc.com/2023/05/26/ispace-announces-results-of-the-hakuto-r-mission-1-lunar-landing/)

### Inferences
- A conservative, source-backed touchdown box for the simulation: vertical <=2.0 m/s (nominal ~1 m/s), horizontal <=1.0 m/s, attitude <=6 deg at contact, rates <=2 deg/s, on terrain of <=5 deg mean slope / <=8-12 deg effective slope with rocks <=20-30 cm; a landed tilt beyond 8 deg (HLS threshold) counts as degraded and beyond ~12 deg (Apollo ascent limit; IM-1 slope) as failure.
- Apollo's budgeting rule, 2 minutes of hover-capable propellant below ~150 m plus a redesignation allowance of ~60 fps (18 m/s), is the only published terminal-descent margin standard; it corresponds to roughly 85 + 60 = 145 fps (44 m/s) of delta-V above the nominal, and Apollo 11 consumed almost all of it (45 s left). A 2026-era autonomous lander with hazard-avoidance diverts of 10-50 m (Chang'e-4 12 m, Blue Ghost two diverts) needs far less lateral translation budget than Apollo's 300-600 m manual redesignations, but modern landers have carried very thin margins (SLIM at 98%, ispace M1 to depletion).

### Gaps
- No commercial lander (Nova-C, Blue Ghost, Griffin, Blue Moon, Starship) has published touchdown-velocity or leg-stroke limits, leg geometry, or propellant margin at touchdown.
- No HLS requirement for maximum touchdown velocity or landing-site slope was found in public documents; HLS-RQMT-002 is not public.

## Key question 4: NASA architecture documents on landing pads near a base: distance from habitats, pad size, required precision

### Takeaway
NASA's 2024 Moon-to-Mars architecture papers give the siting numbers used today: tens of meters separation for lander shadowing, >1,000 m for lander blast ejecta, and up to 5,000 m from landing areas to habitation zones; pad requirements remain notional (slope <5 deg, rocks >20 cm cleared, 3,000 Pa shear, landing within 100 m) with a pad diameter requirement of 200 m being negotiated down to 100 m, and the near-vs-far-from-habitat decision was still open as of the LSIC community report (decision expected ~2027).

### Cited Findings
- 2024 Moon to Mars ACR "Lunar Mobility Drivers and Needs" white paper: relocation of cargo from landing site to point of use is driven by "darkness caused by a lander's shadow, point of use contamination by landers, or blast ejecta"; distances: "Separation from lander shadowing (tens of meters)"; "Lander blast ejecta constraints (>1,000 m) due either to separation between the lander and existing infrastructure or lander ascent"; "Support for aggregation of elements in ideal habitation zones from available regional landing areas (up to 5,000 m)"; traverses from landing zones to habitation zones "could encounter slopes of up to 20 degrees"; planned surface mobility limited to ~1,500 kg cargo — [NASA ACR24 compiled white papers, Dec 2024](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/acr24-compiled-white-papers.pdf); same figures repeated in NASA's 2025 PAC briefing — [NASA Moon to Mars Architecture, PAC 2025](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/cds/researchers/nac/pac/2025/6-%20Moon%20to%20Mars%20Architecture_TAGGED.pdf)
- 2024 "Lunar Surface Cargo" white paper: cargo-lander reference mission must "ensure successful landing at an accessible and useable location on the lunar surface with sufficient precision" and perform end-of-life operations "including potential relocation" so cargo/assets are not adversely affected; gap identified "between 500 kg and 12,000 kg"; Table 1 lists CLPS task orders 70-475 kg, Human-class Delivery Lander (HDL) 12,000-15,000 kg, JAXA cargo lander up to 2,100 kg, ESA Argonaut under study — [NASA ACR24 white papers](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/acr24-compiled-white-papers.pdf)
- 2023 "Safe and Precise Landing at Lunar Sites": precision landing enables co-locating assets and keep-out zones; "The larger the engine, the higher the potential for PSI events that could damage existing surface assets"; Apollo 12 "sandblasted the Surveyor 3 lander located 155 meters away"; NASA "currently lacks direct in-situ measurements of PSI"; CLPS stereo cameras (SCALPSS) to measure ejecta size/velocity — [NASA 2023](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/safe-and-precise-landing-at-lunar-sites.pdf)
- 2022 ACR white paper: "Landing location accuracy will be required to place habitats, power systems and mobility systems without disturbing previously placed infrastructure ... Automated landing will be required for pre-deployed cargo" — [NASA 2022 ACR white paper](https://www.lpi.usra.edu/lunar/artemis/resources/WhitePaper_2023_MarsForwardCapabilitiesAtTheMoon.pdf) (search excerpt)
- LSIC/KSC notional Lunar Landing & Launch Pad requirements ("To be Reviewed", Oct 2020): terrain slope <5 deg; cleared of rocks >20 cm; withstand 3,000-4,000 deg C gas, 2,000-3,000 m/s gas velocity, 3,000 Pa shear stress; support landing within 100 m of a point; good visibility for pilots/sensors; survive launch ignition; "Over Flight Path (cannot fly over base for safety)" listed as a landing consideration; a 44.5 kN (10,000 lb) engine analysis at Mach 5 in lunar vacuum shown — [Mueller, LSIC E&C, 30 Oct 2020](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20205009125/downloads/Lunar%20Landing%20&%20Launch%20Pads%20v1.pdf)
- Pad diameter: Artemis requirement 200 m, effort to reduce to 100 m; pad mass scales with (D/2)^2; 100 m or smaller fits a single Starship delivery — [Gelino et al., ASCE 2022](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220000334/downloads/In%20situ%20Lunar%20Launch%20and%20Landing%20Pad%20Construction%20with%20Regolith%20and%20Thermoset%20Polymers%20ASCE%20Earth%20and%20Space%202022%20Paper%20SUBMITTED.pdf)
- LSIC community report: decision on landing near vs far from habitats expected by 2027; landing near requires a flat, hard pad with a berm to contain ejecta; landing far requires a traverse — [LSIC, The Path to an Enduring Lunar Presence](https://lsic.jhuapl.edu/Resources/files/The%20Path%20to%20an%20Enduring%20Lunar%20Presence.pdf) (search excerpt)
- Plume-ejecta scaling for HLS-class landers: ejecta mass scales as thrust^2.5; LM (~5 t landing mass) ejected ~2.6 t; a 40 t lander delivering 9 t "would eject (40 mT/5 mT)^2.5 = 181 times more ejecta than the LM, or 470 mT" (worst-case bound); erosion within a 12 m radius, ~1 m deep bowl; Constellation-era outpost on Shackleton rim had its landing zone 2.2 km from the habitation zone and 290 m lower; particles of 1-100 um reach the outpost if ejected above 10.5-21 deg, so "additional mitigation may be needed" — [Metzger et al., SSERVI ESF 2019](https://www.lpi.usra.edu/exploration/publications/MetzgerEtAl_SSERVI%20ESF2019_LanderPlumeEjecta.pdf)
- High-velocity ejecta risk vs distance (10 t lander, 1.7-2.3 km/s fraction): ~594 kg in that velocity band, ~537 kg re-deposits; "at 1 km, an unprotected base might expect as many as 8 impacts per landing, each with a kinetic energy of 23.5 J. At 5 km, one would expect a 37% chance of impact"; a larger lander could generate ~6,917 kg in that band — [Wittal et al., AAS 20-511 (NTRS)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20205003590/downloads/20_Wittaletal_LanderPlumeEjecta.pdf)
- Apollo 12 plume damaged Surveyor 3 at ~160 m with ~19 kN thrust — [UNOOSA presentation 2022](https://www.unoosa.org/documents/pdf/copuos/stsc/2022/09_For_All_Moonkind_STSC_Presentation_2022.pdf) (search excerpt); ~90% of south-pole ejecta re-impacts near the site falling off exponentially with radius — [ScienceDirect survey 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0094576524006672) (search excerpt); a 2026 proposal to use <1.5 km craters as natural PSI containment — [Nature 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44453-026-00045-w) (search excerpt)
- Apollo dust/visibility: dust sheet from <100 ft (30 m) on Apollo 11; Apollo 12 blind below ~300 ft; Apollo 15 nothing visible below ~60 ft (18 m) — [LSIC pads deck](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20205009125/downloads/Lunar%20Landing%20&%20Launch%20Pads%20v1.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a south-pole base simulation the defensible layout numbers are: landing zone >=1 km from any unprotected infrastructure (NASA 2024 ejecta constraint), with habitation up to 5 km away and traverse slopes up to 20 deg; a prepared pad of 100 m diameter (200 m if the 100 m 3-sigma accuracy is not met), graded to <5 deg and cleared of >20 cm rocks; no overflight of the base during approach.
- NASA's own numbers imply that precision landing (50-100 m) is what makes a pad affordable: pad mass scales with the square of the dispersion radius, so halving dispersion from 100 to 50 m cuts pad material by 4x and is the difference between one and several Starship deliveries.
- Expect sensor degradation from PSI in the last 20-30 m (Apollo visibility loss below 18-30 m; IM-2 dust plume disturbing laser/rangefinder readings), which argues for freezing the hazard map and relying on IMU/NDL velocity below ~30 m, as the SPLICE and Astrobotic terminal-descent designs already do.

### Gaps
- No NASA document gives a definitive, approved minimum pad-to-habitat distance; the >1,000 m figure is a mobility-planning constraint, not a safety standard, and the LSIC near/far decision was reported as pending.
- No post-2022 NASA pad-diameter requirement was found; the "200 m -> 100 m" statement is from a 2022 conference paper quoting discussions.
- No in-situ PSI ejecta measurement exists yet (SCALPSS flew on Blue Ghost M1 and is planned for Blue Moon MK1; results not found in this search).
