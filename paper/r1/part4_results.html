5. Results

The experiment ran {{R1-N-YEARS}} simulated years in {{R1-N-CELLS}} cells (Section 4, Table 5). A cell mean carries a Student-t 95 % interval over its 20 seeds; a paired effect is the mean per-seed difference with its t interval. For urgent delay, which is skewed, we also give medians, the geometric-mean ratio with its interval and the Wilcoxon signed-rank p (Section 4.4). The seeds start in four calendar years, 2028–2031, five in each, so every interval includes the calendar variation of those four years; for the primary comparisons we also give the interval with the start year as the unit (the four yearly means of the paired differences, t on 3 degrees of freedom). The six primary comparisons are numbered as in Section 4.4, and all intervals are per comparison, not adjusted for multiplicity. Unless a sentence says otherwise, a result is from Stage A, with pads at 1 km, the criterion any part of the disc, the 24 h deferral limit, the fixed night share and the plume keep-out off. Two conditions hold throughout: the relay is assumed to see every route point, and the fleet is lightly used, busy {{R1-UTIL-ALWAYS}} of the year with Earth always in view.

5.1 Where Earth can be seen

Figure 1 puts the link fractions of Table 2 on the map. The four sites on the crest of the ridge, the base, the power plant, the construction site and the depot, see some part of Earth {{R1-FRAC-CREST}} of the time. The oxygen pilot plant sits in a hollow east of the base and never sees Earth in 2028–2031. The science site and the shadowed crater see it {{R1-FRAC-SCI}} and {{R1-FRAC-PSR}} of the time, in windows of about three days near each monthly maximum of Earth's elevation, and the science site has no window at all from January to late November 2028 (Section 3.5). A route is linked only while every 100 m sample on it is linked. Every route to the oxygen plant is therefore dark, every route to the science site or the crater is linked at most {{R1-FRAC-PSR}} of the time, and pad B at 1 km, itself linked {{R1-FRAC-PADB1}} of the time, has routes that are linked less (Section 3.5).

5.2 Throughput: what is lost and why

With DTE only, every fleet that needs a link to drive loses about the same tonnage (Table 6, Figure 2; primary comparison 1). The supervised fleet under the strict policy delivers {{R1-T-DTE-CI}} in the year against {{R1-T-ALWAYS-CI}} with Earth always in view, a paired change of {{R1-T-LOSS-T}} ({{R1-T-LOSS-PCT}}); with the start year as the unit the interval is {{R1-T-LOSS-YB}}. The teleoperated fleet, the reference mix and the look-ahead policy lose {{R1-T-LOSS-PCT-RANGE}}. The strategic policy, which drives without a link and needs one only when an intervention stops a rover, loses {{R1-T-LOSS-STRAT}} on average (Section 5.4), and an autonomous fleet loses nothing.

The loss splits by cargo class, and most of it comes from the demand model rather than from the fleet. Bulk regolith falls from {{R1-BULK-ALWAYS}} to {{R1-BULK-DTE}} a year ({{R1-BULK-LOSS-PCT}}). Phase 2 hauls 300 t a year from the construction site to the base and 200 t a year to the oxygen plant (Section 4.1, Assumed in [33]), and the plant never sees Earth, so no rover that needs a link can deliver its feedstock. The cargo made ready in the year for destinations never linked under DTE is {{R1-BOUND-T}}, and all of it is lost ({{R1-LOST-NEVER-T}}): {{R1-LOSS-SHARE-NEVER}} of the gap of {{R1-GAP-T}}. Cargo from landers does not depend on that assumption, and it falls from {{R1-LANDED-ALWAYS}} to {{R1-LANDED-DTE}} a year, {{R1-LANDED-LOSS-PCT}} (geometric-mean ratio {{R1-LANDED-GM}}; with the start year as the unit, {{R1-LANDED-YB}}). Returns to the pads fall by {{R1-RETURN-LOSS-PCT}}. If the oxygen plant dug its own feedstock (Stage E), the same fleet would deliver {{R1-LOCAL-DTE}} a year under DTE against {{R1-LOCAL-ALWAYS}} always linked, a loss of {{R1-LOCAL-LOSS}}. The headline percentage is therefore mostly the feedstock assumption; the loss of lander cargo is not.

The rest of the gap, {{R1-GAP-ELSE-T}}, is cargo for sites that Earth does reach, and little of it is still under way at the end of the year: the strict fleet picks up {{R1-PICKED-DTE}} under DTE and drops off {{R1-DROPPED-DTE}} inside the year. Most of the rest is never picked up. The backlog at the end of the year is {{R1-BACKLOG-DTE}}, of which {{R1-BOUND-T}} is for the oxygen plant, and {{R1-READY-GAP}} less cargo becomes ready in the year than with Earth always in view. Pad B at 1 km receives nothing under DTE, against {{R1-PADB-ALWAYS}} a year always linked.

Any relay restores the tonnage, under the two conditions stated at the start of this section: the relay sees every route point, including the oxygen plant in its hollow 139 m below the base, and the fleet has slack. At the lowest coverage tested, {{R1-P-LOW}}, the strict fleet recovers {{R1-RESTORE-LOW-STRICT}} of the lost tonnage and {{R1-RESTORE-LOW-LANDED}} of the lost lander cargo, and every relay level recovers at least {{R1-RESTORE-MIN}} of the lost tonnage for every fleet. The throughput knee, the smallest coverage at which the fleet reaches 95 % of its full-coverage throughput, is at {{R1-A-KNEE-P}}{{R1-KNEE-REMARK}}. This fits a loss that turns on whether a route can be driven at all, not on how often: a relay that is up a few hours at a time opens every route (Figure 2). The one exception in Stage E is a periodic relay with the 30 h period of Reference Constellation 3.1 at coverage 0.1, under which the teleoperated fleet's tonnage changes by {{R1-E-RC31-TELEOP-T-01}} (Section 5.8).

Extra rovers and a mast restore none of the tonnage. Under DTE one extra supervised rover changes the delivered tonnage by {{R1-ROVER-GAIN-T}} and urgent completion by {{R1-ROVER-COMP-DTE}}; a 10 m mast at the base changes them by {{R1-MAST-GAIN-T}} and {{R1-MAST-COMP-DTE}} (Stage B, strict policy). The mast links the inner routes, but the dark routes are the far ones (Section 3.5). Under DTE the strict fleet is busy {{R1-UTIL-STRICT-DTE}} of the year, and more rovers cannot add throughput to a fleet with that much slack; whether they would in a busy fleet we did not test.

Under DTE half or fewer of the urgent items that become ready in the year are delivered in it (Table 6; primary comparison 2). Under DTE this percentile is a lower bound in every seed.

Means of the censored measure are dominated by a few seeds. 5.4 Driving without a link: the strategic and look-ahead policies

Under DTE, letting supervised rovers drive between interventions without a link appears to recover most of the urgent deliveries (primary comparison 4, extended to DTE), but the gain rests on a second chance that the model gives for free. The strategic fleet's completion share has a median over seeds of {{R1-STRAT-COMP-MED}} against {{R1-STRICT-COMP-MED}} for the strict fleet. It delivers {{R1-STRAT-LANDED-GM}} times as much lander cargo (geometric-mean ratio), and its censored 90th percentile is {{R1-STRAT-CP90-GM}} times the strict fleet's (Wilcoxon {{R1-STRAT-CP90-W}}).

Under the strategic rule an intervention waits for its route sample's next link-up (Table 4). If that sample never sees Earth in 2028–2031, no wait is finite, so the job is refused and dispatch tries it again later with a new draw: a rover never stops at such a point. Under DTE {{R1-F-NEVER-SAMPLES}} route samples (the route cells of Section 3.1 and the 17 sites and pads that end the legs) never see Earth. They lie on {{R1-F-NEVER-LEGS}} legs of the main network, among them every leg to or from the oxygen plant, whose own site never sees Earth. The strategic fleet re-draws {{R1-F-REDRAWS}} jobs a run ({{R1-F-REDRAWS-BY-YEAR}} for runs starting in 2028, 2029, 2030 and 2031). If the rover instead stays where the intervention stopped it until the year ends (Stage F), the strategic fleet delivers {{R1-F-STRAND-T}} a year ({{R1-F-STRAND-T-BY-YEAR}} by start year). That is {{R1-F-STRAND-VS-STRAT}} against the strategic fleet and {{R1-F-STRAND-VS-STRICT}} against the strict fleet. It delivers {{R1-F-STRAND-COMP}} of urgent items ({{R1-F-STRAND-VS-STRICT-COMP}} against the strict fleet) and waits {{R1-F-STRAND-WAIT}} rover-hours a year for Earth. Neither end is realistic. No operator gets an intervention moved for free, and a rover stopped at a point that never sees Earth might be reached by another rover parked in line of sight or by a tow.

Stage G puts a delay on that help (the helper's own time is not modelled, so the delays are optimistic). The fleet needs such help {{R1-G-RESCUES}} times a run, fewer as the delay grows. With help within a week, 0 to 168 h, the strategic fleet delivers {{R1-G-WEEK-T}}, not significantly different from the free re-draw: at 168 h the difference is {{R1-G-WEEK-VS-STRAT}} and the censored urgent 90th percentile is {{R1-G-WEEK-CP90-VS-STRAT}} times the re-draw's, and no delay up to 168 h differs significantly in either. With help after a month, 720 h, it delivers {{R1-G-MONTH-T}}: {{R1-G-MONTH-VS-STRAT}} against the free re-draw and no more than the strict fleet ({{R1-G-MONTH-VS-STRICT}}). Yet it still delivers {{R1-G-MONTH-COMP}} of urgent items, {{R1-G-MONTH-COMP-VS-STRICT}} against the strict fleet, and its censored 90th percentile is {{R1-G-MONTH-CP90-GM}} times the strict fleet's (Wilcoxon {{R1-G-MONTH-CP90-W}}). Against the free re-draw, help after a month lowers urgent completion by {{R1-G-MONTH-COMP-VS-STRAT}} and lengthens the censored 90th percentile {{R1-G-MONTH-CP90-VS-STRAT}} times (Wilcoxon {{R1-G-MONTH-CP90-VS-STRAT-W}}). Against the strict fleet the tonnage break-even lies at about 720 h or beyond (95 % bootstrap interval {{R1-G-BE-T-CI}}; {{R1-G-BE-T-BEYOND}} of the draws beyond 720 h and {{R1-G-BE-T-BELOW}} below 0 h), and the urgent-completion break-even lies beyond 720 h in every draw. Within these intervals, then, the strategic gain under DTE holds when help takes days; when help takes a month the tonnage gain is gone and the urgent gain is smaller but still well ahead of the strict fleet. With no help it is lost.

Even with the free re-draw, the tonnage recovers in only {{R1-STRAT-GOOD-YEARS}} start years (Figure 4), because the rule's second feature, waiting out any finite wait, is costly too. The strategic fleet's mean tonnage under DTE is {{R1-STRAT-T-BY-YEAR}} for runs starting in 2028, 2029, 2030 and 2031. In 2028 the fleet spent {{R1-STRAT-WAIT-2028}} rover-hours a year waiting for a link, of the 52,560 rover-hours of six rovers, against {{R1-STRAT-WAIT-OTHER}} in the other years, and the cause is one place on one route. Under the strategic policy a rover stopped by an intervention waits where it stands until that point sees Earth (Table 4), and interventions fall on uniformly drawn route points. One 100 m sample on the regolith haul from the oxygen plant to the construction site sees Earth at no hour from January 2028 until {{R1-SY-FIRST-LINK}}, and it holds {{R1-SY-SHARE}} of the rover-hours the fleet spends stranded in 2028 (waits longer than 24 h). A rover stopped there stays for the rest of the year, so the fleet is captured rover by rover: the stranded share of the six rovers rises from {{R1-SY-JAN}} in January 2028 to {{R1-SY-LATE}} on average from May to December, and {{R1-SY-NOTRESUME}} of the 2028 strandings are still waiting at the year's end, against {{R1-SY-NOTRESUME-OTHER}} in the later years. In those years the same spot strands rovers too, but it releases them within the year and the spare rovers carry the work. Urgent cargo suffers far less: it is about {{R1-SY-URG-N}} items a year, {{R1-SY-URG-BASE}} of them bound for the base on short pad legs far from the dark spot, and the less than one rover left free from June on ({{R1-SY-FREE-LATE}} on average) clears most of it ({{R1-SY-URG-COMP}} delivered in the 2028 runs). Help at never-linked points alone does not change 2028: the dark sample sees Earth again in {{R1-SY-FIRST-LINK}}, so its interventions are ordinary long waits, and with any rescue time of Stage G the fleet delivers {{R1-G-2028}} in 2028, with no trend across the rescue times (five seeds each).

Help for any long wait does change it (Stage G2). If every rover held longer than the rescue time gets help, the strategic fleet delivers {{R1-G2-WEEK-T}} a year with help within a week, and {{R1-G2-WEEK-2028}} in 2028. In every start year that is within 5 % of the {{R1-T-ALWAYS-STRAT}} the same fleet delivers with Earth always in view (2028 at 168 h: {{R1-G2-2028-VS-STRAT}} against the strategic fleet), and {{R1-G2-COMP-168}} of urgent items arrive at 168 h. Against the strict fleet that is {{R1-G2-VS-STRICT-168}} at 168 h. Even with help after a month the fleet delivers {{R1-G2-MONTH-T}} ({{R1-G2-MONTH-2028}} in 2028), {{R1-G2-MONTH-VS-STRICT}} against the strict fleet, and the break-evens against the strict fleet lie beyond 720 h in {{R1-G2-BE-T-SHARE}} of the bootstrap draws for tonnage and in every draw for urgent completion. The help is frequent: {{R1-G2-RESCUES-168}} times a run at 168 h, and {{R1-G2-RESCUES-0}} at 0 h, where no intervention waits at all and which is therefore an upper bound. Under DTE, then, most of what driving without a link loses is lost to waits without limit. A rule or service that ends every wait within about a week recovers it, at the price of the helper's time, which is not modelled.

The calendar, not the demand, decides it. Rerunning eight seeds with their landings fixed (the manifests are drawn again from the same distribution) and only the start year changed, {{R1-SY-SWAP-N}} of {{R1-SY-SWAP-N}} seeds deliver least in 2028 (means {{R1-SY-SWAP}} for 2028–2031). Earth climbs as high in 2028 as later ({{R1-SY-MAXEL}} at most at the base), but its direction at those heights moves: above 5.5° it stands at grid bearings {{R1-SY-BEAR-2028}} in 2028 and {{R1-SY-BEAR-2030}} in 2030, while the dark sample sees Earth only at bearings {{R1-SY-SAMPLE-BEAR}}, past a ridge. That part of the sky holds Earth for {{R1-SY-BOX-HOURS}} hours in 2028, 2029, 2030 and 2031. The shape of the libration pattern repeats with the beat between the anomalistic and the draconic month, about six years, so such a year should recur; we did not check later years. The wide interval of the strategic fleet's tonnage is therefore the calendar acting through one dark spot, not chance.

The look-ahead policy does not recover the loss. It checks each route sample before committing and never takes a route with a never-linked sample (Table 4), so under DTE it refuses the same dark routes as the strict policy. Its completion share is {{R1-AVOID-COMP-DTE}} against {{R1-COMP-DTE-STRICT}} for the strict fleet, its censored 90th percentile is unchanged (ratio {{R1-AVOID-CP90-GM}}) and it delivers {{R1-AVOID-T-DIFF}} more. It does avoid the strategic policy's long waits: {{R1-WAIT-AVOID-DTE}} rover-hours a year against {{R1-WAIT-STRAT-DTE}}.

A bound on the wait does not find a middle (Stage F). Consider a rule that defers a job when an intervention on its route would wait longer than h hours, with h from 24 to 720. If a never-linked sample refuses its route, as under the look-ahead, the rule delivers {{R1-F-REFUSE-T}} at every bound, and no feedstock reaches the oxygen plant. The bound then only trades waiting ({{R1-F-REFUSE-WAIT}} rover-hours a year) for nothing. If never-linked samples are left to the free re-draw, the rule keeps {{R1-F-KEEP-BULK}} of the strategic fleet's bulk haul and delivers {{R1-F-KEEP-LATER}} in runs starting in 2029–2031. In 2028, however, it delivers {{R1-F-KEEP-2028}}, no more than the strategic fleet's {{R1-F-STRAT-2028}} (paired at h = 24 h: {{R1-F-KEEP-2028-VS-STRAT}}, five seeds). The cause is new. Each dispatch call auctions only the {{R1-F-POOL-CAP}} most urgent, then oldest, waiting items, and in 2028 the deferred feedstock items fill that pool. At hour {{R1-F-POOL-T}} of one run, {{R1-F-POOL-ISRU}} of the {{R1-F-POOL-CAP}} places hold them and none can be taken. Meanwhile {{R1-F-POOL-SERVABLE}} items that could be served wait outside the pool, and {{R1-F-POOL-FREE}} rovers stand idle. The run is typical: in all {{R1-F-POOL-N}} 2028 seeds the pool is full on {{R1-F-POOL-FULL-ALL}} of days, and feedstock items for the oxygen plant hold {{R1-F-POOL-ISRU-ALL}} of its places. The collapse moves from stranded rovers to a clogged dispatcher (Section 7).

With a relay, the strategic step shortens the delay of the urgent items that are delivered but does not significantly change the share delivered or the censored delay. At coverage 0.1 the strategic fleet's delivered-item 90th percentile is {{R1-STRAT-P90-GM-01}} times the strict fleet's (Wilcoxon {{R1-STRAT-P90-W-01}}; with the start year as the unit, a difference of {{R1-STRAT-P90-YB-01}}), and at 0.5 it is {{R1-STRAT-P90-GM-05}} times. On the censored 90th percentile the ratios are {{R1-STRAT-CP90-GM-01}} at 0.1 (Wilcoxon {{R1-STRAT-CP90-W-01}}) and {{R1-STRAT-CP90-GM-05}} at 0.5 ({{R1-STRAT-CP90-W-05}}), and the completion shares differ by {{R1-STRAT-COMP-DIFF-01}} at 0.1. The look-ahead policy behaves the same with a relay ({{R1-AVOID-P90-GM-01}} on delivered items and {{R1-AVOID-CP90-GM-01}} censored, at 0.1). Both policies cut the blackout wait at coverage 0.1 by {{R1-STRAT-WAIT-CUT-01}}. With a relay no route sample is never linked, so nothing is re-drawn and the stranding variant of Stage F reproduces the strategic fleet's results exactly. Because the relay is assumed to see every route point, the relay results of this section and of Section 5.5 do not depend on the free second chance; a relay masked at the oxygen plant would bring it back. 5.6 Operator demand

With the soft operator pool of the main stages (Table 4), link windows push the teleoperated fleet's linked operator demand above the three operators on duty (Figure 6; primary comparison 6). Its hourly peak, averaged over seeds, is {{R1-OP-PEAK-TELEOP-DTE}} operators under DTE, above three in {{R1-OP-SEEDS-TELEOP-DTE}} of 20 seeds. It is highest at coverage {{R1-OP-PEAK-TELEOP-MAXP}}, {{R1-OP-PEAK-TELEOP-MAX}} operators, above three in {{R1-OP-SEEDS-TELEOP-MAXP}} of 20 seeds, and lowest at full coverage, {{R1-OP-PEAK-TELEOP-FULL}} operators, above three in {{R1-OP-SEEDS-TELEOP-FULL}}. The reference mix, with two teleoperated vehicles, peaks at {{R1-OP-PEAK-DEFAULT-MAX}} operators at most (cell mean) and never above three in any seed. The peak divided by the mean demand is a poor measure here: under DTE the teleoperated fleet's mean linked demand falls from {{R1-OP-MEAN-TELEOP-FULL}} to {{R1-OP-MEAN-TELEOP-DTE}} operators because less work is done, so that ratio rises even where the peak does not.

Under the soft pool the admission check sees only the demand linked at that moment, so these peaks overstate what a fixed staff would allow. Stage A2 repeats the teleoperated and reference mixes with a hard limit, which keeps the peak at three operators. The teleoperated fleet then waits for an operator {{R1-A2-WAIT-DTE}} vehicle-hours a year under DTE, {{R1-A2-WAIT-MAX}} at coverage {{R1-A2-WAIT-MAXP}} and {{R1-A2-WAIT-FULL}} at full coverage. Its delivered-item 90th percentile {{R1-A2-P90-SUMMARY}}, and its censored 90th percentile does not differ significantly at any coverage. For the reference mix the hard limit never binds, and every result is identical. The teleoperated results of Sections 5.2 and 5.3 are therefore slightly optimistic in delay, not in throughput.

Full relay coverage reproduces the always-linked cell seed by seed in every throughput and delay metric for {{R1-FR-IDENTICAL-N}} of the six fleets. It does not for the teleoperated fleet, because the always-linked mode books operator demand for whole jobs (Section 3.7). That fleet's delivered-item 90th percentile differs in {{R1-FR-P90-SEEDS}} of 20 seeds (by up to {{R1-FR-P90-MAX}}), its censored 90th percentile in {{R1-FR-CP90-SEEDS}} (by up to {{R1-FR-CP90-MAX}}), its delivered tonnage in {{R1-FR-T-SEEDS}} (by up to {{R1-FR-T-MAX}}) and its count of jobs that waited for an operator in {{R1-FR-WAITS-SEEDS}}. Operator demand is therefore compared against full relay coverage, never against the always-linked cell.

5.7 Pads, the link criterion and the deferral limit

Pad standoff decides how much lander cargo DTE alone can move (Table 9). With pads at 0.5 km the reference mix under DTE delivers {{R1-C-LANDED-05}} of lander cargo a year against {{R1-C-LANDED-1}} with pads at 1 km (paired change {{R1-C-LANDED-05-DIFF}}), and it completes {{R1-C-COMP-05}} of its urgent items against {{R1-C-COMP-1}}. With pads at 3 km, where every pad leg is dark (Table 3), it moves no lander cargo and delivers no urgent item in any seed. The {{R1-C-T-3}} it delivers is regolith from the construction site to the base, and its censored 90th percentile, {{R1-C-CP90-3}}, is a lower bound in every seed. The teleoperated fleet behaves the same. With a relay at 0.8 the standoff matters little: with pads at 3 km instead of 1 km, the strict fleet's lander cargo changes by {{R1-C-RELAY-LANDED-3-DIFF}} and its urgent completion by {{R1-C-RELAY-COMP-3-DIFF}}.

Throughput hardly depends on the link criterion: requiring Earth's centre 0.5° or 2° above the horizon instead of any part of the disc changes the delivered tonnage by at most {{R1-CRIT-RANGE}} in any cell. With pads at 0.5 km the stricter criteria lengthen the reference mix's delivered-item 90th percentile by {{R1-CRIT-05-05}} and {{R1-CRIT-05-2}}, with the same completion. With pads at 1 km under DTE that percentile falls instead, from {{R1-CRIT-1-P90}}, but this is censoring. The stricter criteria deliver fewer urgent items ({{R1-CRIT-1-COMP}} for the reference mix, {{R1-CRIT-1-COMP-TELEOP}} for the teleoperated fleet), and the censored 90th percentile rises (ratios {{R1-CRIT-1-CP90}} for the reference mix). {{R1-CRIT-NONE-SHORTER}}

The deferral limit matters little. Under DTE, limits of 12, 24 and 72 h and no limit give the reference mix the same completion share and censored 90th percentile (ratios against 24 h of {{R1-DEFER-DTE-CP90-RANGE}}, every Wilcoxon {{R1-DEFER-DTE-W-MIN}}). For the teleoperated fleet a 12 h limit lowers urgent completion by {{R1-DEFER12-TELEOP-COMP}}. With a relay at 0.8, a 72 h limit or none shortens the strict fleet's delivered-item 90th percentile by {{R1-DEFER-REL-P90}} but lowers its completion share by {{R1-DEFER-REL-COMP}}, a small cost when coverage is high.

5.8 The real Sun and other sensitivities

Stage D replaces the fixed night share with the real Sun. At 2.5 m the base is lit {{R1-SUN-BASE}} of the hours, the depot, the construction site and the pads at 1 km {{R1-SUN-MID}}, the oxygen plant {{R1-SUN-ISRU}} and the shadowed crater never, against 85 % everywhere under the fixed share. Under DTE the real Sun changes neither the tonnage ({{R1-D-DTE-T}} for the strict fleet) nor the completion share or the censored 90th percentile (ratio {{R1-D-DTE-CP90-GM}}): the link already limits those cells. Only the 90th percentile of the items delivered rises (ratio {{R1-D-DTE-P90-GM}}). With a relay at 0.5 it lengthens urgent delay several times over. Tonnage changes by {{R1-D-REL-T}}. The delay results of Sections 5.3–5.5 therefore hold for the fixed night share; with the real Sun the urgent delay at a given coverage is longer.

Stage E compares each remaining setting with its Stage A twin. The relay's gap pattern changes delay but not throughput. With mean gaps of 2 h instead of 7.67 h, the strict fleet's delivered-item 90th percentile is {{R1-E-GAP2-STRICT-01}} times its Stage A value at coverage 0.1 and {{R1-E-GAP2-STRICT-05}} at 0.5; with 24 h gaps it is {{R1-E-GAP24-STRICT-01}} and {{R1-E-GAP24-STRICT-05}} (teleoperated fleet at 0.1: {{R1-E-GAP24-TELEOP-01}}). A periodic relay with the DRM-0 period of 784 minutes gives shorter delays than our exponential stand-in ({{R1-E-DRM0-STRICT-01}} at 0.1, {{R1-E-DRM0-STRICT-05}} at 0.5). At 0.5, though, the strict fleet completes fewer urgent items under it ({{R1-E-RC31-STRICT-COMP-05}}) and its censored 90th percentile rises ({{R1-E-RC31-STRICT-CP90-05}}), and at 0.1 the teleoperated fleet's tonnage changes by {{R1-E-RC31-TELEOP-T-01}}. The shape of the delay curve at low coverage therefore depends on the gap pattern we assumed; the throughput result does not.

Halving the intervention rate helps the strategic fleet (with its free re-draw), by {{R1-E-INTX05-STRAT-T}} of tonnage under DTE and with a censored ratio of {{R1-E-INTX05-STRAT-CP90-05}} at coverage 0.5.
