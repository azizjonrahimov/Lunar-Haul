5. Results

5.1 What each regime delivers

At every load the regimes fall into three groups (Table 5). Owners working alone keep urgent cargo waiting longest, whether they are coordinated from Earth or on the Moon: {{P1-A-URGENT-RANGE-30}} on average at the middle load. Pooled regimes that lose contact with Earth part of the time come next, from {{P1-RELAY08-URGENT-30}} with an 80 % relay to {{P1-B-STRICT-URGENT-30}} with strict fallback. The regimes that decide on the Moon, the full-information marketplace and the dispatcher on Earth with a relay that is always up sit together at the bottom, from {{P1-LIN-URGENT-30}} with learned linear bids to {{P1-LEADER-URGENT-30}} with {{P1-BOTTOM-MAX-NAME}}. Tonnes delivered are almost the same within the pooled group ({{P1-POOLED-T-RANGE-30}}) and much lower for owners alone ({{P1-A-T-RANGE-30}}).

The fleet is lightly used even at the middle load. The two cargo rovers are busy {{P1-CARGO-UTIL-30}} of the year, the whole fleet {{P1-UTIL-FLEET-30}}, because the crew rovers, small rovers and hoppers can lift almost none of the cargo. At light load every pooled regime keeps urgent waits within {{P1-POOLED-SPREAD-10}} of one another except the strict dispatcher on Earth ({{P1-B-STRICT-URGENT-10}}). {{P1-GROUPS-60-SENTENCE}}

5.2 Sharing: capacity and dispatch (H1)

Pooling cut the mean urgent wait by {{P1-H1-DIFF-30}} at the middle load (95 % CI {{P1-H1-DIFF-30-CI}}, paired, n = {{P1-H1-N-30}}), comparing the pooled dispatcher and owners alone, both with Earth always in view. H1 is supported (Holm-adjusted p {{P1-H1-PHOLM}}). {{P1-H1-60-SENTENCE}}

That difference lies almost entirely in cargo that owners alone never deliver. Owner C's small rovers and hoppers cannot lift its crates or its regolith lots, and without cross-owner pairs no owner can lift most team items; by the end of a year owners alone leave {{P1-A-BACKLOG-T-30}} waiting. Counting delivered items only, which conditions on delivery and so compares different sets of items, owners alone with strict fallback wait {{P1-A-DELIV-MINUS-O-30}} longer than the full-information marketplace, and {{P1-H1-UNDELIVERED-SHARE}} of the censored gap lies in items that owners alone had not delivered by the end of the year. Split as designed in Section 4.2, single-robot jobs pooled across owners, together with the pooled dispatcher, account for {{P1-H1-POOL-DIFF}} of the gap ({{P1-H1-POOL-SHARE}}; 95 % CI {{P1-H1-POOL-DIFF-CI}}) and lifts by pairs of different owners' rovers for {{P1-H1-TEAM-DIFF}} ({{P1-H1-TEAM-SHARE}}; {{P1-H1-TEAM-DIFF-CI}}). The pooled side of the first part is the robots' auction, whose difference from the full-information marketplace is given in Section 5.4. By owner, pooling cut the mean urgent wait by {{P1-H1-OWNER-A-DIFF}} for owner A, {{P1-H1-OWNER-B-DIFF}} for owner B and {{P1-H1-OWNER-C-DIFF}} for owner C, the owner without a cargo rover.

5.3 A dispatcher on Earth (H2)

A pooled dispatcher on Earth that stops when Earth sets kept urgent cargo waiting {{P1-H2-STRICT-MINUS-O}} longer than the full-information marketplace, pooled over all start years (95 % CI {{P1-H2-STRICT-MINUS-O-CI}}, n = {{P1-H2-N}}): {{P1-B-STRICT-URGENT-30}} against {{P1-O-URGENT-30-ALL}}. The pre-registered margin was 10 % of the marketplace's mean, {{P1-H2-MARGIN}}; the lower end of the interval clears it by a wide distance in each of the {{P1-H2-N-LOWVIS-YEARS}} start years whose base visibility is below 60 % ({{P1-H2-LOWVIS-YEARS}}), where the year means of the difference range from {{P1-H2-YEAR-MIN}} to {{P1-H2-YEAR-MAX}} (Figure 1). H2 is supported (Holm-adjusted p {{P1-H2-PHOLM}}).

The backlog at the end of a blackout averages {{P1-B-STRICT-BACKLOG-30}} under strict fallback against {{P1-O-BACKLOG-30}} for the marketplace, and the share of urgent items picked up within a day falls from {{P1-O-WITHIN24-30}} to {{P1-B-STRICT-WITHIN24-30}}. Planning ahead recovers most of it: uploading per-vehicle queues and a standing rule for blackout landings leaves {{P1-PLAN-MINUS-O}} of extra wait (95 % CI {{P1-PLAN-MINUS-O-CI}}). A relay that is up 80 % of the time leaves {{P1-RELAY08-MINUS-O}} ({{P1-RELAY08-MINUS-O-CI}}). That is outside the 10 % margin we set for it, so the secondary expectation that an 80 % relay comes within 10 % of the marketplace fails.

The original yardstick of H2, half the gap between owners alone and the marketplace, would have been {{P1-H2-OLD-YARDSTICK-HALF}}, larger than anything Earth's availability could cost a pooled fleet; we report it as registered but do not test it (Section 4.6).

5.4 Deciding on the Moon (H3a)

The robots' own auction, at message loss 0.10 over the terrain-limited network, kept urgent cargo waiting {{P1-H3A-DIFF}} than the full-information marketplace ({{P1-H3A-PCT}}; 90 % CI {{P1-H3A-CI90}}, n = {{P1-H3A-N}}). We had registered the expectation that it would come within ±10 % of the marketplace, ±{{P1-H2-MARGIN}}. {{P1-H3A-VERDICT}} Because the auction, the leader and the marketplace never wait for Earth, a seed gives the same run in every start year; this comparison therefore uses the {{P1-H3A-N}} runs of block one only (change 4 of the pre-registration [43]). An earlier interim reading that counted the repeated runs as independent suggested the auction beat the marketplace; on independent runs that difference is not distinguishable from zero.

The elected leader, which runs the marketplace itself on the base computer and loses messages on the way, came within {{P1-LEADER-MINUS-O}} of it ({{P1-LEADER-MINUS-O-CI}}).

5.6 Learned bids (H4)

The learned linear correction cut the mean urgent wait relative to the hand-built auction by {{P1-H4-LIN-DIFF-30}} at the middle load ({{P1-H4-LIN-PCT-30}}; 95 % CI {{P1-H4-LIN-CI-30}}, n = {{P1-H4-N}}) and by {{P1-H4-LIN-DIFF-10}} at light load ({{P1-H4-LIN-PCT-10}}; {{P1-H4-LIN-CI-10}}). For the linear model H4's first part is supported (Holm-adjusted p {{P1-H4-LIN-PHOLM}}); its second part, no difference beyond ±2 % at light load, fails. {{P1-H4-60-SENTENCE}}

The network did not improve on the hand-built bids. At the middle load its difference from the auction was {{P1-H4-MLP-DIFF-30}} (95 % CI {{P1-H4-MLP-CI-30}}), and at light load {{P1-H4-MLP-DIFF-10}} ({{P1-H4-MLP-CI-10}}); neither interval excludes zero. H4 as registered names the learned policy without saying which model; for the network it is not supported.

{{P1-LIN-WEIGHTS-SENTENCE}} The gain does not come from other cargo: in the guard runs at 60 % load, delays of normal-priority and bulk cargo and tonne-days waiting all fell with the learned bids ({{P1-GUARD-TONNEDAYS-PCT}} for tonne-days), and tonnes delivered did not change. Outside its training range, at two and five owners, message loss 0.25 and 0.40, light load and start year 2038, the linear model kept the same sign in all {{P1-OOD-N-CELLS}} cells, with intervals clear of zero in {{P1-OOD-N-SIG}} of them (Table 6). A fleet twice as large could not be run in our time budget.

5.8 Sensitivities

Letting the hoppers refuel every month changed nothing that matters: the full-information marketplace's urgent wait moved by {{P1-HOPPER-O-DIFF}} ({{P1-HOPPER-O-CI}}).

5.9 What did not work

Imitating the full-information marketplace taught the bidding models nothing, because the marketplace uses the same bid rule: the hand-built bids already chose the marketplace's item in {{P1-IMIT-AGREE}} of held-out rounds, and every disagreement we examined ({{P1-IMIT-DISAGREE-30}} at 30 % load and {{P1-IMIT-DISAGREE-60}} at 60 %) was an item the marketplace gave to another robot in the same round. The network's best tuning score came in its first generation; the linear model's came in its last. The network trained by search on its output layer did not beat the hand-built bids. The relay at 80 % coverage missed its equivalence margin. {{P1-E41-DEPTH-CLAUSE}}.
