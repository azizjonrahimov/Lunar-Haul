5. Results

5.1 What each regime delivers

At every load the regimes fall into three groups (Table 5). Owners working alone keep urgent cargo waiting longest, whether they are coordinated from Earth or on the Moon: {{P1-A-URGENT-RANGE-30}} on average at the middle load. Pooled regimes that lose contact with Earth part of the time come next, from {{P1-RELAY08-URGENT-30}} with an {{P1-RELAY08-SHARE}} relay to {{P1-B-STRICT-URGENT-30}} with strict fallback. The regimes that decide on the Moon, the full-information marketplace and the dispatcher on Earth with a relay that is always up sit together at the bottom, from {{P1-LIN-URGENT-30}} with learned linear bids to {{P1-LEADER-URGENT-30}} with {{P1-BOTTOM-MAX-NAME}}. Tonnes delivered are almost the same within the pooled group ({{P1-POOLED-T-RANGE-30}}) and much lower for owners alone ({{P1-A-T-RANGE-30}}).

The fleet is lightly used even at the middle load. The two cargo rovers are busy {{P1-CARGO-UTIL-30}} of the year, the whole fleet {{P1-UTIL-FLEET-30}}, because the crew rovers, small rovers and hoppers can lift almost none of the cargo. At light load every pooled regime keeps urgent waits within {{P1-POOLED-SPREAD-10}} of one another except the strict dispatcher on Earth ({{P1-B-STRICT-URGENT-10}}). {{P1-GROUPS-60-SENTENCE}}

5.2 Sharing: capacity and dispatch (H1)

Pooling cut the mean urgent wait by {{P1-H1-DIFF-30}} at the middle load (95 % CI {{P1-H1-DIFF-30-CI}}, paired, n = {{P1-H1-N-30}}), comparing the pooled dispatcher and owners alone, both with Earth always in view. H1 is supported (Holm-adjusted p {{P1-H1-PHOLM}}). {{P1-H1-60-SENTENCE}}

The size of that difference follows from the scenario and from the one-year horizon, and it should not be read as hours that pooling saves on each item. Owner C has a third of the cargo and no cargo rover: its small rovers and hoppers cannot lift its crates or its regolith lots, and alone it delivered {{P1-OWNER-C-ALONE-T}} in a year against {{P1-OWNER-C-POOLED-T}} in the pooled fleet. Without cross-owner pairs no owner can lift most team items. By the end of a year owners alone had left {{P1-A-BACKLOG-T-30}} waiting. An urgent item that was not delivered counts with the time from its arrival to day 365, about half a year on average, so the {{P1-H1-DIFF-30}} would roughly double in a two-year run. The ownership split was not varied in E40 to E42. Two comparisons on delivered items only say what pooling changes for cargo that does move; both condition on delivery and so compare different sets of items. In the H1 pair itself, owners alone with Earth always in view waited {{P1-H1-DELIV-DIFF}} longer than the pooled dispatcher that is always in contact ({{P1-H1-DELIV-DIFF-CI}}, n = {{P1-H1-N-30}}). In a second comparison, which adds the loss of Earth, owners alone with strict fallback waited {{P1-A-DELIV-MINUS-O-30}} longer than the full-information marketplace ({{P1-A-DELIV-MINUS-O-CI}}, n = {{P1-A-DELIV-N}}), where the censored difference of the same pair is {{P1-A-CENS-MINUS-O-30}}. A delivered-only difference and a censored one are means over different items and do not add, so neither pair yields a share of the gap. Split as designed in Section 4.2, single-robot jobs pooled across owners, together with the pooled dispatcher, account for {{P1-H1-POOL-DIFF}} of the censored difference ({{P1-H1-POOL-SHARE}}; 95 % CI {{P1-H1-POOL-DIFF-CI}}) and lifts by pairs of different owners' rovers for {{P1-H1-TEAM-DIFF}} ({{P1-H1-TEAM-SHARE}}; {{P1-H1-TEAM-DIFF-CI}}). The pooled side of the first part is the robots' auction, whose difference from the full-information marketplace is given in Section 5.4. By owner, pooling cut the mean urgent wait by {{P1-H1-OWNER-A-DIFF}} for owner A, {{P1-H1-OWNER-B-DIFF}} for owner B and {{P1-H1-OWNER-C-DIFF}} for owner C, the owner without a cargo rover.

5.3 A dispatcher on Earth (H2)

A pooled dispatcher on Earth that stops when Earth sets kept urgent cargo waiting {{P1-H2-STRICT-MINUS-O}} longer than the full-information marketplace, pooled over all start years (95 % CI {{P1-H2-STRICT-MINUS-O-CI}}, n = {{P1-H2-N}}): {{P1-B-STRICT-URGENT-30}} against {{P1-O-URGENT-30-ALL}}, both means over the 80 matched rows, in which the marketplace's seeds 1 to 10 appear six times (Table 5 shows its 30 distinct runs). That interval resamples the 80 pairs as if they were independent, although most of the variance lies between the {{P1-H2-N-YEARS}} start years; resampling whole start years gives {{P1-H2-YEARCLUSTER-CI}}, and the per-year tests rest on {{P1-H2-N-PER-YEAR}} runs each. The margin, as revised in change 2 after a five-seed development run of the same cells and before the main runs, was 10 % of the marketplace's mean, {{P1-H2-MARGIN}}; the lower end of the interval clears it by a wide distance in each of the {{P1-H2-N-LOWVIS-YEARS}} start years whose base visibility is below 60 % ({{P1-H2-LOWVIS-YEARS}}), where the year means of the difference range from {{P1-H2-YEAR-MIN}} to {{P1-H2-YEAR-MAX}} (Figure 1). H2 as revised is supported on either interval (Holm-adjusted p {{P1-H2-PHOLM}}).

The backlog at the end of a blackout averages {{P1-B-STRICT-BACKLOG-30}} under strict fallback against {{P1-O-BACKLOG-30}} for the marketplace, and the share of urgent items delivered within a day falls from {{P1-O-WITHIN24-30}} to {{P1-B-STRICT-WITHIN24-30}} (again over the 80 matched rows). Planning ahead recovers most of it: uploading per-vehicle queues and a standing rule for blackout landings leaves {{P1-PLAN-MINUS-O}} of extra wait (95 % CI {{P1-PLAN-MINUS-O-CI}}). A relay that is up {{P1-RELAY08-SHARE}} of the time leaves {{P1-RELAY08-MINUS-O}} (95 % CI {{P1-RELAY08-MINUS-O-CI}}; 90 % interval, used for the equivalence test, {{P1-RELAY08-MINUS-O-CI90}}). That is outside the 10 % margin we set for it, so the secondary expectation that an {{P1-RELAY08-SHARE}} relay comes within 10 % of the marketplace fails.

The original yardstick of H2, half the gap between owners alone and the marketplace, would have been {{P1-H2-OLD-YARDSTICK-HALF}}, larger than anything Earth's availability could cost a pooled fleet. Against it H2 {{P1-H2-OLD-YARDSTICK-MET}}: the difference is {{P1-H2-STRICT-MINUS-O}}. We replaced it before the main runs because it measures fleet capacity (Section 4.6).

5.4 Deciding on the Moon (H3a)

The robots' own auction, at message loss 0.10 over the terrain-limited network, kept urgent cargo waiting {{P1-H3A-DIFF}} than the full-information marketplace ({{P1-H3A-PCT}}; 90 % CI {{P1-H3A-CI90}}, n = {{P1-H3A-N}}). We had registered the expectation that it would come within ±10 % of the marketplace, ±{{P1-H2-MARGIN}}. {{P1-H3A-VERDICT}} Because the auction, the leader and the marketplace never wait for Earth, a seed gives the same run in every start year; this comparison therefore uses the {{P1-H3A-N}} runs of block one only (change 4 of the pre-registration [43]). An earlier interim reading that counted the repeated runs as independent suggested the auction beat the marketplace; on independent runs that difference is not distinguishable from zero.

Two direct comparisons were not registered and are reported because a reader will look for them. The relay that is always up gives the same run as the full-information marketplace for every seed, so the auction's difference from it is the one above. Against the relay that is up {{P1-RELAY08-SHARE}} of the time, on the {{P1-AUCTION-DIRECT-N}} seeds of block one, the auction's urgent wait was {{P1-AUCTION-MINUS-RELAY08}} shorter (95 % CI {{P1-AUCTION-MINUS-RELAY08-CI}}), and against the plan-ahead dispatcher on Earth {{P1-AUCTION-MINUS-PLAN}} shorter ({{P1-AUCTION-MINUS-PLAN-CI}}).

5.6 Learned bids (H4)

The learned linear correction cut the mean urgent wait relative to the hand-built auction by {{P1-H4-LIN-DIFF-30}} at the middle load ({{P1-H4-LIN-PCT-30}}; 95 % CI {{P1-H4-LIN-CI-30}}, n = {{P1-H4-N}}) and by {{P1-H4-LIN-DIFF-10}} at light load ({{P1-H4-LIN-PCT-10}}; {{P1-H4-LIN-CI-10}}). The linear model is the secondary model of the registration, and its row entered the Holm family after its result on these seeds had been seen (Section 4.6). For it H4's first part is supported (Holm-adjusted p {{P1-H4-LIN-PHOLM}}); its second part, no difference beyond ±2 % at light load, fails. {{P1-H4-60-SENTENCE}}

The network did not improve on the hand-built bids. At the middle load its difference from the auction was {{P1-H4-MLP-DIFF-30}} (95 % CI {{P1-H4-MLP-CI-30}}), and at light load {{P1-H4-MLP-DIFF-10}} ({{P1-H4-MLP-CI-10}}); neither interval excludes zero. H4 as registered is about the network, the registered learned regime, with the linear model as a secondary report; for the network it is not supported.

{{P1-LIN-WEIGHTS-SENTENCE}} We have not interpreted these weights: no weight was removed in turn to see what it contributes, the policy is not monotone in the inputs an operator would expect it to be (Section 5.11), and the search kept its last generation, so it may not have converged. {{P1-LEARNED-EVAL-SENTENCE}} Ten further runs per load check whether other cargo pays for the gain. At 60 % load it does not: the urgent wait and the delays of normal-priority and bulk cargo and tonne-days waiting all fell with the learned bids ({{P1-GUARD-TONNEDAYS-PCT}} for tonne-days), each with an interval below zero, and tonnes delivered did not change. {{P1-GUARD-30-SENTENCE}} {{P1-OOD-LABEL-SENTENCE}} Outside its training range, at two and five owners, message loss 0.25 and 0.40, light load and start year 2038, the linear model kept the same sign in all {{P1-OOD-N-CELLS}} cells, with intervals clear of zero in {{P1-OOD-N-SIG}} of them (Table 6). A fleet twice as large could not be run in our time budget.

5.8 What the protocol costs the radio (E53, H13)

A rover that reports its position once a second offers {{P1-E53-PER-ROVER-1HZ}} ({{P1-E53-PER-ROVER-1HZ-BPS}}) with authentication: the position report, a packet of {{P1-PROP6-SECURED-B}}, and status and intent reports and the heartbeat that carries the declaration once every ten seconds. These loads are a calculation on a traffic model, sizes times assumed rates, with the sizes of version 0.2. Event messages add less than a thousandth of that. At 0.1 Hz the heartbeat also fills the silence and the load falls only to {{P1-E53-PER-ROVER-01HZ}}; at 10 Hz it is {{P1-E53-PER-ROVER-10HZ}} (Table 9). {{P1-E53-H13-SENTENCE}} {{P1-E53-DRAWS-SENTENCE}} {{P1-E53-AUTH-SENTENCE}}

5.10 Sensitivities

Letting the hoppers refuel every month changed nothing that matters: the full-information marketplace's urgent wait moved by {{P1-HOPPER-O-DIFF}} ({{P1-HOPPER-O-CI}}).

5.11 What did not work

Labelling the hand-built auction's rounds with the full-information marketplace's choice, the first iteration of the imitation stage, taught the bidding models nothing, because the marketplace uses the same bid rule: the hand-built bids already chose the marketplace's item in {{P1-IMIT-AGREE}} of held-out rounds, and every disagreement we examined ({{P1-IMIT-DISAGREE-30}} at 30 % load and {{P1-IMIT-DISAGREE-60}} at 60 %) was an item the marketplace gave to another robot in the same round. {{P1-IMIT-RERUN-SENTENCE}} The network's best tuning score came in its first generation; the linear model's came in its last. The network searched on its output layer did not beat the hand-built bids.
