6. Discussion

6.1 What the results say about the question

The question had three parts: what the vehicles of 2028–2032 can carry, what Lunar ATC needs inside each of them and whether it fits, and under which payment rules a market among owners is fair and hard to game. On the first part the answer needed no experiment and is the least comfortable: the fleet's capacity is a plateau at {{P3-CAP-MAX-PAIR-KG}} per lift, set by two cargo rovers nobody has bought, and the crew rovers NASA has bought carry {{P3-CLV1-PAYLOAD-KG}} and drive {{P3-CLV1-RANGE-KM}} on a charge. Whatever a dispatcher does, elements heavier than the plateau wait for a vehicle that does not exist, and the base's stated need runs to 15,000 kg [5, 6]. The sizing of this paper is for the fleet that will exist, which is small; the network it needs is correspondingly small.

On the second part, {{P3-E48-DISCUSSION-CLAUSE}} The safety program is explicit switching by design, the form that costs least in computation in the one measured comparison [45], and the planner's learned part is {{P3-LIN-N-WEIGHTS}} weights; the sizing question was always the bookkeeping of the market and the reservation table rather than any arithmetic, and the measurement says how much bookkeeping there is.

On the third part, {{P3-E46-DISCUSSION-CLAUSE}} {{P3-E46-POSTHOC-SENTENCE}} The registered test of the first clause was therefore underpowered: the measure it registered, the deviator's whole profit, carries the deviator's own delay term, whose spread over seeds is many times the cash, and the cash part on its own is positive. That is a statement about the test's power, made after the fact, not a change of the verdict, which stands as registered. The Shapley sharing of the posted price moves money from the owner of the small vehicles to the two cargo-rover owners: {{P3-E46-TRANSFERS-CLAUSE}}. That is what the Shapley rule's definition of fairness means, each owner's marginal contribution averaged over the orders in which owners could join, and it is a definition, not a measurement: the owner whose vehicles carry least and whose cargo waits longest contributes least to the coalition's value and pays the others. The alone baseline is weak, because an owner alone is dominated by its cargo's delay cost at its own value of time, so every pooled rule clears it by a wide margin. The harder comparison is against the pooled market with no payments, and there {{P3-E46-VS-NONE-CLAUSE}}. The market's purpose in this design is not to extract the last hour of efficiency but to let three companies share eight vehicles without a treaty; the number that matters for that is whether any owner, in particular the smallest, loses by joining, and E46 measures it directly against both baselines.

6.2 Comparison with earlier studies

{{P3-E48-COMPARISON-CLAUSE}} Nearly all of those bytes are the status heartbeat at 1 Hz (Section 5.2 gives its share), and the market's own messages are a small fraction of one per robot-hour at today's fleet; the honest comparison is therefore between a one-message-a-second heartbeat and a convoy controller that exchanges state many times a second, not between a market and a convoy. The mesh itself agrees with NASA's viewsheds [16]: a 10 m mast on this ridge sees {{P3-MESH-MAST-DIRECT-SHARE}} of the route points directly, within the 10.6–23.7 % that a 4 m tower and the 12.1–29.2 % that a 20 m tower covered of a 20 × 20 km polar area in that study (Section 2.2), and the rest of the network is multi-hop, as the Subterranean Challenge teams found underground [54, 55]. {{P3-E46-COMPARISON-CLAUSE}} The second-price deficit is mechanical rather than a prediction of theory: the payout is floored at the carrier's own ask and the tariff is the reserve when no competitor exists, so on every job the operator pays out at least what the shipper pays in and on most jobs more.

6.3 Implications

For the companies that will own the vehicles, the implication of Section 3.2 is that the first base cannot be served by any one of them, and that the first thing worth agreeing is not a market but a coupling: two rovers of two owners lifting one load, which is the only way anything above {{P3-CAP-MAX-SINGLE-KG}} moves in this fleet [25, 60]. For the designers of those vehicles, Table 3 is the list of what a vehicle must carry to take part in a shared network: one small program that refuses unsafe moves, a radio that floods a few dozen bytes a second to whatever it can see, and a copy of the entries it has heard, and Section 5.1 says what they cost . For the agencies that write LunaNet, the surface-to-surface link that the specification leaves bilateral [15] is where every number in Section 5.3 lives; a lunar UHF band [24] would change the link budget more than any software choice. For the operator of the base, the payment rule is a policy choice with a measured price: {{P3-E46-IMPLICATION-CLAUSE}} Earth's role in this design is the one it can play from 400,000 km away: it sets the prices and the rules, keeps the ledger, settles the accounts and audits a sample, and nothing it does has to happen within a radio window.

6.4 Limitations

The findings hold for one base, one fleet of eight vehicles in three ownerships, one radio model with assumed antenna heights, range and loss, and one implementation of the software; the sizing is scaled to flight hardware by a factor, not measured on it; the market has three owners, one manipulation and one private-cost spread, prices each run after one allocation with no owner responding to a rule, and tests individual rationality against a weak alone baseline; the Shapley transfers are a definition of fairness, not a measurement; and H5's decision rules were written after the registration, before any run. Section 7 states each in full.

6.5 Future work

Four pieces follow directly. The safety program and the auction should be run on the flight-class processor itself, against recorded traffic from the companion experiments, to replace the scaling factor with a measurement. The link budget should be checked against a measured lunar surface link as soon as one exists; none does today [17]. The market should be run with five owners and with the manipulations this paper dropped, false availability, charger hogging and cherry-picking, which an auditable ledger may or may not catch. And the Phase-1 rovers' real battery, mass and radio should replace the assumed values when their design reviews publish them, due in late 2026 [1].

7. Limitations

The sizing is scaled, not flown. Every processor time and memory figure is measured on a laptop in Python and scaled by the ratio of a reference flight processor's published benchmark to the laptop's. The factor is stated with a range, but a flight implementation in another language on another operating system could differ from it by more than that range in either direction. {{P3-E48-LIMITATION-SENTENCE}} The laptop's benchmark is itself assumed: no published CoreMark exists for its processor, and the figure is 6.0 CoreMark/MHz times its 4.5 GHz turbo clock (Assumed, range 4–7 CoreMark/MHz), so the factor's range is the range of that assumption. The processor share reported for H10 takes the 99th-percentile call time at every call, which overstates the mean load.

The radio is a model. Line of sight on a 5 m terrain grid with assumed antenna heights, a fixed range and a fixed loss probability stands in for a radio that nobody has measured on the Moon; the link budget uses standard diffraction and two-ray models over a regolith whose permittivity is unmeasured [20, 21]. The share of route sample points in direct view of the mast is a property of this model on this ridge, and it is a share of points, not of time.

The fleet is the fleet of 2028–2032 as published in October 2026. The cargo rovers are uncontracted, their masses, batteries and one capacity are assumed, and the Phase-1 rovers' batteries are set from a stated range. The design reviews due in late 2026 and early 2027 may change every assumed value in Table 2.

Three owners, one manipulation. The market is tested with three owners in two splits; the theory's warnings about collusion and shill identities [31] concern exactly such thin markets and are not tested. Bid scaling is the only misreport simulated; an owner that misreports position, availability or job completion, or that hogs a charger, is not. The audits compare bids with logged costs and assume the logs are honest.

H5's decision rules are dated after the registration. The first-price cell that H5's first two clauses need was dropped on 27 October and restored on 29 October, before any E46 row (change 7 of [59]); the numeric decision rules for all three clauses were written then, not in the original registration, and are dated in [59].

Private costs are assumed and their spread is not swept. Each owner's cost of energy, wear and time is the engine's job cost times a lognormal multiplier with σ = 0.2, a distribution we chose; profits and individual rationality are measured against those costs. The declared range of 0.1–0.3 was not run, because each value needs a new year of physical runs, so the results hold at one spread, and no spread replaces a company's own accounting.

Payments are priced after one allocation. Each rule prices a run that the auction has already allocated, so no owner responds to a rule and the honest allocation is the same under all four rules. The manipulation test is one deviator at a time over four fixed factors; no best response, no joint deviation and no equilibrium is computed. The gains reported are what a fixed scaling would have earned, not what a strategic owner would find.

The alone baseline is weak. An owner alone is dominated by its cargo's delay cost at its own value of time, so individual rationality against alone is easy to pass under every rule; the comparison against the pooled market with no payments is the harder one, and Section 5.6 reports it.

The Shapley transfers are a definition, not a measurement. The transfers under the posted price are large relative to the cash the tariff moves, and they follow from the Shapley rule's definition of fairness; the paper measures what that definition pays each owner, not whether the owners would call it fair.

The first-price clause failed for power. The registered measure, the deviator's whole profit, carries the deviator's own cargo delay, whose spread over seeds is many times the cash the scaled asks earn; the cash part is positive (Section 6.1, post hoc). The clause is not supported as registered, and that is not evidence that shading does not pay.

The first-price budget is a calibration gap. With honest asks the first-price budget is the tariff receipts less the true carrier cost; the tariff was set to the mean cost per tonne-kilometre on the calibration seeds, so the budget on the test seeds is the gap between the two seed sets, not a property of the rule.

The laptop's benchmark is assumed and the load is a 99th percentile. The scaling factor rests on an assumed CoreMark for the laptop, and the processor share of H10 uses the 99th-percentile call time at every call (above).

The lander carries only the radio unit and the landing announcement. In this design a lander publishes its landing time through the base computer and is otherwise a closure; it carries no planner and no safety program (change 7 of [59], Table 3), and the radio unit and announcement part it does carry are not sized here, because E48 measures the on-robot part on the driving and flying classes.

8. Conclusion

This paper asked what the vehicles of 2028–2032 can carry, what a shared traffic and market network must put inside each, whether it fits, and under which payment rules owners who do not trust one another gain by joining. We built the vehicle table from published sources, described the radio network, measured our implementation at one, four and eight times the fleet, and ran {{P3-E46-N-RUNS}} simulated market years under four payment rules with bid scaling and audits. The fleet's capacity is a plateau at {{P3-CAP-MAX-PAIR-KG}} per lift, set by two uncontracted cargo rovers. {{P3-E48-CONCLUSION-CLAUSE}} {{P3-E46-CONCLUSION-CLAUSE}} {{P3-H5-POWER-CONCLUSION-CLAUSE}}

For practice, Table 3 lists what a vehicle needs to join a shared network, at a cost measured rather than guessed; for policy, the surface link that LunaNet leaves bilateral is where the design's numbers live, and the payment rule is a choice priced in fairness and budget. The results hold for one base, one small fleet, a modelled radio and a scaled, not flown, implementation. The vehicles are known, the network can be small, and the market can be priced.
