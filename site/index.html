Cargo logistics at a lunar south-pole base, on real terrain

A discrete-event simulator of how landed cargo gets from the pads to where it is needed: landers, rover fleets from several owners, the lunar night and Earth–Moon latency.

Terrain near the lunar south pole from NASA's Lunar Orbiter Laser Altimeter, lit by a sun that stays within 1.5° of the horizon and circles once per lunar day; long shadows cross the ridges and low ground the sun cannot reach stays black. Shadows are cast only by terrain inside this tile.

What it is

A simulator of surface cargo movement at a south-pole base: lander manifests, multi-owner rover fleets, the lunar night, Earth–Moon control latency, and four ways of dispatching the vehicles, from each owner using its own rovers to a pooled marketplace. A Python reference engine and a browser port agree within confidence intervals; thirteen verification checks run on both.

How it works

What is new

Version 3 puts the base on real LOLA terrain and adds learned models as inputs to the simulation: a landing-dispersion model for the landers and a driving model for the rovers. The aim is a measured relation between landing accuracy, pad distance, plume keep-out radius and the cost of surface delivery.
