Simulator: a year of cargo at the base

Set up a Moon base, its rover fleet and the rule that gives rovers their jobs, then simulate years of landings, cargo, breakdowns and lunar nights. It is the engine behind the paper, ported line by line to run in your browser; nothing you enter leaves this page. The previous version stays at /simulator/v2/ for one release.

Scenario

Landed cargo arrives on landers at two pads. Moon soil (regolith) is dug at work sites and hauled to the base for building.

Each simulated year uses a different random seed: other landing dates, cargo and breakdowns. More years give tighter error bars.

What is simulated

Landers arrive at random times at one of two landing pads, each more than 1 km from the base. There are three sizes: CLPS-class landers (100–500 kg of cargo), Blue Moon MK1-class landers (2–3 t) and large landers (12–30 t). Each landing brings a random load drawn from five cargo types, with NASA's published mass ranges; an item can be picked up once it has been unloaded. Moon soil for building and return items (trash, samples) add regular extra jobs.

Each rover has an owner, a load limit, a speed, a way of being driven, random breakdowns and other work for its owner. Driving time follows the site map, weighted by slope. Rovers driven from Earth go slower when the signal delay is longer, as measured in NASA JSC trials: 3.24, 2.56, 2.03 and 1.76 km/h at 0, 4, 6 and 8 s round trip.

The four job rules

Separate fleets (called in-house in the paper): each cargo owner is served by one contracted carrier; the oldest item goes first, to the nearest rover.

Shared fleet, nearest free rover (pooled greedy): the same, but any rover may carry any owner's cargo.

Shared fleet, hourly matching (batched assignment): every hour, free rovers are matched to waiting items so that the total cost is lowest (Hungarian algorithm).

Marketplace: each item is auctioned. A rover's bid is its cost (wear and driver time) plus the cost of waiting to the cargo owner, minus a credit for urgent cargo. On top of the auction: items from the same pad share a trip; soil loads wait until 70 % full or 24 h; 2–3 rovers lift items too heavy for one; return items ride back on the same trip; jobs that would end in the dark are refused; and an idle rover waits at the pad before a scheduled landing.

Reading the numbers

The main result is how long urgent (priority 1) cargo waits: the time within which 90 % of it is delivered, and the share delivered within 24 h. Cargo no rover can move is landed mass that is heavier than any rover or team in the fleet, or lacks the standard handling interface. Cost is the fleet's yearly capital cost plus driver time, per kilogram of everything moved and per kilogram of landed cargo only. Error bars are 95 % confidence intervals (1.96 standard errors across simulated years). The precision button keeps adding years until the 99 % interval of the average urgent wait is within 2 % of the average.

Relation to the paper and the code

This page is a line-by-line port of the Python package lunarsim used for the preprint Who Hauls the Habitat? The Verification tab runs the same checks as the Python tests and compares both engines on the paper's Phase 2 runs. Save a scenario as JSON and run it from the command line with lunarsim run --scenario file.json. Numbers the paper marks as assumptions (hauler cost, soil amounts, driver cost, value of time) are the defaults here; the soil amount can be changed in the left panel.
