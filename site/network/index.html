A learned check on position reports · simulated data

Can a small network tell that a rover's position report is wrong?

A rover hears another rover's position once a second and measures the range to it. We trained a network of parameters to say, from the last two minutes, whether the other rover's reports are wrong, and compared it with the simple tests it would replace. Everything here is simulated: motion from our simulator, simulated sensors and five kinds of simulated fault.

What does it do with two minutes of data?

Ten inputs per second, all things the rover already knows, go through six causal convolutions that look ever further back (dilations 1 to 32), so the last second of the last layer sees the whole 127 s window. A last step turns that into one number: the probability that the other rover's reports are wrong. The network below runs in your browser on the same weights the experiment used.
