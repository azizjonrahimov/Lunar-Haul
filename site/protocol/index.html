The Lunar Rover Interaction Protocol

Rovers of several companies working at one base have to agree on where they are, what the ground looks like, what time it is and how to talk, and keep working when Earth is out of sight. This page will take the eight problems one at a time.

What this page will hold

One section per problem: the question, our answer in two sentences, the method, who we would work with, one figure you can play with, and a link to the paper section. The eight problems:

  1. Positioning: “Where am I?”, and how fast a rover's position error grows between fixes.
  2. A shared map and coordinate system: how a returning rover catches up on new hazards.
  3. Time: how far apart two rovers' clocks drift, and what that does to ranging.
  4. Communication: radio links over the terrain, and relays when a hill is in the way.
  5. The protocol itself: what a message carries, byte by byte, and how a rover says what it can do.
  6. Coordination as a team: sharing jobs, and taking over when a rover fails.
  7. Reliability and security: catching a wrong position report before it causes harm.
  8. Market and reality: who has flown what so far.

Nothing on this page is a result yet. Until the experiments are in, the Lunar ATC page shows the network design these answers build on.
